# The Public IF Language Contract

> **Agent proposal. Not public policy, advice, a service or an institutional
> commitment.** Ren drafted this contract at Fernando Bordallo's request. The
> `verb + [blank] + IF` idea is Fernando's. The claim classes, transition
> language and safeguards below are proposed scaffolding. They require his
> review and comprehension testing with affected people before public use.

## 🦅 TL;DR

- **A public claim is incomplete until the verb, object, conditions, scope,
  evidence and authority are visible.**
- An outcome, forecast, option, commitment and negotiation are different
  claims. One cannot silently become another.
- Refusal, redistribution and reduction are legitimate paths to describe. The
  language must show who may choose them, whose rights are affected and which
  IFs govern review.
- Conditions change through named, dated events. Old wording, evidence,
  challenges and disagreements remain visible.
- `No automated action` describes an authority boundary. It does not establish
  that acting or waiting is safe.
- The aim is to make an **evidence-and-authority-appropriate choice** possible
  and contestable.
- Prominence remains an accountable **editorial judgment**, not an output that
  the evidence decides. Any display rule must publish its loss function,
  affected-party priorities, plausible harms, uncertainty and accountable owner.

## 🧩 Complete the blank

The short form is:

> **verb + [blank] + IF**

The blank must be large enough to expose the actual public claim. The minimum
complete form is:

> **[WHO] may [VERB] [OBJECT] IF [CONDITIONS], for [SCOPE], based on
> [EVIDENCE], under [AUTHORITY].**

Where the claim concerns an outcome, add the outcome standard and period.
Where it concerns a choice, add alternatives, safeguards, review and expiry.
Where it affects another party, add that party's rights, participation and
challenge route.

| Field | Plain-language question |
|---|---|
| `WHO` | Who is making the claim or may make the choice? |
| `VERB` | What observable change, offer, decision or refusal is being described? |
| `OBJECT` | What exactly is achieved, forecast, offered, promised, negotiated, refused, redistributed or reduced? |
| `IF` | Which conditions must hold, fail or remain unresolved? |
| `SCOPE` | Which people, places, services and periods are included and excluded? |
| `EVIDENCE` | What supports the statement, with what uncertainty and strongest alternative? |
| `AUTHORITY` | Who may legitimately decide, commit, deliver, enforce, refuse or appeal? |

Use `Unknown` when a field is unresolved. Do not remove the field or fill it
with a confident implication.

## 🗂 Eight claim classes

The classes below can appear together, but each keeps its own label and proof
burden.

### 1. Outcome claim

> **[WHO] can [VERB] [OUTCOME] to [STANDARD] in [SCOPE] by [PERIOD] IF
> [CAPABILITY, REACH, AGENCY, DURABILITY AND FAIRNESS CONDITIONS].**

An outcome claim describes what people can actually achieve, not merely what a
system can produce. `Can access` is incomplete unless access includes the
relevant cost, eligibility, quality, delivery, choice and remedy conditions.

Public boundary: say who is outside the evidence. A population average does
not establish an individual's outcome.

### 2. Forecast claim

> **[FORECASTER] estimates [OUTCOME OR EVENT] within [RANGE] by [PERIOD] IF
> [MODEL ASSUMPTIONS AND RESOLUTION CONDITIONS], for [SCOPE], based on
> [ISSUED EVIDENCE].**

A forecast must carry its issue time, horizon, probability or interval,
assumptions, reference class, resolution rule and score when resolved. A
scenario without a calibrated probability stays a scenario.

**A forecast does not make an option necessary.** It does not prove inevitable
benefit, harm, crisis or social response. Every forecast rendering must say
that it does not establish inevitability.

### 3. Option claim

> **[ACTOR] could consider [VERB] [BOUNDED OBJECT] IF [ELIGIBILITY
> CONDITIONS], while [SAFEGUARDS] hold, until [REVIEW OR EXPIRY].**

An option is a possibility for legitimate decision-makers to consider. It must
show alternatives, reversibility, expected benefits, possible harms, affected
parties, evidence gaps and the authority still required.

**An eligible option is not a commitment.** `Could consider` must not become
`will`, `must`, `activates` or `is available` without a separately verified
commitment.

### 4. Commitment claim

> **[AUTHORISED INSTITUTION] commits to [DELIVERABLE AND SERVICE LEVEL] for
> [BENEFICIARIES] IF [LAWFUL ACTIVATION CONDITIONS], funded by [SOURCE], from
> [START] until [EXIT OR EXPIRY].**

**A commitment requires separately verified authority**, funding, accountable
ownership, delivery capacity, safeguards, help, appeal and an exit rule. An IF
cannot be used to remove an existing right or duty that applies independently.

The public text must identify who made the commitment and how readers can
verify it. An agent proposal or forecast cannot supply that authority.

### 5. Negotiation claim

> **[PARTY] proposes to negotiate [TERM] with [OTHER PARTY] IF [OPENING
> CONDITIONS], while preserving [RIGHTS AND ALTERNATIVES], by [REVIEW DATE].**

**A negotiation opening is not an agreed settlement.** Record each party's
proposal, evidence, authority, non-negotiable safeguard, alternative and right
to decline. Do not describe silence as consent or disagreement as ignorance.

If a condition changes during negotiation, publish the change event before
using the new condition in a public summary. The other party's response remains
separate from the initiating party's claim.

### 6. Refusal or no-deployment claim

> **[RIGHTS-HOLDER OR AUTHORISED ACTOR] may refuse, pause or not deploy
> [SYSTEM OR USE] IF [CHOICE, VETO OR SAFEGUARD CONDITIONS], with [VIABLE
> ALTERNATIVE, REVIEW AND APPEAL].**

Refusal may be an individual right, a collective bargaining position or an
institutional decision. Name which one. A person does not need a dashboard to
authorise a lawful right to refuse. Institutional no-deployment needs the
relevant mandate and must disclose effects on people who depend on the service.

**Refusal is not evidence that deployment is harmful.** Deployment is not
evidence that refusal is irrational. Both choices retain their own evidence,
rights, costs and review conditions.

### 7. Redistribution claim

> **[AUTHORISED BODY] could [TRANSFER, SHARE OR RETURN] [RESOURCE OR RETURN]
> from [CONTRIBUTORS OR SOURCE] to [RECIPIENTS] IF [DISTRIBUTION,
> AFFORDABILITY AND RIGHTS CONDITIONS], for [PERIOD], with [REVIEW AND
> APPEAL].**

**Redistribution is a value and authority choice.** Evidence can estimate
incidence, costs, benefits and alternatives. It cannot decide who owes what to
whom. Public wording must name the resource, source, recipients, exclusions,
funding, duration, administrative burden, rights, possible avoidance and
unintended effects.

A redistribution path must not imply that cash, public provision, ownership,
taxation and negotiated gain-sharing are interchangeable. Compare them under
the same protected outcome and scope.

### 8. Reduction claim

> **[AUTHORISED ACTOR] could reduce [NAMED QUANTITY, ACTIVITY OR BURDEN] by
> [AMOUNT OR RANGE] IF [THRESHOLD AND SAFEGUARD CONDITIONS], while protecting
> [OUTCOME, PEOPLE AND ESSENTIAL CONTINUITY], until [REVIEW OR EXIT].**

**Reduction must name what is reduced and what is protected.** Reduced paid
hours, prices, emissions, administrative burden, deployment scope and service
capacity are different claims. A reduction in one does not prove a gain in
another.

A reduction path must show the baseline, unit, distribution, rebound or
substitution risk, affected people, viable alternatives and restoration rule.

## 🚧 Claims cannot borrow from one another

| Evidence supports | Permitted wording | It does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Observed association | `moved with`, `differs across` | Cause |
| Forecast | `estimated probability or range` | Inevitable outcome or required response |
| Eligible option | `could consider if` | Approval, commitment or availability |
| Verified commitment | `commits to deliver if` | Delivery before the activation IF passes |
| Negotiation proposal | `proposes to negotiate` | Consent or settlement |
| Refusal | `declines or withholds consent` | General proof of benefit or harm |
| Distribution analysis | `estimated incidence` | A legitimate redistribution choice |
| Reduction estimate | `quantity may fall under assumptions` | A protected human outcome |

When several classes appear in one update, use separate sentences and labels.
Do not use a smooth paragraph to hide the boundary between them.

## 🔄 How conditions evolve

Conditions are versioned public objects. Their current wording is useful, but
their history explains why the wording deserves trust.

Maintain an **append-only condition history**. Every event records:

```yaml
event_id: <stable identity>
condition_id: <stable identity>
operation: <typed event>
recorded_at: <time>
effective_from: <time or Unknown>
author: <person or accountable institution>
authority: <authority for a choice, or not-applicable for an evidence update>
previous_wording: <exact earlier text or null>
previous_scope: <exact earlier scope or null>
previous_evidence: <exact earlier evidence references or null>
new_wording: <text or null>
new_scope: <scope or null>
new_evidence: <references or null>
reason: <why this operation occurred>
challenge_refs: <open and resolved challenges>
review: <date, owner and possible next operations>
```

In plain language, the public record preserves the **previous wording**,
**previous scope**, **previous evidence**, **reason** for change and **author**
of the event.

| Operation | Public meaning |
|---|---|
| `added` | A new condition entered the graph. It starts unresolved unless evidence establishes another state. |
| `narrowed` | Wording, threshold or scope became more specific. The broader claim remains visible as the previous version. |
| `split` | One condition became two or more independently testable conditions. Every child points to the parent. |
| `merged` | Two or more conditions became one because their distinction no longer changed evaluation or choice. Every parent remains linked. |
| `challenged` | A named party submitted a reasoned objection to wording, evidence, scope, authority or interpretation. A challenge does not decide the result. |
| `satisfied` | The condition passed its declared evidence rule for its stated scope and valid period. It may later fail or expire. |
| `failed` | The condition did not pass its declared rule. This is not proof of cause or fault. |
| `expired` | Its evidence, authority or valid period ended. Expiry is not failure. |
| `superseded` | A named replacement governs future evaluations. **Superseded is not deleted.** |
| `disputed` | Credible evaluations remain incompatible after review. **Disputed is not false.** |
| `withdrawn` | The proposer retracted the condition or claim and gave a reason. Withdrawal is not proof that the opposite is true. |

`Challenged` records that an objection exists. `Disputed` records the review
result when credible disagreement remains. `Superseded` points forward.
`Withdrawn` closes the proposer's claim. These events must not be collapsed.

History remains visible in chronological order, including minority reports and
resolved challenges. If privacy or safety requires redaction, retain the event,
redaction reason, accountable redactor and review date. Never rewrite the old
record to make the path look cleaner.

## 🛑 No automated action is not a safety finding

> **No automated action is authorised. The evidence does not establish that waiting is safe.**

`No automated action` means the Observatory cannot approve, activate, refuse,
pause, enforce or end a material response. No automated action does not mean
inaction is safe, harmless or authorised by default.

Assess the **safety of acting** and the **safety of waiting** separately. Each
may be supported, unsupported, unknown or disputed. Also state who owns any
existing legal, professional or operational duty outside the Observatory.

| Automated authority | Evidence about acting | Evidence about waiting | Public wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | Unknown | Unknown | No automated action. The effects of acting and waiting remain unknown. |
| None | Material concern | Unknown | No automated action. A named human authority should review the concern by the stated date. This is not an instruction to act. |
| None | Unknown | Material concern | No automated action. Waiting may also carry material risk; the named authority should review both paths. |
| Verified external commitment | Activation IF passed | Waiting assessed | The named owner may act only under the verified commitment and safeguards. The Observatory still does not activate it. |

The absence of a new choice may preserve an existing policy, deployment or
distribution. That default has effects. Name **what happens if no choice is
made**, who remains responsible and when the default will be reviewed.

## 🧭 Public rendering contract

Every public version answers these questions in this order:

1. **What is known?** State the observation or verified institutional event.
2. **What remains unknown?** Include uncertainty, missing people and data lag.
3. **What is the exact claim class?** Outcome, forecast, option, commitment,
   negotiation, refusal, redistribution or reduction.
4. **Which IFs matter now?** Show their current state, scope and evidence rule.
5. **Who may challenge it?** Give the reachable route, response time and owner.
6. **What happens if the IF fails?** State the claim, option or commitment
   consequence without inventing an automatic action.
7. **What happens if no choice is made?** Describe the default path and its
   known, unknown and disputed effects.
8. **When it will be reviewed?** Give the date or event, owner and treatment of
   delay.

### Non-priming rules

- Give the **same factual description** to every audience. Present the
  strongest credible alternatives with **evidence-proportional prominence**
  under a predeclared display rule. Author preference receives no weight.
- A **credible alternative** must be compatible with the scoped observations,
  name a mechanism or source of error, state a discriminating observation and
  remain unresolved under a predeclared display rule. Plausibility alone does
  not earn equal visual weight. Label weak, untested, mixed, supported and
  contradicted accounts distinctly, and retain a low-support alternative when
  its omission would materially distort a high-consequence decision.
- Separate observation, inference, forecast, value choice, option, commitment
  and action. Do not slide between them through tone.
- Use neutral actor names. Do not label people enlightened, resistant,
  backward, selfish, fearful or irrational.
- State absolute values, denominators, uncertainty, scope and timing before a
  relative change or dramatic example.
- Put benefit and harm paths in parallel language and comparable detail.
- Do not imply consensus from silence, participation from page views, or
  legitimacy from technical precision.
- **No countdowns, inevitability language, moral labels or instructions to fear.**
  Do not use urgency styling unless a separately authorised public
  safety service requires it.
- Say `the forecast does not prove inevitable change` rather than asking the
  reader to imagine the forecast as their future.

The communication should support an evidence-and-authority-appropriate choice,
not pronounce the correct choice for everyone.

## 🧪 Worked neutral example

The names and facts below are fictional.

> **Observed:** A fictional service recorded a longer median wait for a defined
> cohort. The cause is unknown.
>
> **Forecast claim:** The evaluation team estimates waits may remain above the
> agreed range next quarter if demand and staffing follow the stated
> assumptions. This does not establish inevitability.
>
> **Option claim:** The service owner could consider adding reversible capacity
> if the confirming observation, funding, staffing and safety conditions pass.
>
> **No automated action:** The Observatory cannot activate that option. The
> effects of adding capacity and waiting both require review.
>
> **Challenge:** Workers and service users may challenge the measurement,
> assumptions, option or default through the fictional review route.

This example does not tell the reader which path to prefer. It shows what would
need to be known and who would need authority to choose.

## ⚠️ Unresolved risks

- **Comprehension:** a complete claim can still be too complex. Test short and
  detailed versions with affected people without changing their truth content.
- **Strategic wording:** institutions may design an IF that is technically
  measurable but practically impossible to satisfy or challenge.
- **Authority verification:** the repository does not yet authenticate public
  commitments, mandates, funding or service capacity.
- **History and privacy:** append-only records can expose sensitive challenge
  histories. Redaction needs independent rules and review.
- **Delay:** review can become a substitute for protection. Existing duties and
  urgent specialist routes must remain visible outside this protocol.
- **Translation:** literal translation may change modality, refusal rights or
  certainty. Each language needs comprehension and back-translation testing.
- **Power:** equal-looking consultation does not create equal bargaining power.
  Record representation, dissent and who can bear the cost of waiting.
- **Automation drift:** a downstream system may treat a condition state as an
  instruction. Machine interfaces must preserve claim class, authority and the
  explicit no-automation boundary.

## ✅ Adoption gate

Before public adoption, test whether readers can correctly identify:

1. the claim class;
2. what the IF does and does not establish;
3. who has authority;
4. whether acting, waiting or both remain uncertain;
5. how to challenge the claim;
6. the default if nobody chooses; and
7. the next review.

If readers systematically infer inevitability, instruction, commitment or
safety from wording that does not establish it, revise the language before
publication and preserve the failed version in the review record.
