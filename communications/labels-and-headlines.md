# Epistemic, Provenance and Headline Rules

> **Proposal status:** Ren, an AI agent, wrote these rules. They are not
> Fernando's opinion and require his approval before public use.

## 🦅 TL;DR

- Every consequential claim gets one epistemic label and one provenance label.
- Labels describe claim type and authorship, not whether a claim is good, true
  or endorsed.
- Headlines name scope and time, keep uncertainty visible and use causal or
  crisis language only when the evidence supports it.
- Fictional examples below are invented solely to test the rules.

---

## 🔎 Epistemic labels

| Public label | Meaning | Required fields | Allowed language | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Observed** | Directly reported measurement | Source, unit, population, place, period, vintage, uncertainty | measured, reported, was, changed | caused, proves, will |
| **Derived** | Calculation from observations | Formula, inputs, transformation, propagated uncertainty | calculated, indexed, estimated from | observed directly |
| **Hypothesis** | Testable proposed explanation | Mechanism, alternatives, falsifier, evidence grade | may, could, hypothesis | shows, explains, will |
| **Forecast** | Probabilistic future claim | Probability, event, horizon, model or reference class, interval, scoring rule | X% chance by date | expected without probability |
| **Scenario** | Conditional illustration with no probability | Assumptions and `Not a forecast` | if assumptions hold, then | likely, expected, projected risk |
| **Value choice** | Contestable judgement about what should matter | Decision authority, affected parties, alternatives, dissent | should, priority, acceptable | evidence proves we should |
| **Proposal** | Suggested action not yet authorised | Proposer, owner candidate, costs, evidence, harms, approval state | proposes, recommends for review | will, has activated |
| **Commitment** | Authorised and funded action | Authority, owner, scope, trigger, service level, appeal, exit | will provide, is accountable | aspiration without delivery |
| **Unknown** | Important question without adequate evidence | Why it matters and learning plan, if one exists | unknown, not measured | zero, safe, no effect |
| **Contested** | Material evidence or value dispute | Strong opposing positions and resolution path | disputed, unresolved | fringe, misinformation without test |
| **Correction** | A prior public statement was materially wrong | Original claim, error, corrected value, implications, owner | wrong, corrected, changed conclusion | clarified when it was wrong |

### Label rules

- `Measured` does not mean causal.
- `Derived` does not mean less useful, but its method is part of the claim.
- `Forecast` is invalid without a horizon and resolution record.
- `Scenario` never receives a probability-looking colour or percentage.
- `Value choice` cannot inherit authority from an adjacent chart.
- `Proposal` cannot appear in a voice that implies institutional commitment.
- `Unknown` never becomes zero or green by default.
- `Contested` requires a strong opposing account, not a token objection.
- `Correction` is a primary content type, not a footnote.

## 🧬 Provenance labels

| Public label | Meaning | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Fernando, captured** | Traceable to Fernando's recorded statement | Capture link, date and retained context |
| **Fernando, endorsed** | Fernando explicitly adopted a later formulation | Endorsement capture and date |
| **External source** | Third-party evidence or published analysis | Author or institution, link, version and retrieval date |
| **Ren or agent proposal** | Agent synthesis, hypothesis, design or advice | Agent name, commission context and approval state |
| **Participant evidence** | Consented lived or community evidence | Collection method, scope, consent and privacy treatment |
| **Institutional commitment** | Named body accepted responsibility | Authority, owner, date and review mechanism |

### Provenance display

Every evidence card places the two labels together:

```text
[OBSERVED] [EXTERNAL SOURCE]
[HYPOTHESIS] [REN OR AGENT PROPOSAL, REQUIRES APPROVAL]
[VALUE CHOICE] [INSTITUTIONAL COMMITMENT]
```

Do not collapse them into one badge such as `verified`. A captured view can be
uncertain. An external observation can be strong evidence without becoming
Fernando's view.

## 📰 Public headline contract

A defensible headline answers as many of these as fit without becoming
unreadable:

```text
[CLAIM TYPE] + [OUTCOME OR MEASURE] + [COHORT/PLACE] + [PERIOD]
+ [OBSERVED CHANGE] + [LIMIT OR ACTION STATUS]
```

The evidence card directly below completes every omitted field.

### Headline rules

1. **Lead with the observation or honest unknown.** Do not lead with the
   preferred explanation.
2. **Name scope.** A world result, national result and cohort result are
   different claims.
3. **Name time.** `Latest` is insufficient without the observation period or
   vintage.
4. **Show uncertainty when it could change interpretation.** Put it in the
   headline or immediate subhead.
5. **Use cause verbs only for causal evidence.** Otherwise use `coincided`,
   `was associated`, `followed` or `remains unexplained`.
6. **Use crisis words only against a validated public definition.** An unscored
   failure mode is not a crisis probability.
7. **Do not turn coverage into outcome.** More measured indicators means better
   instrumentation, not more progress or risk.
8. **Do not turn exposure into harm.** Exposure, adoption, task change,
   displacement and household effect are separate.
9. **Do not turn absence into safety.** Missing data is `unknown`, not green.
10. **Do not call consumer-price zero resource-cost zero.** State who pays and
    which costs remain.
11. **Do not infer preference from behaviour alone.** Participation, use and
    non-use can have many causes.
12. **State action status.** Distinguish no action, review, preparation,
    authorised activation and recovery.
13. **Avoid inevitability.** Ban `will transform`, `is coming`, `unstoppable`,
    `certain` and equivalents unless literally supported.
14. **Avoid implied villains.** Name observed control or conduct rather than a
    moral actor when causality is unresolved.
15. **Keep the falsifier reachable.** A reader should find what would reverse
    the interpretation within one step.

## ✅ Fictional headline examples

Every organisation, place, figure and event below is fictional.

| Weak | Stronger |
|---|---|
| Automation crisis hits Harbourvale | **Observed:** registered job exits rose among Harbourvale records clerks in April. The cause remains unresolved. |
| Our crisis risk is now 70% | **Instrumentation:** 7 of 10 warning measures are available. No crisis probability has been estimated. |
| AI destroyed 400 jobs | **Observed:** fictional provider Aster Systems reported 400 fewer records-clerk roles after a workflow change. The share attributable to AI has not been isolated. |
| Workers are choosing freedom | **Observed:** labour-force participation fell in the fictional pilot cohort. Reasons for non-participation were not measured. |
| Free learning has arrived | **Observed:** the fictional Lumen course charged no consumer fee. Device, time, public funding and data costs remain. |
| Access is safe | **Observed:** aggregate access coverage was stable. Cohort results and uncertainty are incomplete, so safety has not been established. |
| Warning triggered | **Review status:** the first of two fictional confirmation periods crossed the access threshold. No support activation is authorised yet. |
| We fixed the problem | **Recovery:** fictional access coverage cleared the recovery threshold twice. Housing stability and appeal outcomes remain under review. |

## ❌ Headline stop words

The following require explicit review and evidence:

- crisis
- collapse
- safe
- solved
- proven
- caused
- protected
- free
- optional
- everyone
- nobody
- inevitable
- imminent
- historic
- unprecedented
- failing
- passing
- high risk
- low risk

They are not forbidden in all contexts. They are forbidden as shortcuts.

## 🔬 Headline review checklist

- What is the epistemic class?
- What is the provenance class?
- Does the verb match the evidence design?
- Are cohort, place and period clear?
- Could uncertainty reverse the apparent result?
- Does a missing subgroup make the headline unsafe?
- Could readers mistake instrumentation for risk?
- Could readers mistake a scenario for a forecast?
- Does `free` hide a payer or non-money cost?
- Does the headline imply an action has activated?
- Is the strongest competing explanation visible below it?
- Is the correction history one step away?

If two reviewers disagree on the claim class or implied action, the headline
does not ship.
