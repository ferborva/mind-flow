# Public Communications Operating Kit

> **Proposal status:** Ren, an AI agent, wrote this kit after Fernando
> commissioned a deeper public communication system for the abundance
> transition work. It is not Fernando's opinion and does not imply his
> endorsement. All rules, templates and acceptance gates require his approval
> before they govern public communication.

## 🦅 TL;DR

- **One evidence base, different assistance.** Facts, uncertainty and claim
  status do not change by audience. The useful next action can.
- Every update answers seven things: what was observed, who appears affected,
  what remains inferred, which IF changed, what action follows and who owns it,
  what would falsify the reading, and when it will be checked again.
- Every consequential sentence carries an epistemic label and a provenance
  label. Public readers should never need to infer whether they are seeing a
  fact, forecast, scenario, value or proposal.
- Headlines state scope, time and uncertainty. They do not turn data coverage
  into risk, correlation into cause, or a national average into a cohort result.
- Crisis and positive-horizon paths receive equal scrutiny, but not forced equal
  word count. The evidence decides the emphasis.
- Communication aims to improve understanding and agency. It must never use
  fear, vulnerability or political identity to manufacture consent.

## 🎯 Communication objective

The job is not to make people agree with the abundance thesis. The job is to
help them answer:

1. What happened?
2. Does it apply to me or people I care about?
3. How sure are we?
4. Which condition opened or closed a path?
5. What can be done, by whom, under which authority?
6. How can I challenge the evidence, decision or effect on me?
7. When will the claim be checked again?

Success means informed agency, not reach, confidence, support or attention by
themselves.

## ⏱ One-minute explainer: verb + blank + IF

The student example below is hypothetical.

> Technology can do remarkable things. But `a system can teach algebra` is not
> the same sentence as `every student can learn algebra`.
>
> Complete the promise: **a person can, verb, fill in the blank, IF.** A student
> can learn algebra if the lesson works, if they can reach and understand it,
> if they have the time and tools it needs, if they can choose or refuse it, and
> if help and appeal exist when it fails.
>
> Those IFs change by person, place, service and time. Solving price may expose
> delivery. Solving delivery may expose language, safety or permission.
>
> This project maps those conditions. It separates what we observed from what
> we infer, shows uncertainty and competing explanations, and links a warning
> only to action that a legitimate institution has authorised. It is not a
> prediction of one future. It is a way to see more possible paths and navigate
> them earlier.

### Thirty-second version

> **Every technology promise has an IF.** A system may be capable of teaching,
> diagnosing or creating, but a person benefits only if quality, cost,
> eligibility, delivery, rights and choice hold for them. We map those
> conditions, show what is known and unknown, and make prepared actions
> contestable before harm compounds.

### One-sentence version

> **Capability becomes abundance only if real people can achieve valuable
> outcomes with access, agency, quality and durable choice.**

None of these scripts claims that abundance, displacement or crisis is
inevitable.

## 📣 The seven-part public update

The original five-question communication contract becomes seven because two
distinctions should never be hidden inside another answer.

### 1. What did we observe?

State the measure, source, population, place, period, vintage and uncertainty.
Use descriptive language unless a credible design supports causality.

### 2. Who appears affected?

Name the cohort and distribution. Say explicitly who is not represented or
cannot be inferred from the measure.

### 3. What remains inferred?

Separate the proposed explanation, forecast, scenario and competing
explanations from the observation.

### 4. Which IF changed?

Name the condition, scope, previous state, new state, evidence grade and
dependencies. Do not say `risk rose` when only measurement coverage improved.

### 5. What action follows, and who owns it?

State whether the result triggers no action, investigation, preparation,
activation, pause or recovery. Name the accountable role, authority, funding,
safeguard and appeal route.

### 6. What would falsify this reading?

Name a realistic observation that would weaken or reverse the interpretation.
`More research` is not a falsifier.

### 7. When is the next check?

Give a date or defined event. State what new evidence is expected and what will
happen if it is delayed.

### Compact rendering

```text
OBSERVED      [fact, scope, period, source, uncertainty]
AFFECTED      [cohort, distribution, exclusions]
INFERRED      [interpretation, alternatives, confidence]
IF CHANGED    [condition, previous → current state]
ACTION        [mode, owner, authority, help or appeal]
FALSIFIER     [evidence that would change the reading]
NEXT CHECK    [date or event]
```

If any required line is unknown, write `Unknown`. Do not omit the line.

## 🧭 Progressive disclosure

Every public update has three layers built from the same source object:

### Layer 1: the public result

- One evidence-safe headline.
- What changed and what did not.
- Whether any action is required now.
- A direct help or participation route.

### Layer 2: the evidence card

- All seven update fields.
- Provenance and epistemic labels.
- Unit, cohort, place, vintage and uncertainty.
- Competing explanation and falsifier.
- Action owner, authority and appeal.

### Layer 3: methods and record

- Source data and retrieval date.
- Definitions and transformations.
- Missingness, revisions and known bias.
- Forecast issue record and score.
- Decision, override and correction history.
- Public challenges and their status.

Progressive disclosure may shorten detail. It must not change the evidence,
certainty or conclusion.

## 👥 Cohort-specific assistance without different truths

Every cohort receives the same observed evidence, inference status, uncertainty,
IF state, trigger status and next check. What changes is assistance, context and
the most reachable action.

| Cohort | Useful assistance | What must not change |
|---|---|---|
| Directly affected | Protection status, named case route, service time, appeal and remedy | Facts, uncertainty, causal status and decision rule |
| Exposed, not currently harmed | Options, preparation, trigger rules and signs of change | No inflation of personal risk to motivate preparation |
| Essential workers | Workload, staffing, bargaining, safety and continuity routes | Do not replace material conditions with a purpose narrative |
| Wider public | Shared gains, costs, alternatives, public choices and participation | Do not conceal cohort harm behind aggregate benefit |
| Communities and unions | Local cuts, data challenge, negotiation and rapid feedback | Do not frame organising as resistance to be overcome |
| Providers and businesses | Reporting duty, transition obligation, audit and appeal | Do not soften a red result to protect reputation |
| Delivery institutions | Operational threshold, capacity, authority and escalation | Do not turn an analyst's recommendation into authority |
| Cross-border or excluded groups | Eligibility comparison, language, portability and appeal | Do not promise a route that the jurisdiction has not authorised |

### The one-truth rule

These elements are immutable across audience variants:

- observed value and uncertainty;
- population, place and time scope;
- epistemic and provenance class;
- whether a trigger passed;
- strongest competing explanation;
- forecast probability and issue record;
- correction and revision history;
- next check date.

Audience variants may change reading level, language, order, examples, support
links and role-specific actions. Every variant links to the common evidence
card.

## 🫱🏽‍🫲🏼 Assistance hierarchy

For a directly affected person, order communication by immediate need:

1. **Safety and continuity:** what remains available today?
2. **Protection:** what has activated and how is it reached?
3. **Agency:** what choices, alternatives and refusal rights exist?
4. **Appeal:** who reviews an error and by when?
5. **Evidence:** what was observed and how uncertain is it?
6. **Pathways:** what voluntary options exist next?
7. **Participation:** how can the person challenge or redesign the response?

This does not hide evidence. It places immediate help before interpretation.

## 🧯 Anti-panic safeguards

### Always

- Put observed facts before speculative explanations.
- State `No immediate action is required` when that is the honest result.
- State the available action or help beside a warning.
- Use absolute values and denominators, not relative change alone.
- Describe data lag and what may have changed since observation.
- Say who the result does and does not represent.
- Present a competing explanation before asking for action.
- Avoid countdowns, pulsing red elements and urgency animation.
- Repeat the update on a known cadence, including when no conclusion changed.
- Give directly affected people a human route, not only a dashboard.

### Never

- Announce a capability triumph beside an unprotected loss.
- Call an unscored path `high risk`, `likely` or `imminent`.
- Use `crisis`, `collapse`, `emergency` or `failing` without a validated public
  definition and current evidence.
- imply that delay always increases harm.
- show a point estimate without uncertainty when uncertainty is material.
- use one vivid anecdote as prevalence evidence.
- turn an aggregate trend into a personal prediction.
- name a villain when the causal chain is unresolved.
- hide a correction in methods or update it with less reach than the error.

## 🛡 Anti-manipulation safeguards

Communication must inform choice rather than optimise consent.

- No emotional, political or vulnerability-based microtargeting.
- No personalised crisis propensity or political-response score.
- No use of transition data for worker discipline, advertising, policing or
  immigration enforcement.
- No message variant may alter certainty, omit a material downside or conceal a
  trigger failure.
- No `resistance` framing that treats legitimate disagreement as ignorance.
- No claims of inevitability, including inevitable abundance, unemployment,
  conflict or institutional response.
- No simulated community voice presented as testimony.
- Fictional examples carry a visible fictional label.
- Participant quotations require consent, context and protection against
  re-identification.
- Calls to action identify whose objective they serve.
- Persuasive design tests measure understanding and agency, not only conversion.
- A reader can access evidence, disagree and leave without penalty.

### The manipulation self-check

Before release, the author answers:

1. What belief or action could this message cause?
2. Who benefits if it succeeds?
3. Which fact or downside would weaken its persuasive effect?
4. Is that fact or downside equally visible?
5. Are we using fear, identity, urgency or authority beyond the evidence?
6. Can the reader inspect the source and choose no action?
7. Would we accept an opponent using the same technique against us?

Any uncomfortable answer blocks release until reviewed.

## 🌅 Balance crisis with the positive horizon

The Observatory exists to navigate towards more human agency, not to maintain a
permanent emergency atmosphere.

Each substantial update should address four quadrants when relevant:

| | Conditions improving | Conditions worsening |
|---|---|---|
| **Lived outcome** | Which people gained access, quality, time, security or choice? | Which people lost access, quality, time, security or choice? |
| **Preparation** | Which capacity, institution or agreement made the gain durable? | Which prepared action, safeguard or recovery route is needed? |

### Positive-horizon questions

- What valuable action can more people now take?
- Did the improvement reach a previously excluded cohort?
- Did people gain time, health, learning, security, creativity or connection?
- Did meaningful choice, refusal, switching or appeal improve?
- Did total cost fall without hidden payment or externalised harm?
- Did concentration fall or provider exit become easier?
- Did a public or community institution become more capable?
- Did a prior warning fail to materialise, and what did that teach us?

Equal scrutiny does not require false balance. A severe active harm may deserve
most of an update. It does require the system to search for gains with the same
methodological care and to publish evidence that weakens its crisis thesis.

### Language balance

Prefer:

- `possible failure mode` over `coming crisis`;
- `positive pathway` over `promised future`;
- `preparation` over `emergency response` before activation;
- `affected people` over `resistant population`;
- `conditions changed` over `society entered the next phase`;
- `current evidence does not establish` over `safe` when measures are missing.

## 🔁 Cadence and correction symmetry

- Set the next update date before publishing the current one.
- Publish on the expected date even when no state changed.
- Date every change in definition, threshold, model or source.
- Give a material correction the same channels, prominence and audience reach
  as the original error.
- Preserve the incorrect version with a visible correction link.
- Resolve every issued forecast after its horizon, including failed forecasts.
- Record delayed data and missed updates as operational results.

## ✅ Release workflow

1. Draft from a dated evidence object.
2. Assign epistemic and provenance labels sentence by sentence.
3. Complete the seven-part update.
4. Generate audience variants from immutable facts.
5. Run headline and anti-manipulation checks.
6. Run the comprehension and adversarial interpretation test.
7. Obtain approval from the named evidence owner and communication owner.
8. If action is involved, verify authority, funding, safeguards and appeal.
9. Publish all variants with the common evidence card.
10. Archive the exact release and schedule the next check.
