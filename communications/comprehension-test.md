# Comprehension and Adversarial Interpretation Test

> **Proposal status:** Ren, an AI agent, wrote this test. It is not Fernando's
> opinion. Its sample, thresholds and release gates are product acceptance
> proposals, not scientifically validated population benchmarks. They require
> review before use.

## 🦅 TL;DR

- Test whether people understand the evidence, uncertainty, IF change and
  action status, not whether they agree.
- Include supportive, sceptical, directly affected, technical and low-numeracy
  readers.
- Measure dangerous misreadings explicitly: scenario as forecast, coverage as
  risk, aggregate as personal result, association as causation, and proposal as
  commitment.
- Ask how the message could manipulate, stigmatise or centralise power.
- A release fails when comprehension or agency gaps are material, even if
  average trust or approval is high.
- Every stimulus and example in this document is fictional.

---

## 🎯 Test objective

The test asks whether a public update enables a reader to:

1. State what was observed.
2. Identify who the evidence represents.
3. Separate inference, forecast, scenario, value and proposal.
4. Explain which IF changed and what remains unknown.
5. Identify whether an action activated and who owns it.
6. Find help, appeal or a no-action instruction.
7. Name evidence that would reverse the interpretation.
8. Know when the next check occurs.
9. Disagree or leave without pressure.
10. Identify plausible manipulation, misuse and missing perspectives.

Approval, emotional resonance, time on page and click-through rate are not
substitutes for these outcomes.

## 👥 Proposed test panel

For a formative release test, recruit at least 30 participants across the
following perspectives. This is enough to expose recurrent usability failures,
not enough to estimate population opinion precisely.

| Perspective | Minimum inclusion goal |
|---|---|
| Directly affected or analogous lived experience | 6 |
| Exposed but not currently harmed | 4 |
| Essential worker or community representative | 4 |
| Public administrator or delivery practitioner | 3 |
| Business, provider or employer perspective | 3 |
| Strong supporter of rapid technological adoption | 3 |
| Strong sceptic of technological or technocratic transitions | 3 |
| Lower numeracy or data confidence | 6 across the panel |
| Disability or assistive-technology use | 4 across the panel |
| Non-primary-English or translation use | 4 across the panel |

The categories may overlap. Do not recruit or profile by inferred political
belief. Use voluntary self-description and collect only what is necessary for
the test.

### Safeguards for participants

- Explain that the test evaluates the communication, not the participant.
- Do not expose participants to a simulated personal risk score.
- Avoid asking for job, health, immigration or financial details unless
  essential, consented and protected.
- Allow withdrawal without reason.
- Do not share individual responses with employers, service providers or
  enforcement bodies.
- Report subgroup patterns only where re-identification risk is acceptably low.
- Pay people with directly relevant lived experience for their contribution.

## 🧪 Fictional stimulus

Use a rendered update based on the fictional Harbourvale example:

> **[OBSERVED + HYPOTHESIS] Continuity coverage moved from 82% plus or minus 3
> points to 77% plus or minus 4 points for registered Harbourvale records
> clerks. The fictional activation rule requires two confirmed periods below
> 80%, so no support action has activated. Reduced paid hours may contribute.
> An energy-bill change and a benefit-system outage are competing explanations.
> The next check is 15 April.**

Include:

- a seven-part evidence card;
- an instrumentation card reading `7 of 10 indicators available, not a risk
  score`;
- a scenario labelled `Not a forecast`;
- a fictional agent-authored proposed action;
- an institutional commitment that has not activated;
- help, challenge and appeal routes;
- one positive-horizon result;
- full fictional methods.

No name, number, organisation, place, policy or event in the stimulus may be
presented as real.

## 🎤 Facilitator script

### 1. Introduction

Read verbatim:

> Thank you for helping test this communication. We are testing the page, not
> your knowledge. Some information is intentionally uncertain. Please say what
> you believe the page means, even if you think it is wrong or manipulative.
> Nothing here describes a real person, place, organisation or policy. You can
> stop at any time.

Do not explain `capability`, `access`, `IF`, `coverage`, `forecast` or `trigger`
before the unaided tasks.

### 2. Pre-exposure baseline

Ask:

1. On a 1 to 5 scale, how confident are you reading data-based public updates?
2. On a 1 to 5 scale, how worried does the phrase `automation transition
   warning` make you?
3. What is the difference, if any, between a technology being capable of an
   outcome and a person being able to achieve it?

Record verbatim answers and response time.

### 3. One-minute exposure

Show the update for 60 seconds. Do not direct attention or permit scrolling
beyond the first public layer.

Then remove it and ask:

1. In your own words, what happened?
2. Who did the result describe?
3. What did the page say caused it?
4. Did any support or restriction activate?
5. What should a directly affected person do now?
6. When will the result be checked again?

### 4. Evidence-card task

Return the page and allow unrestricted navigation. Ask the participant to point
to:

1. the observation period;
2. the uncertainty;
3. the strongest competing explanation;
4. the IF that changed;
5. the action owner and authority;
6. what would falsify the interpretation;
7. the challenge or appeal route;
8. the next check.

Record accuracy, time and navigation path. Do not coach.

### 5. Dangerous-inference checks

Ask each as a forced choice followed by `Why?`

1. Does `7 of 10 indicators available` mean the crisis probability is 70%, the
   system measured 70% of planned indicators, or neither?
2. Does the scenario show what will probably happen, what could happen under
   chosen assumptions, or what already happened?
3. Does the fictional cohort result apply to every worker in Harbourvale?
4. Did the update establish that automation caused the change?
5. Did the agent proposal become government policy?
6. Did the first threshold movement activate support?
7. Does `no activation` mean nobody is experiencing harm?
8. Does the aggregate change predict this participant's personal outcome?

### 6. IF comprehension

Ask:

1. Complete this sentence from the page: `People can [verb/outcome] IF...`
2. Which condition changed?
3. Was it hypothesised, measured, baselined, trigger-ready or active?
4. Was it unknown, emerging, binding or relieved?
5. What other condition could explain the same observation?
6. What action, if any, is currently justified?

Score lifecycle, binding state and evidence grade independently. Do not require
participants to remember the technical category names if they explain the
meaning correctly.

### 7. Cohort assistance task

Give one randomly assigned role:

- a directly affected fictional records clerk;
- an unaffected Harbourvale resident;
- a union representative;
- an Aster Systems manager;
- a Transition Office caseworker;
- a sceptical civil-society reviewer.

Ask:

1. What information should be the same for every role?
2. What action or assistance is relevant to your role?
3. Can you identify a choice, refusal, challenge or appeal route?
4. Does the message blame anyone in your role?

### 8. Adversarial interpretation

Ask without defending the page:

1. What is this communication trying to make you believe or do?
2. Who benefits if readers accept its framing?
3. Who might carry a cost or lose power?
4. Which fact, downside or alternative appears least visible?
5. What would a strong opponent say is misleading?
6. Does the visual design make any uncertain claim feel more certain?
7. Does the message make political disagreement look irrational or harmful?
8. Could this data be used to surveil, stigmatise or discipline people?
9. Whose perspective or evidence is missing?
10. Would you feel safe submitting a challenge? Why or why not?
11. What would make you trust the process more without asking you to agree?
12. Under what condition should this communication system be stopped?

### 9. Emotional and agency check

Ask:

1. On a 1 to 5 scale, how worried do you feel now?
2. On a 1 to 5 scale, how pressured do you feel to agree or act?
3. On a 1 to 5 scale, how able do you feel to question the claim?
4. On a 1 to 5 scale, how clear is the next action or no-action instruction?
5. What would you do next, if anything?

Do not interpret lower worry as success if it came from misunderstanding, or
higher intended action as success if the update required no action.

### 10. Accessibility and language check

Ask participants using relevant routes to complete the same tasks with:

- keyboard only;
- a screen reader;
- 200% zoom;
- reduced motion;
- a low-bandwidth text version;
- translated content;
- a plain-language evidence card.

Record whether meaning, uncertainty, trigger status and provenance remain
equivalent across formats.

## 📏 Scoring guide

### Core comprehension

| Measure | Proposed release gate |
|---|---:|
| Distinguishes capability from lived access | ≥ 85% |
| Recalls the observed direction and scoped cohort | ≥ 85% |
| Identifies the observation as non-causal | ≥ 85% |
| Identifies no activation after one confirmation period | ≥ 90% |
| Finds uncertainty and data period | ≥ 85% |
| Finds the competing explanation | ≥ 80% |
| Finds the action owner or correct no-action status | ≥ 85% |
| Finds falsifier and next check | ≥ 80% |
| Understands instrumentation coverage is not risk | ≥ 95% |
| Understands a scenario is not a forecast | ≥ 95% |
| Understands a proposal is not a commitment | ≥ 95% |
| Does not generalise cohort evidence to every person | ≥ 90% |

### Agency and trust

| Measure | Proposed release gate |
|---|---:|
| Can identify a challenge, refusal or appeal route | ≥ 85% |
| Reports clarity of action or no-action at 4 or 5 out of 5 | ≥ 80% |
| Reports ability to question at 4 or 5 out of 5 | ≥ 80% |
| Does not report that disagreement is framed as irrational | ≥ 90% |
| Does not report pressure to agree above 2 out of 5 | ≥ 85% |
| Directly affected readers do not report blame | ≥ 90% |
| Correct action intention matches the update status | ≥ 90% |

### Anti-panic

| Measure | Proposed release gate |
|---|---:|
| Participants do not infer imminent crisis | ≥ 90% |
| Participants do not infer a personal risk score | ≥ 95% |
| Mean worry increase | ≤ 0.5 on the 5-point scale for a non-activated warning |
| Any worry increase is accompanied by correct help and action comprehension | ≥ 90% of those reporting increase |

### Equity

For every core comprehension measure:

- No tested perspective group should trail the overall score by more than 15
  percentage points.
- Any assistive, translation or low-bandwidth route must preserve epistemic and
  trigger meaning.
- A severe failure for a directly affected or accessibility cohort blocks
  release even when the overall average passes.

These gates are proposed safeguards, not statistical proof. Small subgroup
numbers require qualitative review and follow-up rather than confident rates.

## 🧮 Response coding

Code unaided answers before reviewing ratings.

### Observation coding

- `2`: Correct measure, direction, cohort and non-causal status.
- `1`: Direction correct but scope or causal status missing.
- `0`: Incorrect, crisis inference, causal overclaim or cannot answer.

### Action coding

- `2`: Correctly says no activation and identifies review or available help.
- `1`: Understands review but is unsure whether support activated.
- `0`: Believes a material action activated, recommends irreversible personal
  action or cannot answer.

### IF coding

- `2`: Identifies condition, state change and at least one alternative.
- `1`: Identifies the condition but not its status or alternative.
- `0`: Treats data availability as the condition or cannot answer.

### Adversarial coding

Tag, without grading the participant:

- hidden advocacy;
- visual certainty inflation;
- implied inevitability;
- blame or stigma;
- political pathologising;
- surveillance or secondary-use risk;
- missing payer or burden;
- missing cohort;
- missing alternative;
- unclear authority;
- inaccessible challenge;
- other.

A repeated tag from three or more participants requires documented review. A
single severe rights, safety or manipulation concern can block release.

## 🚨 Automatic fail conditions

Do not publish when any of these occurs:

- More than 10% believe an unscored pathway is a quantified crisis probability.
- More than 10% believe a scenario is the project's prediction.
- More than 10% believe an agent proposal is authorised policy.
- More than 10% believe support activated when it did not.
- Any audience variant changes a number, uncertainty, forecast probability,
  trigger status or correction history.
- Directly affected testers cannot find immediate help or appeal.
- A material accessibility route changes or hides the claim's meaning.
- The message uses fear, vulnerability or political identity to obtain support.
- Review identifies a plausible surveillance or retaliation route without an
  effective safeguard.
- The release owner cannot state what would falsify the interpretation.

## 🔁 Remediation loop

1. Classify failure as wording, information architecture, visual design,
   evidence, governance, accessibility or concept failure.
2. Correct the smallest underlying cause, not only the test question.
3. Preserve the failed version and rationale.
4. Re-test the failed task with new participants from the affected perspective.
5. Run all dangerous-inference checks again because one wording fix can move
   another inference.
6. Document whether the release passed, remained blocked or changed scope.

Do not average a dangerous misunderstanding away by adding more participants.

## 🧪 Post-release monitoring

Within the first public cycle, examine:

- challenge and appeal submissions;
- support requests inconsistent with activation status;
- citations that repeat the headline without its limit;
- media or social summaries that convert scenario to forecast;
- correction reach compared with original reach;
- differences in comprehension across language and accessibility routes;
- anxiety, stigma or retaliation reports;
- whether the expected cohort used the intended help route;
- whether people could submit contrary evidence safely;
- whether the next check happened on time.

A material public misunderstanding is a communication incident. Correct it with
the same visibility as a data error.
