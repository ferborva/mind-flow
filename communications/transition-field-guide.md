# The Future Has Conditions

> **Agent-proposed public guide. Not policy, prediction or advice.** Ren, an AI
> agent, drafted this guide after Fernando commissioned a clearer public door
> into the project. It does not state Fernando's settled position. It does not authorise action,
> create a service or create a public commitment.

## 🦅 The idea in one minute

Powerful technology can widen what humanity is able to do. It can also widen
the distance between what is technically possible and what real people can
actually reach, afford, trust, choose and sustain.

This project exists to help people see what is changing, for whom, and under
which conditions, while there is still time to widen their choices.

Its core question is simple:

> **Who can do what, to what standard, where and when, if which conditions hold?**

That final **IF** matters. A model may be capable of tutoring a child, helping a
doctor or accelerating a small business. The valuable outcome exists only if
quality, access, language, time, infrastructure, rights, safety, agency and
delivery hold for the people concerned.

This does not predict one inevitable future, declare a crisis or prescribe one
political answer. It maps possible paths, makes the conditions behind them
visible, and asks whether people and legitimate institutions can prepare
before choices close.

Unknown does not mean safe. Unknown does not mean harmful. It means the
evidence cannot yet support either conclusion.

## 🌳 The future is a tree, not a train

Public debate often sounds as if technology places society on a single track.
One group announces automatic abundance. Another announces automatic ruin.
Both skip the branch points.

Consider a fictional worker, Mara. A new system can complete part of her job.
That fact alone does not tell us whether Mara will gain more time, earn more,
move into better work, lose hours, carry a heavier workload, or be excluded
from the change.

Those outcomes depend on conditions:

- whether the system works in Mara's actual setting;
- whether her employer redesigns work or simply removes labour;
- whether gains are shared through pay, time, price or public provision;
- whether Mara has notice, representation and meaningful alternatives;
- whether support exists before income or services are interrupted; and
- whether an affected person can challenge an error and obtain a remedy.

Each condition creates branches. Conditions also interact. Cheap capability
without reliable electricity is not access. Training without available roles
is not transition. A payment without reachable delivery may not protect a
household. Choice without a viable alternative is not meaningful agency.

The point is not to predict Mara from a national average. It is to expose the
conditions that separate the possible paths, learn which path is forming, and
preserve room to choose.

## 🧩 Complete the promise

The public mnemonic is:

> **verb + fill in the blank + IF**

The full form is:

```text
[WHO] can [VERB] [OBJECT] at [STANDARD],
in [PLACE], during [PERIOD],
if [REGISTERED CONDITIONS].
```

For example:

> People seeking entry-level accounting work in a named Australian region can
> reach work with replacement earnings above an agreed floor during the next
> twelve months if suitable roles exist, applicants can access them, selection
> is fair, transition support arrives in time, and the measurement covers the
> people named.

This example is a testable sentence, not a current finding. Each bracket must
be filled before evidence is attached.

Every IF also declares its logical role. A condition may be **necessary**, **one
sufficient route** among alternatives, enabling, inhibiting, correlated only,
or a decision rule rather than a cause. The logic is **open world**. Passing all
registered conditions means only that one registered route passed. An omitted
condition may still defeat the outcome.

That gives the public a stronger question than “is the future going well?”:

> **Which outcome, for whom, and which condition is doing the work?**

## 🔄 Conditions are allowed to change, silently rewriting them is not

An IF is not a permanent checkbox. A condition can change because the world
changed, the evidence improved, a population was overlooked, a definition
failed, or legitimate disagreement remains.

The condition history therefore records eleven kinds of change:

| Change | Public meaning |
|---|---|
| `added` | A newly declared condition enters the route. |
| `narrowed` | Its wording or scope becomes more specific. |
| `split` | One condition becomes distinct conditions without hiding who or what moved. |
| `merged` | Conditions combine only when their meaning and scope remain traceable. |
| `challenged` | A reasoned objection is opened. |
| `satisfied` | Registered evidence passed the declared evaluation rule. |
| `failed` | Registered evidence failed that rule. |
| `expired` | The condition state is too old or outside its validity window. |
| `superseded` | A visible replacement takes over. The old record remains. |
| `disputed` | Legitimate reviewers do not share one interpretation. This is not false. |
| `withdrawn` | The author removes the condition from active use without erasing it. |

Any consequential update should answer:

1. What changed in the condition?
2. Who changed it?
3. What evidence and authority permitted the change?
4. Which people, place, period and decision now have a different scope?
5. What earlier conclusion is no longer valid?
6. What happens if the new condition fails?
7. When will the change be reviewed?

The current state alone is not enough. A green condition can be misleading if
its population was narrowed after the result appeared. A merged condition can
hide disagreement. A satisfied condition can expire. The history is part of
the claim.

## 📡 Seven signals, never one synthetic verdict

A warning is more useful when different kinds of evidence have different jobs.
For each important condition, the project seeks seven roles:

| Signal role | Question it helps answer |
|---|---|
| `leading` | Is a relevant change appearing early enough to investigate? |
| `confirming` | Does an independent collection process see compatible movement? |
| `counter` | What evidence could weaken or reverse the current reading? |
| `outcome` | Did the human result we care about actually change? |
| `readiness` | Could a proposed response really be delivered now? |
| `intervention-exposure` | Who received the intervention, when and at what intensity? |
| `information-harm` | Did the warning itself create anxiety, stigma, false certainty or harmful action? |

A leading signal is not a verdict. A confirming signal cannot be the same
measurement under a new name. A forecast is not a cause. Readiness is not
authority. A good aggregate can hide a harmed group.

There is **no single transition score**. Compressing capability, access,
distribution, preparedness, rights and public harm into one number would hide
the very disagreements the project needs to expose.

## 🚦 From observation to a legitimate next step

Evidence and action are separate records. The Observatory may show what a
declared rule evaluated. It cannot supply authority, consent, money, delivery
capacity, help or appeal.

Possible action states use seven explicit IFs:

```text
watch_if     investigate a scoped change
prepare_if   take a low-regret, reversible preparation step
act_if       use an approved and funded response
pause_if     stop escalation while a hard condition is unresolved
reverse_if   unwind an action that failed or caused unacceptable harm
recover_if   repair continuity, loss, exclusion or trust
graduate_if  end temporary support only after the protected outcome holds
```

Every step must separately assess the **safety of acting** and the **safety of waiting**.
“No automated action” does not mean inaction is safe. It means no
machine is authorised to make the choice.

Before any real activation, a named institution would need to verify at least:

- its legal and democratic authority;
- affected-party participation and consent where required;
- secured funding for the whole obligation;
- tested delivery capacity and dependencies;
- a reachable help route with service levels;
- independent appeal, correction and remedy;
- a pause and reversal path; and
- an expiry or graduation rule.

If these are not verified, the public wording is plain: **No Observatory-linked service exists.**

## 🛠 Preparation is different at every scale

These are categories for public debate, not instructions or existing services.

| Scale | Reversible preparation to evaluate | Hard question before action |
|---|---|---|
| Person | Check whether evidence covers the person's situation, preserve records, identify trusted advice and avoid irreversible choices from a dashboard. | Does the person freely choose the step, and is real help available? |
| Community | Map essential dependencies, excluded groups, trusted local channels and service gaps without turning voluntary care into an institutional substitute. | Who has a mandate, who may refuse, and who carries the workload and cost? |
| Institution | Pre-negotiate triggers, funding, staffing, service levels, appeal, rollback and recovery while testing delivery under failure. | Are authority, capacity and every required hand-off independently verified? |
| Country | Define minimum floors, distributional measures, local variation, fiscal options, legal routes, cross-border dependencies and democratic review before a shock. | Which body can commit, who bears the cost, and can affected groups alter the design? |

Preparation should expand options before a crisis. It should not manufacture a
crisis to justify itself. It should remain reversible until the evidence,
authority and safeguards needed for action are present.

## 🔍 How to read any Observatory update

Ask seven questions:

1. **What was observed?** Check the measure, source, place, period and
   uncertainty.
2. **Who does it cover?** Look for the population, exclusions and distribution,
   not only the average.
3. **What is inferred?** Separate explanations, forecasts and scenarios from
   observations.
4. **Which IF changed?** Inspect its old state, new state, scope, evidence and
   complete history.
5. **What happens now?** Distinguish investigation, preparation, authorised
   action and no action.
6. **What would change this reading?** Look for counterevidence and a genuine
   falsifier.
7. **When will it be checked again?** Find the review date, expected evidence
   and expiry.

If a required answer is absent, treat it as unknown. Do not supply the missing
certainty yourself.

## 🙋 What you can do today

You do not need to accept the thesis to use the method.

1. Take one promise about AI, work, health, education, energy or public
   services.
2. Complete it: who can do what, at which standard, where and when, if what?
3. Check whether the scope actually includes you or the people being discussed.
4. Mark what is observed, inferred, forecast, proposed, committed and unknown.
5. Ask which condition is necessary, which is only one sufficient route, and
   what omitted condition could change the outcome.
6. Ask what would change this reading and when the evidence will be checked.
7. Challenge the evidence, condition, authority or effect through the published
   route. If no route exists, that absence is itself a governance defect.

The same evidence can support more than one legitimate value choice. People
retain the right to disagree, organise, negotiate, propose alternatives and the
right to refuse. Participation does not require agreement with the abundance
thesis or consent to a proposed action.

## 🌅 What success would look like

The destination is not a perfect dashboard. It is a society in which expanding
capability becomes expanding human agency: more people able to learn, create,
care, move, contribute and choose a worthwhile life, with fewer avoidable
constraints and durable protection when systems fail.

The method earns a place only if it helps people understand possible paths and
act earlier without increasing manipulation, stigma, anxiety or false
certainty. That proposition must be tested against simpler public information,
not assumed.

The future may contain extraordinary abundance. **The work is to make its IFs
visible, contestable and achievable for real people.**
