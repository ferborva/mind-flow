# Public Comprehension and Affected-Party Review Protocol

> **Agent analysis. Not approved.** Ren, a Codex agent, drafted this protocol at Fernando Bordallo's request. It is not Fernando's opinion, research ethics approval, Indigenous governance approval, accessibility certification, or authority to recruit participants. Every threshold, payment rule, participant group and release gate requires human review before use.

## Decision

**Do not publish the Observatory merely because its words are accurate or its interface passes automated tests. Publish only when intended readers can correctly explain what the page means, what it does not mean, whether any action is authorised, and how to challenge it.**

The review has two stages:

1. Iterative formative review identifies and repairs misunderstandings, harms and exclusions.
2. A separately recruited confirmatory study tests one frozen release candidate against pre-registered gates.

Agreement with the abundance thesis is not a success measure. Trust, approval, visual appeal, time on page and willingness to share are secondary observations. They cannot compensate for misunderstanding, inaccessible dissent, false authority, unsafe action or loss of data sovereignty.

## Questions this protocol must answer

1. Can a reader state what was observed, for whom, where, when and with what uncertainty?
2. Can they distinguish observation, inference, hypothesis, scenario, forecast, value, proposal and commitment?
3. Can they explain which condition changed, or correctly say that none did?
4. Can they distinguish instrumentation coverage from progress, evidence strength, risk and probability?
5. Can they identify current action, owner, authority and help or appeal, or correctly say none exists?
6. Can they name what would change the reading and when the next check occurs?
7. Can they disagree, refuse, challenge or leave without pressure or penalty?
8. Does the interface preserve the same meaning across disability, language, literacy, device and bandwidth routes?
9. Do affected parties judge the framing, burdens, benefits and proposed actions as recognisable and non-stigmatising?
10. Where Indigenous peoples or data are in scope, did the relevant Indigenous authority control participation, data use, interpretation and publication?

## Governance before recruitment

No participant contact begins until all items below are recorded.

| Required item | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| Named study owner | Person accountable for participant welfare, incidents and stopping the study |
| Independent facilitator | Facilitator who did not write the release candidate and is not rewarded for a pass |
| Ethics determination | Written determination of whether formal human research ethics review is required |
| Participant information | Plain-language purpose, procedures, risks, payment, data use, withdrawal and complaint route |
| Consent | Voluntary, specific and revocable consent for participation, recording, quotation and future contact, captured separately |
| Data plan | Fields collected, reason, access, storage, retention, deletion, publication and incident response |
| Safeguarding plan | Distress, disclosure, retaliation, employment, immigration, benefit and financial vulnerability routes |
| Compensation plan | Rates, expenses, accessibility costs, community payments and payment timing |
| Dissent owner | Independent person accountable for preserving and resolving minority reports |
| Release owner | Human with explicit authority to keep release blocked |
| Indigenous governance determination | Whether Indigenous peoples, communities, knowledges, places or data are in scope and what authority governs them |

The [NHMRC National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research 2025](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/ethics/national-statement-ethical-conduct-human-research) is the Australian baseline for human research ethics. It requires voluntary participation and attention to privacy, recruitment, payment, withdrawal and data management. The protocol should receive an independent ethics determination rather than assuming that product research is exempt.

## Study design

### Stage A: formative review

Run at least three waves of 12 to 16 participants. Each wave must include directly affected, low-data-confidence, disability, language and dissenting perspectives. Revise only between waves, preserve each stimulus and change log, and recruit new participants after each material revision.

Formative review is complete only when:

- two consecutive waves reveal no new critical harm category;
- every observed critical misunderstanding has a documented root cause and resolution;
- no known stop condition remains in the candidate;
- directly affected and accessibility reviewers confirm that their recorded stop conditions are resolved, or the candidate remains blocked, without being asked to endorse the thesis;
- the dissent register records why every unresolved objection is accepted, mitigated, deferred or release-blocking.

Small-wave percentages are diagnostic, not claims about a population. Report counts and words, not just rates.

### Stage B: frozen confirmatory study

Freeze one candidate, content hash, data snapshot, task script, coding guide, analysis code and stop/go criteria before recruitment. Register any exclusions and missing-data rules. Do not change the candidate, script, task order or primary analysis after viewing results.

Target **180 completed participants**, assigned to one primary perspective group of 30 for analysis:

| Primary perspective group | Minimum complete participants |
|---|---:|
| Directly affected workers and households in the named release scope | 30 |
| Community, union, mutual-aid, advocacy and frontline service representatives | 30 |
| Public administrators, service-delivery workers, caseworkers and appeal practitioners | 30 |
| Employers, providers, technical practitioners and market participants | 30 |
| General public with lower numeracy or lower confidence using data | 30 |
| General public with moderate or higher data confidence, balanced across thesis support and scepticism | 30 |

Primary assignment must be declared before analysis. People may belong to more than one perspective, but must not be counted twice in the primary total.

Closely analogous participants may supplement formative work or sensitivity analysis, but they cannot satisfy the directly affected minimum or replace a missing directly affected perspective. Before recruitment, affected representatives must help define what `directly affected` means for the named claim, service, place and period. Report the direct and analogous counts separately.

Across the 180, meet these cross-cutting minimums:

- 36 disabled participants or assistive-technology users;
- 12 screen-reader users across at least two major screen-reader and browser combinations;
- 12 participants with cognitive, learning, memory or attention access needs;
- 36 people who use a language other than English at home;
- 30 users for each translated route that will be publicly released;
- 24 regional, rural or remote participants if the release claims Australian scope;
- 30 people who self-describe as strongly supportive of rapid technology adoption;
- 30 people who self-describe as strongly sceptical of technological or technocratic transitions;
- meaningful spread by age, gender, income security and employment arrangement, reported without exposing individuals.

Do not infer political ideology, disability, Indigeneity, income or vulnerability from other data. Use voluntary self-description and collect only the minimum needed for declared analysis.

### Test the instrument, not only its layout

The confirmatory study must compare whether the Observatory adds decision value over a simpler public instrument. Randomly assign each participant's first exposure to one of three arms using the same facts, source vintage, uncertainty and scope:

1. **Conventional statistical release:** a short official-statistics-style release with source, scope, uncertainty, correction and contact information.
2. **Statistical release plus deliberation:** the same release followed by a facilitated public deliberation process.
3. **Observatory plus deliberation:** the Observatory followed by the same deliberation process, facilitator script, duration and participation rights as arm 2.

Primary analysis uses the first exposure only. Pre-register comprehension, trust calibration, false authority, anxiety, action appropriateness, correction speed and willingness to challenge. Record whether any arm prompts unsafe personal, employer, market, media or government action.

The six perspective-group minimums remain release gates, not proof that the comparison is statistically powered. A statistician must set the required arm and perspective-cell sample before recruitment. Ten participants per arm within a 30-person perspective group cannot support a confirmatory superiority claim. **If the Observatory does not add material decision value within affected-party-approved harm bounds, use the simpler instrument.**

### Indigenous participation is governance, not a demographic quota

Do not recruit Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander people to satisfy a diversity cell. If the Observatory studies, compares, labels or may affect Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander peoples, communities, Country, knowledges or data, establish an Indigenous-led governance agreement before design or recruitment.

The agreement must identify:

- the community or communities in scope and who has authority to speak for the proposed work;
- Indigenous leadership and decision rights across questions, recruitment, facilitation, analysis and publication;
- community benefit, reciprocity, risk and resourcing;
- ownership, access, storage, location, linkage, reuse, retention, deletion and return of data;
- control of quotations, stories, labels, geographic detail and community-level findings;
- review of interpretations before publication without demanding endorsement;
- the right to pause, narrow or withhold community data or findings;
- future-use rules, including whether fresh consent or approval is required;
- a culturally safe complaint, repair and dispute route;
- professional payment for governance, design, interpretation and review.

The [AIATSIS Code of Ethics](https://aiatsis.gov.au/research/ethical-research) centres Indigenous self-determination, leadership, impact and value, sustainability and accountability. AIATSIS states that research involving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples requires ethics review and approval before it begins. The [CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples) add collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility and ethics. These are governance duties, not a checklist that the project team can self-certify.

If Indigenous peoples or data are not currently in scope, record that boundary. Do not describe an Australian result as nationally complete when Indigenous outcomes are absent, suppressed or governed separately.

## Recruitment and compensation

### Recruitment safeguards

- Use independent recruiters where an employer, government service, benefit provider or union relationship could create pressure.
- Never let managers, caseworkers or service providers know who accepted, declined or withdrew.
- State that participation has no effect on employment, benefits, services, immigration matters, advocacy or future eligibility.
- Do not recruit people during an acute personal crisis merely because their distress is relevant.
- Offer asynchronous, remote, in-person, assisted and support-person routes.
- Let participants choose a facilitator of a preferred gender or cultural background where practical.
- Make recording optional. Participation without audio or video must remain possible.
- Pay people who withdraw for the time already reserved or completed. Do not condition payment on finishing or agreeing.

### Minimum compensation standard

Before recruitment, publish the rate and method. At minimum:

- pay the greater of the locally applicable living-wage hourly equivalent or the organisation's standard research-participant rate;
- pay a minimum of two hours for a 60 to 90 minute session, covering preparation and debrief;
- reimburse travel, parking, childcare, caring support, mobile data and other direct participation costs separately;
- fund interpreters, captioners, support people and assistive technology as study costs, never deductions from participant payment;
- pay community representatives, accessibility specialists, Indigenous governance partners and expert reviewers at an agreed professional consulting rate;
- offer accessible payment methods, including bank transfer and alternatives where a transfer could affect safety or access;
- issue payment within five business days, including after withdrawal;
- state any tax or benefit implications the project is qualified to state, and otherwise direct participants to independent advice without collecting unnecessary financial data.

The NHMRC National Statement says reimbursement of participation costs is generally appropriate and payment for time may be appropriate, while disproportionate inducement to take risks is unacceptable. Payment must recognise labour without purchasing consent or silence.

## Stimulus set

Use fictional but structurally realistic data for formative work. The confirmatory release candidate may use real public data only after evidence, privacy and governance gates pass.

Every participant sees these stimulus types in counterbalanced order:

1. the first-screen public update;
2. a complete seven-part evidence card;
3. one instrumentation-coverage display;
4. one positive path and one possible failure path;
5. one scenario with adjustable assumptions;
6. one agent proposal;
7. one institutional commitment that has not activated;
8. one activated fictional commitment with help and appeal;
9. one correction record;
10. one screenshot or copied excerpt separated from its page context;
11. one entity change where data is missing and a fallback would otherwise occur;
12. one low-bandwidth, translated or assistive presentation matching the participant's route.

Do not use fabricated content that resembles a real participant's exact employer, suburb, condition or benefit status. Do not present a simulated personal risk score.

## Challenge the seven-part update itself

Seven fields may be complete for governance and still overload a public reader. The labels `Affected`, `Inferred`, `IF changed` and `Falsifier` may be interpreted differently from their intended meanings. Do not protect the framework from this result.

### Formative variant test

Within the Observatory arm, test these layout variants with equivalent content:

**Variant A: compact institutional labels**

`Observed | Affected | Inferred | IF changed | Action and owner | Falsifier | Next check`

**Variant B: plain questions**

`What do we know? | Who does this cover? | What do we not know? | Which condition changed? | What happens now? | What would change this reading? | When will we check again?`

**Variant C: two-layer sequence**

First layer: `What changed? | Does it apply to me? | What happens now?`

Expandable evidence layer: the remaining seven-part contract, with no change in facts, uncertainty or authority.

Use a randomised first exposure and counterbalanced follow-up. Select the confirmatory variant by unaided comprehension, harmful-misunderstanding rate and accessibility equivalence, not preference or aesthetic ratings. If no variant clears the formative stop conditions, redesign the information architecture rather than renaming labels again.

### Seven-part tasks

After 60 seconds without scrolling, remove the page and ask the participant to explain:

1. what was measured and what direction it moved;
2. who and where it describes;
3. what remains unknown or only inferred;
4. whether a condition changed;
5. whether anything happens now and under whose authority;
6. what evidence would change the reading;
7. when it will be checked again;
8. what they would do next if they were directly affected.

Return the page and ask them to locate each answer without coaching. Record correctness, time, navigation path, hesitations and quotations.

### Seven-part failure interpretations

- `Affected` is read as proof that named people were harmed.
- `Inferred` is read as a lower-confidence observation rather than an interpretation.
- `IF changed` is read as a risk score or forecast.
- `Action and owner` is read as an instruction to the user rather than the status of an authorised response.
- `Falsifier` is not understood, or is read as evidence that the whole thesis is false.
- `Next check` is read as a promise that new data will arrive rather than a planned review with a delay rule.
- Coverage counts are recalled as the observed result.
- The seven fields are recalled individually but combined into a false causal story.

## Challenge IF language itself

`IF` is memorable, but it carries three risks. It can sound like every listed condition is strictly necessary, like meeting all conditions guarantees the outcome, or like the project has mapped the complete causal system. It may also be inaccessible jargon when paired with lifecycle and binding-state labels.

Test `IF` against the plain word `condition` and against this complete sentence:

> A specified person or group can **[verb] [object]**, in **[place]**, for **[period]**, if **[observable conditions]** hold, according to **[evidence]**, with **[help or remedy]** if the claim or service fails.

Without teaching the framework, ask participants to:

1. complete a promise using their own verb and object;
2. identify the person, place, period and conditions;
3. distinguish a condition being measured from a condition being satisfied;
4. explain whether `unknown` means failed;
5. explain whether all listed conditions are necessary, sufficient, both or undecided;
6. identify which condition changed and which did not;
7. give an alternative condition that could explain the same observation;
8. state what evidence could move the condition state;
9. negotiate a hypothetical commitment using `Action by owner if condition, with safeguard, review and exit`;
10. identify who can dispute the condition and what remedy follows if the decision is wrong.

Use meaning-based coding. Do not require people to remember internal terms such as `trigger-ready`, `binding` or `relieved` if they can accurately explain the state and consequence.

### IF failure interpretations

- `unknown` means failure or zero.
- an available measure means the condition holds.
- one condition changing means total transition risk rose.
- the five layers are a complete causal model.
- meeting the conditions guarantees abundance.
- failing one condition makes beneficial action impossible.
- `IF` is the project's forecast rather than a decision predicate.
- condition names are universal across service, cohort, place and time.
- a negotiated IF removes a person's right to refuse or appeal.
- an agent may activate an action because a condition appears satisfied.

## Participant tasks

### Task 1: first-glance authority

Show the first screen for 10 seconds. Ask who made the page, whether it is approved, whether it is public policy, whether any action is active, and how certain it is. Repeat using a cropped screenshot that excludes browser chrome.

### Task 2: unaided narrative

After 60 seconds, remove the page. Ask: `What happened, to whom, why, and what happens next?` Code every clause by claim class and whether the page supported it.

### Task 3: seven-part retrieval

Ask the participant to find each of the seven fields. Record accuracy and time. Ask what each field means in their own words.

### Task 4: entity and cohort scope

Change from World to Australia, then to a place with missing data. Ask what changed, which values are still global, who is excluded and whether the page describes them personally.

### Task 5: instrumentation trap

Show `7 of 10 inputs available`. Ask whether this means 70% risk, 70% evidence, 70% preparedness, 70% progress, or only seven planned inputs are present. Require explanation, not forced choice alone.

### Task 6: scenario trap

Let the participant change assumptions. Ask whether the output is observed, predicted, probable or arithmetic under chosen rates. Ask what seeded the initial rates and whether the result should activate an action.

### Task 7: proposal and commitment

Present an agent proposal beside a non-activated institutional commitment. Ask which is authorised, who owns each, what condition activates anything, what help exists now and who can appeal.

### Task 8: affected-person action

Assign a role with bounded fictional circumstances. Ask the participant what they would do now. Any irreversible action, delayed urgent help, false support application or assumed legal obligation is reviewed as a possible safety incident.

### Task 9: correction

Show the current claim and correction record. Ask what was wrong, what changed, which prior decisions might be affected and where the original remains visible.

### Task 10: dissent and challenge

Ask the participant to submit a challenge without creating an account, revealing protected identity or agreeing with the thesis. Test confirmation, tracking, response expectation, escalation and withdrawal.

### Task 11: adversarial interpretation

Ask who benefits from the framing, who carries burdens, what is missing, what feels manipulative, what the visual design makes too certain, how the data could be misused, and what would justify stopping the Observatory.

### Task 12: accessibility-equivalent journey

Repeat Tasks 1, 3, 6, 7 and 10 using the participant's access route. The task and required meaning remain the same. A simplified route may reduce words but cannot remove scope, uncertainty, provenance, authority, help, appeal or correction.

## Harmful misunderstandings to code

Code unaided answers before asking forced choices. Use these predefined categories:

| Code | Harmful misunderstanding |
|---|---|
| H01 | Instrumentation coverage is crisis risk, probability, evidence strength, preparedness or progress |
| H02 | A scenario is the project's forecast or most likely future |
| H03 | Association or a derived series establishes AI or technology causation |
| H04 | A global or national aggregate describes a cohort or individual |
| H05 | An agent proposal is approved policy or an institutional commitment |
| H06 | An unscored path is a diagnosed or imminent crisis |
| H07 | A threshold observation automatically activated support or restriction |
| H08 | `No authorised action` means no person is currently harmed |
| H09 | A help, case-owner or appeal service exists when it does not |
| H10 | `Unknown` IF means failed IF |
| H11 | Available evidence means the IF condition is satisfied |
| H12 | The five IF layers form a complete predictive or causal model |
| H13 | Meeting all displayed conditions guarantees abundance |
| H14 | Failing one condition justifies coercive, irreversible or emergency action |
| H15 | The dashboard grants permission to identify, profile, surveil or discipline people |
| H16 | Public or aggregate Indigenous data can be reused without the relevant authority |
| H17 | Dissent indicates irrationality, selfishness or opposition to progress |
| H18 | The correction fully repairs prior decisions merely because wording changed |
| H19 | A `next check` date guarantees data availability or action |
| H20 | The project's desired positive horizon is an inevitable or officially endorsed future |

Record whether each error came from recall, wording, missing information, visual hierarchy, interaction, prior belief or a combination. Do not describe participant misunderstanding as low intelligence or user error.

## Qualitative dissent and minority reports

Provide spoken, written, anonymous, assisted and representative routes. Dissent collection must be facilitated by someone who can criticise the project without consequence.

For each material objection, record:

- a stable dissent ID;
- the participant's words or a participant-approved paraphrase;
- affected group and context at the least identifying level;
- whether it concerns evidence, framing, values, power, burden, rights, access, action or data use;
- severity and possible harm;
- requested change or condition for release;
- owner, response, due date and status;
- whether the participant or representative accepted the description of their objection;
- the final decision and rationale;
- whether the public limitations or methods record must include it.

No majority vote can close a severe minority report. One credible account of unsafe action, retaliation, stigma, inaccessible help, coercion or loss of community control pauses the current candidate until an independent reviewer decides whether it is resolved. `Disagrees with thesis` is never, by itself, a defect to fix.

Participants choose separately whether a quotation may be used internally, publicly, anonymously or with attribution. Removing a quotation does not remove the underlying issue from the study record.

## Accessibility protocol

Treat [WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) as a minimum technical gate, not proof of accessibility. W3C notes that WCAG does not address every need, particularly across cognitive, language and learning disabilities, so conformance must be paired with affected-user testing.

Test at least:

- keyboard-only use, including visible focus and no traps;
- VoiceOver with Safari on macOS and iOS;
- NVDA with Firefox or Chrome on Windows;
- TalkBack with Chrome on Android;
- 200% and 400% zoom, reflow at 320 CSS pixels, large text and text spacing;
- high-contrast and forced-colour modes;
- reduced motion and motion disabled;
- colour-independent status and chart reading;
- data tables and text alternatives for every chart;
- headings, landmarks, names, roles, values and live status messages;
- touch target size and mobile orientation;
- low-bandwidth text-only use;
- print, copied text and cropped screenshots;
- plain-language and translated routes;
- challenge, help and appeal as complete accessible processes.

Technical conformance requires automated scans plus manual expert review. Human task success must be reported separately for each tested access route. An assistive route fails if it hides or changes evidence status, uncertainty, authority, action, help, appeal, correction or next check.

## Data handling

- Assign random participant IDs. Store contact and payment data separately from responses.
- Collect no employer, service, health, immigration, benefit or location detail beyond what the approved analysis requires.
- Do not link research responses to Observatory monitoring data, advertising profiles or public records.
- Do not use responses to infer political affiliation, vulnerability, employability, eligibility or personal risk.
- Restrict raw access to named researchers and community-governed access holders.
- Define deletion dates before recruitment. Keep identifiable recordings for the shortest approved period.
- Make transcript correction and withdrawal rules clear, including any point after which aggregate deletion is impossible.
- Report small cells and intersections only when re-identification risk is acceptably low and the relevant governance permits it.
- Treat a privacy, retaliation or unauthorised secondary-use concern as an incident with pause, notification, remedy and deletion procedures.
- Apply the Indigenous governance agreement wherever it is more protective than the general data plan.

## Coding and analysis

### Independent coding

Two coders independently score all primary comprehension answers using the frozen rubric. They reconcile disagreements without seeing approval, trust or demographic ratings. Report raw agreement and Krippendorff's alpha or another pre-registered reliability statistic. If reliability for a primary construct is below 0.80, clarify the rubric and recode all responses before any go decision.

### Score levels

Use task-specific anchors:

- `2`: correct meaning, scope and consequence in the participant's own words;
- `1`: partly correct, but omits a material qualifier without reaching a harmful conclusion;
- `0`: incorrect, cannot answer, or states a harmful misunderstanding.

Publish numerator, denominator, percentage and a pre-registered 95% interval for every primary outcome. Missing answers count as incorrect unless a documented technical or facilitator failure made the task invalid. Do not silently exclude slow readers, assistive-technology failures or translation failures.

### Critical incidents

These are not averaged into a score:

- a participant would take an irreversible personal action based on the page;
- a directly affected participant would delay urgent real help because the page implied support existed;
- an inaccessible route hides action, authority, help, appeal or correction;
- a participant identifies a plausible retaliation, surveillance or stigma path without an effective safeguard;
- Indigenous data, identity or knowledge is used outside agreed authority;
- a facilitator coaches a participant toward agreement or a correct answer;
- a release decision suppresses a material dissent record.

Any critical incident stops the candidate. It requires incident review, remediation and a new affected-participant test before confirmatory work can resume.

## Quantitative stop/go criteria

All gates are conjunctive. A strong average cannot offset a failed subgroup, access route or critical incident.

For the quantitative tables, a `priority group` means each of the six primary groups, the aggregate disabled or assistive-technology group, the aggregate language-other-than-English group, each public translated route, and the strong-supporter and strong-sceptic groups. Each requires at least 30 completed participants before a rate is used for a go decision. Individual device or assistive-technology routes with fewer than 30 participants use the stricter zero-blocking-failure rule instead of a percentage.

### Dangerous-misunderstanding gates

| Outcome | Overall go threshold | Priority-group go threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| Correctly identifies coverage as input availability only | at least 95% | at least 90% in every priority group |
| Correctly identifies scenario as conditional arithmetic, not forecast | at least 95% | at least 90% in every group |
| Correctly distinguishes agent proposal from commitment | at least 95% | at least 90% in every group |
| Correctly says no crisis is scored or imminent when none is | at least 95% | at least 90% in every group |
| Correctly identifies no activation when none occurred | at least 95% | at least 90% in every group |
| Does not generalise aggregate evidence to a person or cohort | at least 95% | at least 90% in every group |
| Correctly says descriptive movement does not establish AI causation | at least 95% | at least 90% in every group |
| Correctly distinguishes IF evidence availability from IF satisfaction | at least 90% | at least 85% in every group |
| Correctly says unknown does not mean failed | at least 95% | at least 90% in every group |

For each first seven outcomes, the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound must also be at least 90% overall. For the last two IF outcomes, it must be at least 85%. Report the chosen interval method in the registration.

### Seven-part and agency gates

| Outcome | Overall go threshold | Priority-group go threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| States observed measure, direction, place, population and period | at least 90% | at least 85% |
| Finds source, vintage and uncertainty | at least 90% | at least 85% |
| Identifies affected scope and exclusions | at least 90% | at least 85% |
| Separates inference and strongest competing explanation | at least 85% | at least 80% |
| Identifies IF state or correctly says no change established | at least 90% | at least 85% |
| Finds action mode, owner and authority, or correctly says none | at least 95% | at least 90% |
| Finds a concrete falsifier | at least 85% | at least 80% |
| Finds next check and delay treatment | at least 90% | at least 85% |
| Chooses action consistent with current status | at least 95% | at least 90% |
| Finds challenge, refusal or appeal without help | at least 95% | at least 90%, and 100% for directly affected participants when an action is active |
| Reports ability to question at 4 or 5 out of 5 | at least 85% | at least 80% |
| Reports pressure to agree or act at no more than 2 out of 5 | at least 90% | at least 85% |

No priority group may trail the overall score by more than 10 percentage points on a primary outcome. Do not calculate a group rate with fewer than 30 completed participants. Recruit more or report that the confirmatory gate was not tested.

### Automatic stop thresholds

Stop and remediate if any of these occurs:

- any critical incident;
- 5% or more overall, or 10% or more in any group, expresses H01 through H07, H09, H12, H14, H15 or H16;
- 10% or more believes `no authorised action` means no one is harmed;
- any public route changes a number, uncertainty, epistemic class, scope, IF state, action authority, correction or next check;
- any directly affected participant cannot find real help or appeal when a commitment is active;
- any disabled participant is blocked from a required task by the release candidate;
- any translated route changes proposal, forecast, uncertainty, action or authority meaning;
- any Indigenous governance condition is unmet;
- any severe minority report remains without an independent disposition;
- the named release owner cannot explain the observation, strongest alternative, falsifier, action authority and rollback plan.

Automatic stops override percentage go thresholds. At a group size of 30, three participants are exactly 10%, so three harmful interpretations covered by the rule stop the candidate. They cannot pass through the `at least 90%` comprehension boundary.

## Exact pre-publication gates

The release record must mark each gate `PASS` with evidence. `Partial`, `planned`, `not applicable without rationale`, or agent assertion does not pass.

### G0. Candidate freeze

- Content, code, schema, snapshot and dependency hashes recorded.
- All public labels survive screen, print, export, copy and screenshot crops.
- No material change after confirmatory recruitment starts.

### G1. Evidence and IF integrity

- One validated source object drives the seven-part update and all audience routes.
- Observation, inference, scenario, forecast, proposal and commitment are structurally distinct.
- Every IF is scoped to verb, object, population, place and period, with previous state, current state, evidence grade, dependencies, falsifier and next check.
- Coverage cannot set IF, risk or action state.
- Independent evidence reviewer signs the record.

### G2. Authority and action legitimacy

- Every public action is either a clearly marked proposal or a valid commitment.
- A commitment names owner, authority, funding, trigger, service level, safeguards, help, appeal, override, review, stop rule and exit.
- No actor is selected by default and no agent can activate a public action.
- Directly affected representatives and the accountable institution review the action meaning separately.

### G3. Ethics and participant welfare

- Independent ethics determination complete and any required approval current.
- Consent, safeguarding, compensation, withdrawal and complaint processes tested.
- No unresolved participant incident or overdue payment.
- Study owner signs the record.

### G4. Indigenous governance and data sovereignty

- Scope determination completed by a suitably qualified human reviewer.
- Where in scope, the relevant Indigenous authority approves governance, data and publication conditions through its own process.
- CARE and AIATSIS responsibilities are mapped to evidence, not self-attested.
- Any community request to pause, narrow or withhold governed material is honoured.

### G5. Accessibility equivalence

- WCAG 2.2 AA technical review passes for the complete process.
- Manual assistive-technology, zoom, reflow, contrast, motion, mobile, low-bandwidth, print and copy tests pass.
- Public language and translation routes preserve all consequential meaning.
- Disabled participants complete the same primary tasks at the required thresholds.
- Independent accessibility lead signs the record.

### G6. Formative saturation

- Minimum three waves completed.
- Two consecutive waves produce no new critical harm category.
- All critical findings resolved and retested with new affected participants.
- Version history and dissent register complete.

### G7. Confirmatory comprehension

- Frozen study and analysis registration published or independently timestamped.
- Minimum 180 valid completions and all primary and cross-cutting quotas met.
- The three-arm instrument comparison is adequately powered and the Observatory clears its pre-registered benefit and harm bounds; otherwise the simpler passing arm is selected.
- Every quantitative gate passes overall and by group.
- Coding reliability reaches the pre-registered minimum.
- No critical incident or automatic stop remains.
- Independent analysis reviewer reproduces the result.

### G8. Dissent and affected-party disposition

- Every severe or repeated objection has an independent written disposition.
- Affected-party reviewers confirm their objection was represented accurately, without requiring agreement with the disposition.
- Unresolved limitations are prominent in the public methods and first layer where they alter meaning.
- No retaliation or unsafe participation concern remains.

### G9. Challenge, correction and incident readiness

- A person can challenge without an account or public identity.
- Every challenge receives a receipt, stable ID, owner, expected response time and escalation route.
- Correction records preserve the original, error, reach, affected decisions, repair, owner and next check.
- A tested rollback can block publication or action without waiting for a normal release cycle.
- A communication incident plan covers harmful summaries, screenshots and media reuse.

### G10. Human release decision

- Named evidence, accessibility, participant-welfare, data-governance and release owners sign their scopes.
- Indigenous governance approval is present where G4 applies.
- The release owner records the residual risks, rollback condition and next review date.
- Fernando's approval, if given, is recorded separately from agent work and cannot substitute for other required authorities.
- The public artefact links to the signed release record.

## Decision states

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `STOP` | A critical incident, automatic stop or failed gate exists. Do not publish. |
| `REVISE` | No immediate critical incident remains, but one or more required gates are incomplete or below threshold. |
| `GO, BOUNDED` | All gates pass for the named release, data, audience, languages, jurisdictions and period only. |
| `WITHDRAW` | Evidence, governance or harm cannot be repaired within the proposed scope. Archive the record and do not publish the candidate. |

A pass does not transfer to a new dataset, claim, action, jurisdiction, language, interface or materially changed visual treatment. Any such change requires an impact assessment and retesting proportionate to the changed inference.

## Required release evidence pack

Publish or retain, according to consent and governance restrictions:

1. candidate hashes and screenshots;
2. approved protocol and ethics determination;
3. participant-group counts without identifying small cells;
4. compensation and expense completion summary;
5. task script, coding guide and analysis registration;
6. aggregate results with intervals and group gaps;
7. accessibility conformance report and task results;
8. Indigenous governance evidence or recorded out-of-scope determination;
9. dissent register with protected content removed;
10. incident, remediation and retest log;
11. gate-by-gate sign-off;
12. rollback owner, condition and next review.

## Post-publication monitoring

For the first 30 days and every update cycle thereafter, monitor:

- support requests inconsistent with actual action state;
- screenshots or summaries that lose scenario, uncertainty or proposal labels;
- challenge and appeal completion times;
- repeated misunderstanding by cohort, language or access route;
- crisis, personal-risk, causation and inevitability claims attributed to the Observatory;
- correction reach compared with the original claim's reach;
- reports of stigma, retaliation, surveillance or exclusion;
- Indigenous governance concerns and unauthorised reuse;
- missed next-check dates and silent source failures.

Any automatic stop pattern changes the release state to `STOP`, invokes the communication incident plan and requires visible correction or withdrawal.
