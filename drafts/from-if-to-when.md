# From If to When

## 🦅 TL;DR

**Start with the condition that is binding today, then ask what would have to
change.** A useful transition discussion connects an observed barrier with an
owner, an action hypothesis and the next evidence. It stays open to a different
diagnosis when the facts change.

This is Ren's commissioned proposal, not Fernando's policy position; the
[evidence and authority boundaries](../boundaries.md) apply throughout.

## 🎯 Goal, signals, actions

Fernando's contribution is the frame and method. **Agency is the goal. Conditions
are the signals. Actions are hypotheses about moving them.** The public
[first piece](name-the-if.md) presents the frame. The
[supply-side piece](every-if-is-somebodys-when.md) turns it towards the people
delivering a service. This proposal asks how to examine transition options
without pretending the conditions will fall in a planned order.

Consider a specified GP consultation for a specified population and place.
List price, permission, proximity, availability and capability. Ask which
condition prevents the consultation, what evidence supports that reading and
who holds the relevant part. If the observations cannot distinguish between
two barriers, retain both questions for investigation.

**Keep favourable movement visible.** A lower cost barrier, a more usable
appointment route or an eligibility change can be progress for the people it
reaches. Record the population and period so that the improvement can be
understood alongside the people still excluded.

## 🗺 Scenarios, not a calendar

The earlier five-phase calendar has been retired. Fernando says conditions
fall progressively, sometimes unexpectedly, and the sequence cannot be
planned. The following situations are an incomplete, unscored scenario
taxonomy. They can coexist, reverse or never arise.

| Scenario | Candidate binding condition today | What would have to change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A useful service exists but people cannot navigate its route | capability; the service designer and support provider hold relevant parts | People in the affected group can find, understand and complete the route |
| A cohort loses resources while the service still carries costs | price; payers, funders and providers hold different parts | Matched observations show that the full service cost is within reach |
| A consultation becomes cheap but the next step cannot be reached | proximity or availability; the referral network holds the next step | The complete route is usable at the required time and standard |
| Access depends on a provider's continuing choice | availability, subtype discretion; identify that provider and funder | Continuity and a workable alternative are demonstrated for the named population |
| The service works but its terms cannot be challenged | permission; the decision maker and review process hold relevant parts | Affected people can use the review or alternative route and report the result |

These descriptions organise an investigation. A barrier's frequency does not
by itself establish its causal importance. The missing information
belongs in the next observation, beside the proposed diagnosis.

**Possible public responses:** people could welcome a change,
seek different terms, organise an alternative or refuse it. Those possibilities
do not make disagreement a symptom to manage. The outcome sought by the
proposal is more practical choice, including the choice to challenge it.

## 🔬 What would make an action hypothesis stronger?

One claim in this debate can be stated so it could fail: a particular change
to a payment or delivery route improves effective access for a named exposed
cohort. Specify the comparison and the reporting period before testing it.
Measure costs alongside waits, travel and successful use. Examine who gains
and who remains excluded.

Candidate mechanisms include lower costs, earnings, ownership income,
transfers and public provision. This proposal asks what a proposed
route changes, for whom, at whose cost, and what would count against its claim.
That is the evidence needed before debating a larger programme.

Historical transition analogies and organisational change models can generate
questions. Their detailed sources and limits remain in the
[precedents review](../research/2026-09-07-transition-precedents-and-adkar.md).
They supply neither a timetable nor a reason to treat today's people as an
earlier population. Test the mechanism in the present scope.

## 🔄 How an IF is allowed to move

Keep the outcome, definition and observations distinct. A changed survey
value updates evidence. A different eligibility rule updates the definition.
A new service route changes the alternatives. These changes have different
consequences for an existing action hypothesis.

The existing kernel records `definition-revised`, `narrowed`, `split` and
`merge`. Each new definition retains its predecessor and the reason for the
change. A consumer of the old definition must be examined again before its
conclusion is carried forward. **The evolution record is the argument's
memory.** It shows what was learned and which earlier interpretation expired.

The action ladder is `watch_if`, `prepare_if`, `act_if`, `pause_if`,
`reverse_if`, `recover_if` and `graduate_if`. A changed condition can make an
option eligible for consideration. The actor, funding, practical capacity and accountable decision belong beside
the option before real-world action.

## 📣 A public update worth reading

The following seven-part update makes
the evidence and open questions easy to find.

1. **What was observed?** Name the measure, source and period in a plain sentence.
2. **Who does this cover?** State the service, population and place.
3. **What is inferred?** Separate the proposed explanation from the observation.
4. **Which IF changed?** Say whether evidence, definition or an alternative route changed.
5. **What happens now?** Name the next authorised step or the evidence still needed.
6. **What would change this reading?** Give the competing explanation and its test.
7. **When is the next check?** Record the date, responsible person and next source.

For a rural clerical worker, the useful page would say which condition stands
between the relevant population and a GP appointment, who holds it, how the
evidence was obtained and how current it is. If the available sources cannot
answer for that worker, the page should say what they do cover. Precision
about the gap is part of making the page useful.

## 🧰 Options to examine

| Actor | Useful preparation question | Evidence needed before a consequential decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual or household | Which cost, travel, eligibility or booking requirement affects this route? | Relevant service information and the person's own circumstances |
| Community organisation | Which reported barriers recur, and whose experience is missing? | Voluntary accounts, consent and clear limits on representation |
| Provider | Which dependency prevents the promised service from reaching the named group? | Booking, delivery and exclusion observations for the same route |
| Public institution | Which candidate change would move the observed barrier without shifting harm elsewhere? | Distributional effects, delivery capacity and a lawful decision process |
| Cross-country collaboration | Which conditions are comparable, and which depend on local institutions? | Matched definitions, populations, periods and accountable local participation |

## 👉 The next useful test

Take one basket item through all five categories using retained public data.
Show the derivation. Ask a different reviewer to challenge the denominator,
the geography, the vintage and the proposed binding condition. Record the
strongest alternative explanation and the observation needed to distinguish it.

**A clear unresolved result is a place to work from.** The programme progresses
when a specific uncertainty is reduced, a wrong interpretation is withdrawn
or a previously hidden condition becomes visible.
