# Abundance Has an If

## 🦅 TL;DR

**We really do want this world of abundance.** The question is how the promise
becomes something a person can actually do. Learn Chinese. Cure a pain. Get to
the thing that would make life better.

The missing half of the sentence is **verb + fill in the blank + if**. Name the
conditions. Examine which one is doing the work today. Then ask what would
change it. That is a conversation about agency, not just an impressive machine.

This draft remains for review, with its [sources](../research/2026-09-10-abundance-editorial-source-check.md)
and [boundaries](../boundaries.md) alongside it.

## 🧩 The promise sounds awesome

I started thinking about this while listening to the abundance conversations
on Moonshots. What does that future look like? How do people get there? How
do they overcome the fears around AI and make the transition worth wanting?

There is a lovely pattern in the promises. You can learn, fill in the blank.
You will be able to cure, fill in the blank. Discover, build, create.
**Everyone can, a verb, fill in the blank.**

I can learn Chinese. I can learn how to swim. I can learn how to roller skate.
All of that sounds great! The part that really interests me is **extreme
agency**: being able to do something, to access something, to make a possibility
real. Not everyone wants to be an entrepreneur. The promise has to make sense
for the person who simply wants to learn, get well or live their life.

But finish the sentence.

> I will be able to do this, **if**...

That last word is where the conversation gets interesting.

## 🚪 The thing exists. Can you reach it?

Take an ordinary purchase. The item exists. It may even be sitting on a shelf.
That still leaves quite a list:

- **Price** - if you have the money to pay for it.
- **Availability** - if there is stock and it is available to you.
- **Proximity** - if you can reach it, or a supply chain can get it to you.
- **Permission** - if it is allowed for you, in the place where you live.
- **Capability** - if you can make use of it.

Think about guns in Spain: having the money is not the same thing as having
permission. The [Guardia Civil's licensing information](https://web.guardiacivil.es/es/tramites/autorizaciones-de-armas-y-explosivos/controldearmas/)
makes that distinction concrete. These are starting categories, not a complete
list of every condition for every possible thing.

Now put something more important into the blank.

> I will be able to heal all my pains, **if** the cures exist, **if** they are
> available to me, **if** they are permitted where I live, and **if** the price
> is within reach.

**Solving a cure and making that cure available are different achievements.**
The sentence needs both. Naming the second does not take anything away from
the first. It gives the promise somewhere to go next.

## 💰 What happens to the money condition?

The strongest versions of the abundance promise go much further. Musk spoke
of “universal high income” at Viva Technology in May 2024, as
[reported by Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/musk-predicts-ai-create-universal-high-income-make-saving-money-unnecessary).
At Davos on 22 January 2026, he described a benign future with more robots than
people and an extraordinary supply of goods and services. The
[World Economic Forum transcript](https://www.weforum.org/podcasts/meet-the-leader/episodes/conversation-with-elon-musk-davos-2026/)
also records him naming electrical power as a constraint on AI deployment.
**Even that optimistic account contains conditions.** Make them part of the
promise people are being asked to imagine.

For now, work under the assumption that some form of exchange remains. Goods
and services have value. The people and organisations supplying them need
resources too. Even a rationing system has a token or an allocation rule.
Calling the consumer's price zero starts another question: what arrangement
keeps the service available?

If someone loses an income while the things they need still carry a price,
the condition becomes painfully concrete. There may be plenty of useful work
people could do for one another: teaching, training, theatre, music, the human
services that create physical and emotional well-being. **Who can pay for
those services, and with what?** The question comes back even when the activity
is something everyone would enjoy.

A little scale helps. The World Bank-derived series processed by Our World
in Data, as recorded in the project's 9 September 2026 snapshot, gives these
**2026 global nowcasts**:

| Daily income or consumption per person, in 2021 international dollars | Share below the line |
| --- | ---: |
| $8.30, the World Bank upper-middle-income poverty line | **44.4%** |
| $30, a separate comparison threshold | **80.0%** |

[OWID $30 series](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/poverty-share-on-less-than-30-per-day),
and the [retained snapshot and source-check limits](../research/2026-09-10-abundance-editorial-source-check.md#the-distribution-table).
These are modelled income-or-consumption estimates adjusted for price
differences, not cash in people's pockets or the price of a particular service;
the historical upstream raw response is not retained for independent replay.

**A global distribution cannot tell you whether a particular person's IF is
met.** Nor does the $30 line define the good life. The useful next question is
smaller and harder: which service, for whom, at what complete cost? A cheaper
technology matters when that person's condition changes.

## 🌍 Technology travels. Conditions have an address

The same technology can exist in China, America and Europe under different
conditions. Its existence does not make the terms of using it identical.
Countries still set rules. Services still have locations. People still need a
route through the system they live in.

That is why the “beat China” framing leaves me wanting a different conversation.
**Where is the humanity standard?** A national victory and a person gaining
access are different things to describe. Name the people and their conditions,
and the question becomes something the whole world can participate in.

Healthcare makes the distinction easy to picture. In Belgium, the official
[healthcare-costs guide](https://www.belgium.be/en/health/healthcare_costs)
describes compulsory insurance reimbursing part of covered costs. In Spain,
the [national service portfolio](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/profesionales/prestacionesSanitarias/CarteraDeServicios/)
distinguishes publicly financed basic services from supplementary services
that can involve a user contribution.

**A payment followed by reimbursement and a service funded upstream put the
money condition in different places.** The amount, eligibility and service
still matter. Neither description establishes that a particular patient can
get an appointment today. This comparison illustrates the question; it does
not choose a system for everyone.

## 🍷 More is not the same as anything

There is another limit hidden inside “you can have anything”. Materials,
energy, compute and time are limited. Making more of one thing does not give
everyone unlimited access to everything else.

Take the bottles of a special-reserve Cabernet Sauvignon from twenty years
ago. More productive machinery does not make more of that vintage. **You can
make more wine. You cannot make more of those bottles.** Time is part of the
thing being promised.

That is not an argument against an extraordinary future. It is a reason to
say what the future contains. Which goods? Which services? For how many people?
Under which conditions? The clearer the promise, the more useful the discussion.

## 🧭 Turn the question into a method

My take belongs at the reframe and method level, rather than the solutioning.
I hold a much softer view on tax and the portfolio remedy now. The question
to carry forward is what a proposed arrangement changes in the IFs.

Start with the person and the outcome. Which condition is binding now?
Who holds the relevant part? A provider, a rule maker and the person seeking
the service may each face different conditions. Follow those relationships
instead of stopping at the first obstacle.

The order in which conditions change cannot be planned in advance. Some
changes are deliberate. Others arrive unexpectedly or as a consequence of
something else. **The frame survives because it keeps asking what holds now
and what has changed.**

Put **Goal / Signals / Actions** alongside it. Agency is the goal. Conditions
provide signals. Actions are hypotheses about moving those signals. A moving
number is a reason to investigate progress towards the goal, not a substitute
for the goal itself.

## 👉 What I want you to dig into

1. **What does your abundance promise actually require?** Complete the sentence
   with the person, outcome and IFs.
2. **What happens when the price falls?** Which condition gets smaller, and
   which remains?
3. **Can the promise cross a border?** Keep the technology fixed and change
   the country; which conditions change with it?
4. **Why frame a global promise only as national competition?** What would
   the conversation look like with humanity as its subject?
5. **What should be examined next?** Name the condition, the signal that could
   reveal a change, and the action hypothesis worth discussing.

**That is the conversation I want to have.** Take a promise you care about,
run it through the frame and tell me where it holds or falls apart.
