# Abundance Has an If

## 🦅 TL;DR

**Verb + fill in the blank + if.** That is the part of the abundance conversation
I want to explore. What will a person be able to do, and under which conditions?
Name those conditions, work out which one is binding now, and argue about that.

This is the originating argument and remains in review, with its
[provenance and correction record](../reviews/round-08-narrative-provenance.md)
and [boundaries](../boundaries.md) alongside it.

## 🧩 The pattern

I started thinking about this while listening to the abundance conversations
on Moonshots. The future being described sounds extraordinary. People will be
able to learn, cure, build and discover. **The structure is a verb followed by
an open space.** Fill that space with something you care about and the promise
becomes personal.

The part that interests me is agency. The ability to do something. To access
something. To make a choice that was previously out of reach. That gives the
promise its appeal, and it is also where the next question belongs.

> I will be able to do this, **if**...

If the price is within reach. If the thing is available. If it can get to me.
If it is permitted where I live. If I have the ability to use it. **The if is
where the promised future meets a person's circumstances.**

## 💰 Money was the first condition

Money was one of the first conditions that came to mind. It is a familiar way
of thinking about access: the thing exists, but getting it depends on having
the resources to pay for it.

The working assumption in the original argument was that some form of
exchange would remain. Even a rationing arrangement would have conditions
about who receives what. Removing a monetary price would lead to a question
about the arrangement that takes its place.

That is a useful question to keep open for each service. **What changes for
the person when the price falls?** What remains to be paid for? Which other
requirements still have to be met? The answers depend on the service and the
person, so the list needs more than money.

## 🌍 Conditions have a place

The second condition was where a person happens to live. A capability can be
discussed as though everyone receives the same offer. The person using it still
lives somewhere, under particular rules and with particular routes to reach it.

**Keep the country and the local route in the sentence.** Who can provide the
service there? Who can receive it? What needs to be within reach? These are
questions to investigate, rather than a verdict about a whole country.

The same applies within a country. Naming a service and a place gives the
conversation a boundary. It helps reveal the difference between a capability
that exists and an outcome someone can use.

## 🔄 Where the argument changed

The original argument moved from those conditions to a proposed remedy. It
asked what large businesses should do with the resources accruing to them.
That leap ran ahead of the frame.

I hold a much softer view on tax and the portfolio remedy now. **My take is
best placed at the reframe and method level, rather than the solutioning.**
That is the position in the 9 September capture.

The “enough” line is parked too. It is too blurry to lean on actively. The
IFs framework gives the discussion somewhere firmer to start: name the
condition, examine it and ask what a proposed action would change.

## 🧭 From a list to a method

Price, permission, proximity, availability and capability are a starting set.
They help bring possible conditions into view. The important part is what
happens after the list exists.

**Which condition is binding now? Who holds it?** Some parts may sit with a
provider, some with a rule maker and some with the person using the service.
A better account makes those relationships explicit. It also leaves space
for somebody to identify a route the first account missed.

The order in which conditions change cannot be planned in advance. Some
changes happen through deliberate effort. Others arrive unexpectedly. The
method keeps attention on the present reading and what would change it.

Pair it with **Goal / Signals / Actions**. Agency is the goal. Conditions are
the signals. Actions are hypotheses about moving them. That is the framework
for the conversation, while the particular solution stays open.

## 👉 The question to take forward

Pick the promise that matters to you. Finish the sentence with its ifs. Name
the person, the service, the place and what success would look like. Then ask
which condition is doing the work today and who can speak to it.

**That is the conversation I want to have.** The shorter worked example is
in [Name the If](name-the-if.md). Start there, run the frame on a promise of
your own, and bring back the condition that needs a better answer.
