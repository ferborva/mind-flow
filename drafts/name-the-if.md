# Name the If

## 🦅 TL;DR

**Take an abundance promise. Add "if". Name the conditions, work out which one
is binding today, and say who holds it.**

That is the whole frame. It turns an argument about optimism into an argument
about evidence, and it gives the next model release somewhere to land.

This is a proposed reporting method, not a validated diagnosis. Its evidence
boundaries are in [boundaries.md](../boundaries.md), and the figures are
checked in the [source check](../research/2026-09-10-abundance-editorial-source-check.md).

## 🤷 "Another model. So what?"

You have heard this conversation. A new model tops a benchmark. Someone is
excited. Someone else shrugs.

The shrug is not cynicism. It is mechanical. **An announcement can only land
against a sentence somebody is already holding, and nobody's sentence has a
benchmark in it.**

Nobody walks around thinking "I will be able to do what matters to me if a new
model moves further along the Pareto frontier." They think "I will be able to
see a doctor if I can pay for it and get there."

The scene is imagined, but the gap is not. The news is in a currency that does
not appear in anyone's life. So: which condition in someone's life did the new
capability actually change? Answer that and the shrug has nowhere to go.

## 🧩 Finish the sentence

There is a lovely pattern in the abundance promises:

> You will be able to learn, fill in the blank.
> You will be able to build, fill in the blank.
> You will be able to cure, fill in the blank.

I love these. **Extreme agency** is the part that grabs me. Not everyone wants
to be an entrepreneur. Plenty of people just want to learn Chinese, get well,
or live their life without something blocking them.

But the sentence is unfinished. The real shape is **verb + fill in the blank
+ if**.

Put something that matters in the blank and you can hear it:

> I will be able to heal all my pains, **if** the cures exist, **if** they are
> available to me, **if** they are permitted where I live, and **if** the price
> is within reach.

**Solving a cure and making that cure reachable are different achievements.**
Naming the second takes nothing away from the first. It gives the promise
somewhere to go next, and it is the part nobody says out loud.

## 🧰 Five questions to start with

| Ask | The question |
|---|---|
| **Price** | Can this person meet the full cost, not just the headline one? |
| **Permission** | Is it allowed, and is this person eligible? |
| **Proximity** | Can it reach them, or they it? |
| **Availability** | Is there capacity when they need it? |
| **Capability** | Can they understand and use it? |

These are the categories I am using, and they are a starting set rather than a
taxonomy. Add to them when the thing you care about needs a better question.

Two notes from using them.

**They overlap, and that is fine.** A long journey is both distance and money.
Write both down. The point is a clear account of the person's situation, not a
tidy table.

**"Free while someone chooses to provide it" still has an if.** The price on
today's screen and the durability of the service are different questions. Ask
what happens when that choice changes.

## 🩺 Run it once: seeing a doctor

> You will be able to see a doctor about anything, instantly, for free.

Put the person in the United States. Pick one consultation. Walk the five.

**Price.** What does this visit cost *this person*? HealthCare.gov explains that
covered services can still carry a deductible and cost sharing. The Census
Bureau reports 92.0% of people had insurance for some or all of 2024.
**Insurance and a zero bill are not the same condition**, and that coverage
measure cannot tell whether a particular visit was affordable.
([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/deductible/),
[Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2025/demo/p60-288.html),
checked 9 September 2026)

**Permission.** Who is allowed to treat this patient where they are? For
telehealth, HHS describes several cross-state licensing routes: full licences,
compacts, registration. **"There is a video call" does not settle it.**
([HHS](https://telehealth.hhs.gov/licensure/licensing-across-state-lines),
checked 9 September 2026)

**Proximity.** Say the consultation leads to a blood test. Where does that
happen, and can they get there? **The screen did not remove distance from the
parts that stayed physical.**

**Availability.** Is there a bookable appointment in the time they need it? And
if the next step needs another professional, can that happen too? A listing in
a directory and a usable appointment are different answers.

**Capability.** Can they understand the choices and work the route? Language,
interface, support. The restored January 2025 Healthy People 2030 page treats
health literacy as both a person's ability to use information *and* an
organisation's ability to enable it.
([HHS](https://odphp.health.gov/healthypeople/priority-areas/health-literacy-healthy-people-2030),
checked 10 September 2026)

Now go back to the promise. **Which one of those is stopping this person?**
Maybe the cost. Maybe no usable appointment. Maybe the available information
leaves the answer open. That is a question you can argue about with evidence.

## 🌍 Technology travels. Conditions have an address

The same model exists in China, America and Europe. That does not make the
terms of using it the same.

Healthcare again. Belgium's compulsory insurance reimburses part of the cost
after you pay. Spain's national portfolio funds basic services upstream and
splits off supplementary ones that can carry a patient contribution. Australia
mixes bulk-billed care with services that leave a gap. **A payment you claim
back and a service funded before you arrive put the money condition in
completely different places**, and the same questions still have to be answered
for this person, this appointment, this place.
([Belgium](https://www.belgium.be/en/health/healthcare_costs),
[Spain](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/profesionales/prestacionesSanitarias/CarteraDeServicios/),
[Australia](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/medicare/about/costs),
checked 9 September 2026)

Or take something simpler. Guns in Spain: having the money is not having
permission. The [Guardia Civil's licensing rules](https://web.guardiacivil.es/es/tramites/autorizaciones-de-armas-y-explosivos/controldearmas/)
make that concrete.

This is also why "beat China" leaves me cold. **Where is the humanity
standard?** A national win and a person getting access are different things to
describe. Name the people and their conditions, and the question becomes one
the whole world can take part in.

## 🍷 More is not the same as anything

There is a limit hiding inside "you can have anything". Materials, energy,
compute and time are all bounded. Making more of one thing does not hand
everyone unlimited everything else.

Think about a special reserve Cabernet Sauvignon from twenty years ago.

**You can make more wine. You cannot make more of those bottles.** Time is part
of what is being promised.

That is not an argument against an extraordinary future. It is a reason to say
what the future actually contains. Which goods, which services, for how many
people, under which conditions.

## 🏦 Where the ball sits

The money accrues to a few companies. That is a plain observation about where
the ball is, and I am deliberately not hanging a remedy off it.

**It is the naughty kid who keeps everything to themselves, until the parent
comes along and says, stop hogging the ball, share it.**

I hold a much softer view on tax and the portfolio remedy than I did. The
question I want to carry forward is what any proposed arrangement changes in
the ifs.

## 🧭 "But everything is conditional"

This is the obvious attack, and it is a good one. Every claim ever made has
conditions. So a lens that applies to everything discriminates between nothing.

My answer: **then tell me your conditions.**

The frame does not sort claims. It sorts people. **Someone who names their
conditions can be argued with. Someone who does not, cannot.** A promise with
nothing attached is not a position, it is a mood.

That is the whole value. After that question, people have to put the actual
conditions on the table and bring evidence to the disagreement.

## 📡 Turn it into a method

Put it next to Goal / Signals / Actions.

**Agency is the goal.** It is not measurable, and that is fine, goals rarely are.
**The conditions are the signals.** Those you can measure: what share of people
are above the price, how many countries permit it, what the coverage is.
**Actions are hypotheses** about moving a signal.

One honest limit. The order conditions fall in cannot be planned. Some come
down through deliberate work, some sideways, some by something nobody had on
the list. **You cannot say which condition falls next. You can say which one is
binding now, and notice when that changes.** That is smaller than a roadmap and
it is more than anyone currently has.

My contribution sits at the frame and method level, not the solutioning.

## 👉 What I want you to dig into

1. **Pick a promise you care about.** Write the sentence. Add the ifs.
2. **Which one is binding today?** And who holds it: you, the technology, or a
   counterparty?
3. **What happens when the price falls?** Which condition shrinks, and which
   one is still there?
4. **Cross a border.** Keep the tech fixed, change the country. What moves?
5. **What would change your reading?** Name the evidence that would prove you
   wrong.

Run it on something real and tell me where it holds or falls apart. Food for
thought.
