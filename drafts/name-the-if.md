# Name the If

## 🦅 TL;DR

**Take any promise about the abundant future. Add the word "if". Then name what
has to be true, work out which one is actually stopping people today, and say
who can fix it.**

That is the whole idea. It turns "are you optimistic or not" into "what exactly
is in the way". And it gives the next big AI announcement somewhere to land.

This is a proposed reporting method: a way of asking questions, not a proven
answer. What it cannot do is in [boundaries.md](../boundaries.md), and the
figures are checked in the
[source check](../research/2026-09-10-abundance-editorial-source-check.md).

## 🤷 "Another model. So what?"

You have heard this one. A new model tops a benchmark. Someone is excited.
Someone else shrugs.

The shrug is not cynicism. There is a reason for it. **News only lands if it
answers a question you were already asking, and nobody is walking around asking
about benchmarks.**

Nobody thinks "I will be able to do what matters to me if a new model moves
further along the Pareto frontier." They think "I will be able to see a doctor
if I can pay for it and get there."

The scene is made up. The gap is real. So ask a better question: **what did this
actually change for someone?** Answer that and the shrug has nowhere to go.

## 🧩 Finish the sentence

There is a pattern in these promises:

> You will be able to learn, fill in the blank.
> You will be able to build, fill in the blank.
> You will be able to cure, fill in the blank.

I love these. **Extreme agency** is the part that grabs me. Not everyone wants to
be an entrepreneur. Plenty of people just want to learn Chinese, get well, or
get on with their life without something in the way.

But the sentence stops too early. The real shape is **verb + fill in the blank
+ if**.

Put something that matters in the blank and you can hear it:

> I will be able to heal all my pains, **if** the cures exist, **if** they are
> available to me, **if** they are permitted where I live, and **if** the price
> is within reach.

**Finding a cure and getting that cure to a person are two different jobs.**
Saying so takes nothing away from the first one. It just tells you what is left.

## 🧰 Five questions to start with

| Ask | The question |
|---|---|
| **Price** | Can they afford it? The whole thing, not the sticker price. |
| **Permission** | Is it allowed where they are, and are they allowed? |
| **Proximity** | Can they get to it, or it to them? |
| **Availability** | Is there any left when they need it? |
| **Capability** | Can they understand it and use it? |

These are the categories I am using, and they are a starting set rather than a
finished list. Add your own when the thing you care about needs a better
question.

Two things I have learned using them.

**They overlap, and that is fine.** A long trip to a clinic is both distance and
money. Write both down. You want a clear picture of the person's situation, not
a tidy table.

**"Free while someone chooses to give it away" still has an if.** What it costs
you today and whether it is still there next year are different questions.

## 🩺 Run it once: seeing a doctor

> You will be able to see a doctor about anything, instantly, for free.

Put the person in the United States. Pick one appointment. Walk the five.

**Price.** What does this visit cost *this person*? In the US you can have
insurance and still pay an excess, plus a share of the bill. The Census Bureau
says 92.0% of people had insurance for some or all of 2024. **Having insurance
and paying nothing are not the same thing**, and that number cannot tell whether
a particular visit was affordable.
([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/deductible/),
[Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2025/demo/p60-288.html),
checked 9 September 2026)

**Permission.** Is this doctor allowed to treat this patient, where the patient
is standing? US health authorities describe several routes for treating someone
in another state. **"There is a video call" does not settle it.**
([HHS](https://telehealth.hhs.gov/licensure/licensing-across-state-lines),
checked 9 September 2026)

**Proximity.** Say the appointment leads to a blood test. Where does that
happen, and can they get there? **A screen does not move the blood test any
closer.**

**Availability.** Is there an appointment they can actually book, in the time
they need it? And if the next step needs another specialist, can that happen
too? Being listed in a directory and having a bookable slot are different
things.

**Capability.** Can they follow what they are being told, and work the system?
Language, an interface that makes sense, someone to help. US health guidance
treats this as two things at once: whether the person can understand the
information, and whether the service makes that easy.
([HHS](https://odphp.health.gov/healthypeople/priority-areas/health-literacy-healthy-people-2030),
checked 10 September 2026)

Now go back to the promise. **Which one of those five is actually stopping this
person?** Maybe the cost. Maybe there is no appointment. Maybe nobody knows.
That is a question you can argue about with evidence.

## 🌍 Technology travels. Conditions have an address

The same model exists in China, America and Europe. That does not make using it
the same in each place.

Healthcare again. **In Belgium you pay, then claim part of it back. In Spain the
basic services are paid for before you walk in, and some extras you chip in
for. In Australia some visits cost you nothing and some leave you a bill.**
Same care, three different answers to "can you afford it".
([Belgium](https://www.belgium.be/en/health/healthcare_costs),
[Spain](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/profesionales/prestacionesSanitarias/CarteraDeServicios/),
[Australia](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/medicare/about/costs),
checked 9 September 2026)

Or something simpler. Guns in Spain: having the money is not having permission.
The [Guardia Civil's rules](https://web.guardiacivil.es/es/tramites/autorizaciones-de-armas-y-explosivos/controldearmas/)
make that concrete.

This is also why "beat China" leaves me cold. **Where is the humanity standard?**
A country winning and a person getting care are different things. Name the
people and what is in their way, and the whole world can join in.

## 🍷 More is not the same as anything

There is a limit hiding inside "you can have anything". Materials, energy,
compute and time all run out. Making more of one thing does not hand everyone
unlimited everything else.

Think about a special reserve Cabernet Sauvignon from twenty years ago.

**You can make more wine. You cannot make more of those bottles.** Time is part
of what is being promised.

That is not an argument against an amazing future. It is a reason to say what
the future actually contains. Which things, for which people, under what
conditions.

## 🏦 Where the ball sits

The money accrues to a few companies. That is just a plain observation about
where the ball is, and I am not hanging a solution off it.

**It is the naughty kid who keeps everything to themselves, until the parent
comes along and says, stop hogging the ball, share it.**

I hold a much softer view on tax and the portfolio remedy than I did. The
question I want to carry forward is simpler: what would any proposed fix
actually change in the ifs?

## 🧭 "But everything is conditional"

This is the obvious attack, and it is a good one. Every claim ever made has
conditions. So a test that applies to everything tells you nothing.

My answer: **then tell me your conditions.**

The frame does not sort claims. It sorts people. **Someone who names their
conditions can be argued with. Someone who will not, cannot.** A promise with
nothing attached is not a position, it is a mood.

That is the whole value. After that question, people have to put the real
conditions on the table and bring evidence.

## 📡 Turn it into a method

Put it next to **Goal, Signals, Actions.** One thing you actually want. A few
numbers that tell you whether you are getting closer. Things you try, to move
those numbers.

**The goal is agency**: people able to do the thing. You cannot measure that
directly, and that is fine, most goals are like that. **The conditions are the
signals**, and those you can count: how many people can afford it, how many
countries allow it, how many can get to it. **Every action is a bet** that you
can move one of those numbers.

One honest limit. **You cannot plan the order these fall in.** Some come down
because someone worked at it. Some fall sideways. Some go because of something
nobody had on the list. So you cannot say which one goes next. You can say which
one is stopping people now, and notice when that changes. That is smaller than a
roadmap, and it is more than anyone currently has.

My take is best placed at the reframe and method level, not the solutioning.

## 👉 What I want you to dig into

1. **Pick a promise you care about.** Write the sentence. Add the ifs.
2. **Which one is actually stopping people today?** And who can fix it: you, the
   technology, or someone else you depend on?
3. **What happens when the price falls?** Which condition gets smaller, and
   which one is still sitting there?
4. **Cross a border.** Same technology, different country. What changes?
5. **What would prove you wrong?** Name it.

Run it on something real and tell me where it holds up or falls apart. Food for
thought.
