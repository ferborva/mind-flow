## 2. The email

> **Subject:** The half of the abundance sentence we never finish
>
> Hi Peter, Dave and the Moonshots crew,
>
> Long-time listener, and the abundance thread is the one I keep coming back to.
> I run engineering at Atlassian, I am about as pro-technology as they come, and
> I want the world you are describing. This is not a note from a sceptic.
>
> It is a note about one missing word.
>
> Every abundance promise has the same shape: **verb + fill in the blank.** You
> will be able to learn anything. Cure anything. Build anything. Listen for it
> and you cannot stop hearing it.
>
> The sentence is not finished. The real structure is **verb + fill in the blank
> + if.** If you can pay for it. If it is legal where you live. If it can
> physically reach you. If you have the time and the foundation to use it.
>
> **That missing word is not pessimism, it is the part you can actually work
> on.** A promise with no conditions attached cannot be checked, planned around,
> or disagreed with, which I think is why the conversation loops back to the
> same place every few episodes. Name the conditions and you get something
> better than a destination: you get a way to read the news. Every model
> release, every chip rule, every national regulation becomes answerable as
> "which condition did that just lower, for whom, and which one is binding now".
>
> Sometimes the honest answer is "none yet, and that is fine". Which is, I
> think, exactly what your kids mean, Peter, when they say *it's just another
> model, so what*. They are not being cynical. **Nobody is walking around with
> the sentence "I will be able to do X if a model tops the benchmarks by y%",
> so the announcement has nothing to land in.** The sentences people do carry
> are about a gap fee, a licence, a queue, eight hours a week they do not have.
> None of those ifs say "benchmark".
>
> I have written it up properly here: [link]. It comes with a worked example
> (the "see a doctor about anything, instantly, free" promise, run across four
> countries) and a starting set of condition categories, offered as a tool for
> you to use on air rather than an argument for you to agree with.
>
> If it is useful, run it on your own promises and tell me where it breaks. If
> it is not, I will happily keep listening 😄
>
> Thanks for the show. It has genuinely changed how I think about the next
> decade.
>
> Fer

## Notes on the iteration he asked for

The next version gets **specific callbacks to things they have actually said on
air**, so it reads as a reply to their conversation rather than a cold pitch.

land, the pass is:

- quote the promise verbatim, then run the frame on it
- pick one moment where the frame would have unstuck a discussion they had

  precise reference to an episode does more than three compliments.
