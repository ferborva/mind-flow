# Round 04 external review brief

> **Pre-freeze agent proposal. Not a public release or approval request.** The
> review target is branch `ren/abundance-transition-program`. The coordinating
> reviewer must record the exact commit before dispatch. Review success cannot
> establish empirical truth, approve recruitment, create legal authority or
> adopt agent-authored values as Fernando's views.

## The claim under review

The strongest claim is methodological:

> **Technical capability does not become human agency automatically. For a
> named population, outcome, standard, place and period, a candidate route
> depends on observable conditions. Those conditions can be named, challenged,
> monitored and revised before consequential action.**

Round 04 does not claim that abundance is coming, that the complete condition
set is known, that the current synthetic IF state is empirically true, or that
any actor may act. It asks whether the repository can preserve those boundaries
while joining one condition across eight independently validated artifacts and
rendering it for public inspection.

preserving the failing state.

## What is joined

The synthetic Round 04 pre-projection core joins these exact artifact roles:

1. executable IF kernel;
2. condition evolution ledger;
3. signal registry;
4. condition agency map;
5. possible path;
6. preparation register; and
7. forecast.

The complete core adds one executable dashboard projection. All eight resolve
`condition.worker-option.nsw`. The complete-core builder requires every
non-dashboard byte reference, canonical reference, scope binding, clock and
boundary to remain identical to the pre-projection core.

The mechanical rule computes `true` at the supplied untrusted clock. That means
only that the registered fixture observations satisfy the registered fixture
predicates. Empirical truth, scope-mapping truth, causality, clock trust,
authority, action and publication remain unestablished.

The visual Observatory and proposed 2 by 2 experiment derive from the same
pre-projection core. The interface is a prototype. The experiment binds
declared inputs only, cannot approve recruitment and has not yet assessed
rendered parity.

## Independent review lanes

Each reviewer submits a sealed first pass before seeing other reviewers. A
coordinator reveals the reports together, records disagreement and assigns
dispositions. Reviewers should attack adjacent claims, not only their named
lane.

### 1. Thesis and evidence

Test whether each public sentence stays below its cited source ceiling. Look
for inevitability, exposure presented as outcome, coverage presented as access,
projection presented as observation, analogy presented as validation, and a
candidate condition presented as a diagnosis.

Start with the two main drafts, the crisis-point register and the Round 04
claim-evidence audit. For every disputed claim, provide the strongest available
primary evidence, a rival interpretation and wording that would survive both.

### 2. Semantic and integrity

Attempt identity substitution, resealed bytes, condition split or merge drift,
scope drift, cropped history, stale evidence-tip reuse, copied evaluation
receipts, probability-to-state laundering, and dashboard derivation cycles.

Distinguish content integrity from publisher authentication and real-world
truth. A checksum proves neither. Determine whether every displayed claim can
be regenerated from retained bytes by a fixed validator rather than trusted
copy.

### 3. Forecasting and causal inference

Test event resolvability, issue-time information, baseline independence,
dependency clusters, proper scoring, revision handling, rolling-origin design
and the difference between current IF state and future probability.

Challenge every causal arrow with a credible rival graph. The current synthetic
forecast is a contract fixture, not evidence of skill. No calibration claim is
eligible without an adequate resolved sample and uncertainty.

### 4. Affected-party and rights

Test who defined the outcome, standard, loss rule and acceptable preparation.
Identify omitted populations, coercion, refusal, retaliation, privacy,
accessibility, cultural safety, appeal and remedy failures.

External agent review cannot substitute for affected-party review. Name the
people and institutions who must be able to challenge, revise or reject each
condition before any live use.

### 5. Public comprehension and accessibility

Compare the Observatory with the simplest adequate statistical release. Test
whether readers can reconstruct WHO, VERB, OUTCOME, STANDARD, PLACE, PERIOD,
the candidate IF set, evidence class, uncertainty, forecast, authority and next
check without help.

Look for visual authority, threat priming, false reassurance, dense provenance,
colour dependence, keyboard traps, screen-reader ambiguity, zoom failures,
low-bandwidth failure and translation loss. Rendered parity between experiment
arms has not been established and blocks recruitment.

### 6. Security, governance and operations

Test path traversal, symlink substitution, oversized input, denial of service,
untrusted time, compromised dependencies, unauthenticated publishers,
bypassable validators and release outside governed build paths.

Then test the human system: owner identity, lawful authority, consent, funding,
capacity, service level, last-mile reach, override, support, appeal, recovery
and exit. Repository validity cannot create any of them.

## Highest-value attacks

1. Make all local hashes agree while changing the public meaning.
2. Preserve one condition ID while changing its population, period or standard.
3. Make a forecast probability appear to validate a current condition.
4. Keep aggregate outcomes favourable while producing severe subgroup harm.
5. Make `unknown`, `stale` or `conflicted` appear safe.
6. Turn reversible preparation into behavioural pressure or implied policy.
7. Hide an omitted condition behind a complete-looking graph.
8. Make a beautiful interface increase confidence without comprehension.
9. Make the experiment favour the Observatory through salience, time or help.
10. Bypass every validator and publish a plausible artifact directly.

## Finding format

Every material finding should include:

- severity: `P0`, `P1`, `P2` or `P3`;
- exact artifact, path and line or JSON pointer;
- claim or gate affected;
- minimal reproduction;
- concrete harm or false inference;
- smallest safe repair;
- neighbouring attack after repair;
- falsifier or evidence that would close the finding; and
- disposition: accept, narrow, reject, defer or duplicate.

Absence of a discovered bug is not evidence of correctness. Agreement among
agents is not independence and does not close a finding.

## Known blockers that review must preserve

- All current evidence and observations in the Round 04 joined core are
  synthetic research fixtures.
- The operator-supplied clock is untrusted.
- Scope mappings have structural integrity, but mapping truth is not assessed.
- The registered condition set may be incomplete and is not causally validated.
- No publisher, reviewer or actor identity is externally authenticated.
- No authority, consent, funding, delivery capacity or publication approval is
  established.
- The Observatory has no independent comprehension, screen-reader, security or
  affected-party result.
- The experiment has no ethics or privacy approval, validated outcome, power
  plan, assignment implementation, rendered parity or independent monitor.
- The repository has advisory validators, not an enforced issuance boundary.

Any repair that makes one of these blockers disappear without external evidence
is a regression.

## Exit from external review

Round 04 exits review only when every report is preserved, material findings
have explicit dispositions, accepted repairs survive neighbouring attacks and
the exact repaired commit reproduces cleanly. The result may be a narrower
method, a retired feature or a blocked programme. It cannot by itself authorise
live data claims, participant recruitment, warnings or action.
