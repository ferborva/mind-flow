# Public Charter for the Abundance Transition Observatory

This is Ren's commissioned governance proposal, with [evidence and authority
boundaries](../boundaries.md), not Fernando's adopted policy.

## 🦅 TL;DR

- The Observatory exists to test when technological capability becomes shared,
  durable human agency, and to help people act before an important condition
  fails.
- It does not predict one inevitable future, optimise society from above, or
  treat disagreement as a defect.
- Every public statement must identify what kind of claim it is, who authored
  it, what evidence supports it, what remains uncertain, and what could change
  it.
- Every proposed trigger must identify its owner, authority, funding,
  safeguards, appeal route, review date and exit test.
- This charter proposes that affected people be able to participate in
  decisions. No such process exists yet.

---

## 🎯 Public purpose

The Observatory starts with a simple problem:

> **A technology being capable of doing something does not mean a person can
> benefit from it, choose it freely, or rely on it.**

The programme follows **Goal / Signals / Actions**: agency is the goal and
remains unmeasured; conditions supply the signals; actions are hypotheses about
moving them. Price, permission, proximity, availability and capability are the
starting prompts, with revocable discretion under availability.

Its public purpose is to:

1. Make the conditions between capability and lived agency visible.
2. Show which conditions are observed, uncertain, emerging or binding for a
   named service, cohort, place and time.
3. Distinguish observed evidence from hypotheses, forecasts, value choices and
   proposals.
4. Let affected people challenge the model, evidence and proposed response.
5. Help legitimate institutions prepare reversible, proportionate actions
   before harm compounds.
6. Track gains as seriously as risks, including health, learning, security,
   discretionary time, choice, creativity and the ability to exit bad systems.
7. Keep a permanent record of predictions, decisions, corrections and misses so
   the project can learn in public.

The proposed north star is:

> **Map the conditions under which growing capability becomes shared human
> agency, then make the evidence and choices legible enough for people to shape
> the crossing together.**

## 🚫 Non-goals

The Observatory is not:

- a claim that abundance is inevitable;
- a claim that technology is the main cause of every economic or social change;
- an oracle, a central planner, or a system for optimising people;
- a substitute for democratic institutions, collective bargaining, community
  knowledge or expert judgement;
- a system for predicting, targeting or suppressing political movements;
- a single score that decides whether a country or population is succeeding;
- a justification for surveillance of workers, communities or dissenters;
- a tool for shifting systemic responsibility onto households;
- a promise that everything can or should become free;
- a definition of the good life imposed on every culture or person;
- a licence to turn a provisional agent proposal into Fernando's opinion.

## 👥 Who it serves

The Observatory should be usable by people experiencing the transition, not
only by people governing it. Its audiences include:

| Public | What the Observatory owes them |
|---|---|
| People directly harmed | Immediate protection information, an accountable owner, an appeal route, and recovery measures |
| Exposed people and households | Uncertainty, options and trigger rules without panic or blame |
| Essential workers | Recognition of bargaining power, service constraints, workload, autonomy and safety |
| Communities, unions and civil society | Cohort and place data, challenge rights and meaningful participation before decisions |
| Businesses and service providers | Stable definitions, auditable obligations and a fair route to contest findings |
| Governments and delivery institutions | Decision-ready evidence, stress tests and explicit limits on delegated authority |
| Citizens and rights-holders | Alternatives, trade-offs, costs, beneficiaries, harms and decision rights |
| International and excluded groups | Comparable rules without erasing local choice, language, identity or jurisdiction |

**The same underlying evidence should be available to everyone.** Assistance and
actions can differ by situation. The truth must not.

## 🔎 The public evidence contract

Every consequential statement must carry two independent labels:

1. **Provenance:** where the statement came from and who stands behind it.
2. **Epistemic class:** what kind of knowledge claim it makes.

### Provenance classes

| Label | Meaning | Public requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Fernando, captured** | Traceable to Fernando's recorded words | Link the capture and retain context |
| **Fernando, endorsed** | Fernando explicitly reviewed and adopted a later formulation | Link the endorsement and date |
| **External evidence** | A third-party fact, dataset or published finding | Name the source, method, retrieval date and material limits |
| **Ren or agent proposal** | Agent-authored synthesis, hypothesis, design or advice | Name the agent and display `Requires Fernando approval` where relevant |
| **Participant evidence** | Lived experience or community-supplied evidence | State consent, collection method, scope and protection against re-identification |
| **Institutional commitment** | A named body has accepted an action or obligation | Name its authority, accountable owner, date and review mechanism |

Authorship is not confidence. A claim can be Fernando's view and still be
uncertain. A high-quality external fact does not become Fernando's view merely
because it supports an essay.

### Epistemic classes

| Label | Meaning | Minimum disclosure |
|---|---|---|
| **Observed** | Directly reported measurement | Unit, population, place, period, source, vintage and missingness |
| **Derived** | Calculation from observations | Formula, inputs, transformations and uncertainty propagation |
| **Hypothesis** | Testable explanation or anticipated relationship | Mechanism, alternatives, falsifier and evidence grade |
| **Forecast** | Probabilistic claim about a future observation | Horizon, probability, reference class or model, interval and scoring rule |
| **Scenario** | Conditional illustration, not assigned a probability | Assumptions and the statement `This is not a forecast` |
| **Value choice** | A judgement about what should matter | Decision-maker, affected parties, alternatives and dissent |
| **Proposal** | An intervention not yet authorised | Owner, cost, evidence, risks, safeguards and approval state |
| **Commitment** | An authorised action that should occur under stated conditions | Trigger, authority, funding, service level, appeal and exit test |
| **Unknown** | Important condition without adequate evidence | Why it matters and the plan to learn, if one exists |
| **Contested** | Evidence or values remain in material dispute | Strongest competing positions and what could resolve or narrow the dispute |

No visual treatment may make a scenario look like a forecast, instrumentation
coverage look like risk, or a proposal look like a commitment.

## 🧭 Public principles

### 1. Capability is not access, and access is not agency

Capability asks whether something can work. Access asks whether a person can
reach and use it. Agency additionally asks whether the person can understand,
choose, refuse, switch, protect their rights and appeal.

### 2. The unit of analysis is explicit

Every headline must name the service or outcome, cohort, place, period and
distribution. A national average cannot speak for a harmed town or occupation.

### 3. Conditions are plural and dynamic

Affordability may stop binding while permission, delivery, safety, language,
privacy or monopoly dependence starts binding. The project tracks that change
rather than declaring that an `if` disappeared.

### 4. Uncertainty is part of the result

Unknowns, revisions, intervals, disagreement and missing instruments appear in
the primary view. They are not footnotes added after a confident headline.

### 5. Prepare before predicting

When prediction is weak but potential harm is serious, favour no-regret,
reversible preparation over false precision. Do not invent a probability to
make a decision look scientific.

### 6. No warning without a response path

A warning must say who can act, under what authority, with what resources, and
how an affected person can obtain help or appeal. An unowned warning is not a
control system.

### 7. No intervention without a harm model

Every proposed action names plausible side effects, who may carry them, how
they will be monitored, and the condition that pauses or ends the action.

### 8. Gains and losses receive equal scrutiny

The project publishes green signals, red signals, failed pilots, successful
adaptation and cases that contradict its thesis. Its purpose is learning and
agency, not proving that a crisis narrative was right.

## 🗳 Anti-technocracy safeguards

The Observatory can inform a decision. It cannot manufacture legitimacy for
one. Public implementations should include:

- **Named decision rights.** The page states which institution may decide,
  which people must be consulted, and which decisions require fresh democratic
  authority.
- **Affected-party participation.** People carrying a material risk join the
  definition of the condition, threshold, safeguard and recovery test before
  activation wherever time permits.
- **Plural options.** The system presents credible alternatives and their
  trade-offs, not one optimised recommendation disguised as evidence.
- **Reversible first responses.** Automatic action is limited to actions that
  were lawfully delegated, funded, tested, proportionate and reversible.
- **Appeal and remedy.** Any person denied access or harmed by a triggered action
  has a reachable human review, a decision deadline and a remedy path.
- **Override transparency.** Activating, delaying, overriding or ending a
  trigger creates a public record of who decided, why and with what evidence.
- **Sunsets.** Emergency measures and exceptional data access expire unless
  renewed through their stated authority.
- **Data minimisation.** Measure the condition with the least personal data
  reasonably required. Never repurpose transition data for policing,
  immigration enforcement, advertising or worker discipline.
- **Independent challenge.** Fund audit and counter-analysis, including from
  communities and researchers who reject the project's framing.
- **No personalised persuasion.** Do not use vulnerability, political identity
  or behavioural profiling to target messages designed to obtain consent.

## ✊ Dissent is a signal of agency

Protest, unionisation, refusal, migration, litigation, demands for public
ownership and rejection of abundance language are not automatically crises.
They can be legitimate responses to harm, exclusion or disagreement over the
goal itself.

The project may forecast institutional stress or conditions that historically
produce conflict. It must not assign people to predicted movements, label
political opposition as pathology, or optimise communications to neutralise
resistance.

Every contested public page should provide:

1. The strongest counterargument to its framing.
2. A route to submit contrary evidence or lived experience.
3. A public status for challenges: received, assessing, accepted, rejected, or
   unresolved.
4. A dated explanation when a challenge changes the model.
5. Protection against retaliation for participating or dissenting.

## 🔐 Red lines

The Observatory must not publish or operate:

- secret risk scores about people or communities;
- a composite social score presented as objective truth;
- a forecast without a horizon and later scoring;
- a trigger without an accountable owner and lawful authority;
- an automatic coercive response;
- a public claim without provenance and epistemic class;
- a commissioned proposal in Fernando's voice without a visible warning;
- a current-state verdict derived only from a global or national average;
- a success claim that omits quality, rights, hidden payment and durability;
- surveillance of political beliefs, organising or dissent.

## ✅ Public release gates

Before a consequential dashboard, forecast or action compact is described as
public-ready, it should pass these checks:

- Readers can distinguish capability, access and agency.
- Readers do not mistake measurement coverage for probability or progress.
- The service, cohort, place, period, unit, source, vintage and uncertainty are
  visible from the claim.
- Observations, hypotheses, forecasts, scenarios, values and proposals are
  visually distinct.
- Affected participants have reviewed the framing and can publish dissent.
- Every action has an owner, authority, funding source, safeguard, appeal,
  review date and exit test.
- The strongest counterargument and disconfirming evidence are present.
- Plain-language, translation, low-bandwidth, numeracy, accessibility and
  privacy reviews have passed.
- Forecasts and model changes enter a permanent versioned record.

## 📈 What success means

Success is not a more impressive dashboard or a forecast that attracts
attention. It is:

- more people achieving essential outcomes reliably;
- narrower unjustified gaps between cohorts and places;
- greater freedom to choose, refuse, switch and appeal;
- more discretionary time, security, health, learning and creative capacity;
- preparation reaching people before avoidable harm compounds;
- institutions correcting errors quickly and visibly;
- decision power remaining contestable;
- public understanding and calibrated trust improving;
- the project changing or abandoning claims when the evidence turns.
