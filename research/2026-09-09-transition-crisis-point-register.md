# Transition crisis-point register

> **Agent proposal. Not a forecast, warning or policy.** Ren drafted this
> register after Fernando asked the project to help people, communities,
> institutions and countries act early through an abundance transition. The
> candidate pathways, priorities, signals and responses require domain,
> affected-party and lawful institutional review. None is a current finding.

## Decision

**Do not monitor whether AI is powerful. Monitor whether capability is becoming
shared, durable agency, and where that conversion could break.**

The Observatory should organise the transition around this open chain:

```text
technical capability
→ practical adoption
→ changed tasks, services and production
→ changed price, time, income, access and power
→ expanded or reduced choices for named people
→ durable outcomes under stress
```

Every arrow is an IF. None is automatic. Each arrow needs its own population,
place, period, measure, rival explanation, harm test and actor-specific WHEN.
The chain can progress, stall, reverse, fork or bypass a registered route.

The project's job is not to tell the public where history is going. Its job is
to make the important conversion conditions visible early enough for
legitimate actors to prepare, negotiate and learn before avoidable harms become
locked in.

## What the evidence allows us to say now

Current authoritative sources support the need for conditional monitoring.
They do not establish an abundance transition or crisis.

source's own reference period and should be rechecked before reuse.

The common lesson is narrower than “act now.” A capability signal, exposure
index, adoption rate, energy forecast, incident count or dashboard state cannot
stand in for the outcome. The chain between them is the research object.

## The crisis point is a failed conversion

A crisis point is not a dramatic number. It is a condition under which a
valuable conversion may fail while harm, lock-in or response delay is growing.

Use this public form:

> **[NAMED PEOPLE] may lose or gain [SCOPED CHOICE OR OUTCOME], in [PLACE],
> during [PERIOD], if [CANDIDATE CONDITION] changes through [PROPOSED
> MECHANISM]. We would look for [LEADING], [CONFIRMING], [COUNTER], [OUTCOME]
> and [HARM] evidence. [NAMED ACTOR] could prepare by [REVERSIBLE STEP] when
> [PREPARATION CONDITION] passes. No action is authorised unless [AUTHORITY,
> RIGHTS, FUNDING, CAPACITY AND SAFEGUARDS] pass.**

The same condition can support several possible paths. The path register must
include a beneficial route, an adverse route, no material change, measurement
error, refusal or reduction, and credible domain-specific alternatives. These
are hypotheses to discriminate, not equally likely futures.

## Candidate crisis-point map

### CP1: Capability without useful adoption

**Question:** Can a technically capable system solve the scoped task under
real operating conditions, or does benchmark progress fail at workflow,
reliability, liability, cost, integration, trust or service quality?

- **Possible beneficial path:** validated capability is adapted into a useful,
  safe and accessible service.
- **Possible adverse path:** investment and institutional attention move before
  real-world quality, making people carry errors or unfinished work.
- **Rival accounts:** measurement improvement, marketing, task selection,
  complementary investment, regulation, infrastructure or no material change.
- **Leading evidence:** bounded deployment attempts and task-level capability
  tests with declared populations and environments.
- **Confirming evidence:** independently measured workflow, quality, time and
  service outcomes.
- **Counterevidence:** withdrawal, manual rework, exception rates, hidden human
  labour, failed integration and non-adoption.
- **Harm evidence:** safety incidents, rights failures, exclusion, work
  intensification, environmental burden and information harm.
- **Actor WHEN:** a provider could prepare a controlled evaluation when the
  task, cohort, failure mode, rollback and independent measurement are defined.
- **Do not cross:** deployment pressure cannot convert exposure or a benchmark
  into a claim of useful adoption.

### CP2: Adoption without worker or user agency

**Question:** Does deployment expand what affected people can do and choose, or
does it transfer discretion, surveillance, risk or uncompensated work to them?

- **Possible beneficial path:** people gain capability, time, voice, safer work
  or a genuinely rejectable alternative.
- **Possible adverse path:** output rises while autonomy, privacy, job quality,
  bargaining power, appeal or access falls.
- **Rival accounts:** broader management change, demand, regulation, labour
  scarcity, composition effects or selection into adopting organisations.
- **Leading evidence:** independently verified deployment exposure at task and
  person level, including who chose the system and who may refuse it.
- **Confirming evidence:** time use, work intensity, control, earnings,
  accessibility, service quality and appeal outcomes for the same population.
- **Counterevidence:** shadow work, overrides, turnover, absence, complaints,
  contested consent and divergence between output and experienced benefit.
- **Harm evidence:** retaliation, coercion, bias, unsafe pace, deskilling,
  monitoring and concentrated losses.
- **Actor WHEN:** an employer, service owner, union or regulator could negotiate
  a bounded redesign when affected people can participate without penalty and
  the no-deployment path remains legitimate.
- **Do not cross:** aggregate output cannot compensate for a severe,
  non-compensable loss carried by one group.

### CP3: Productivity without broad material transmission

**Question:** If productivity or production rises, do price, income, time,
access and public capacity improve for the named population?

- **Possible beneficial path:** lower resource cost and complementary demand
  support higher real income, time or service access.
- **Possible adverse path:** rents, concentration, transition costs or weak
  demand retain the gain while income or bargaining power falls elsewhere.
- **Rival accounts:** business cycle, monetary conditions, sector composition,
  trade, fiscal policy, market power, measurement and lagged investment.
- **Leading evidence:** source-linked changes in unit cost, output per hour,
  mark-ups, labour share, hours and vacancy composition.
- **Confirming evidence:** cohort-specific real disposable resources, time,
  essential-service access and distributional outcomes.
- **Counterevidence:** falling prices with falling incomes, higher measured
  output with longer hours, or aggregate gains with subgroup deterioration.
- **Harm evidence:** involuntary loss, debt distress, unmet need, displacement,
  geographic concentration and weakened essential-service capacity.
- **Actor WHEN:** fiscal, competition, labour, service and enterprise actors
  could prepare separately when their scoped transmission condition is
  evidenced and their authority, funding and review route are identified.
- **Do not cross:** GDP, profit, productivity and poverty-threshold statistics
  cannot be combined across units as a personal abundance verdict.

### CP4: Cheap or available services without effective access

**Question:** Can the named person actually obtain a suitable service in time,
under the full price, eligibility, proximity, availability and capability
conditions that apply to them?

- **Possible beneficial path:** new delivery capacity removes a genuine access
  barrier while quality, rights and alternatives hold.
- **Possible adverse path:** nominal supply or a zero listed price hides waits,
  gap costs, exclusions, inaccessible channels or degraded quality.
- **Rival accounts:** workforce capacity, local distribution, administration,
  eligibility, trust, language, disability access, transport and digital
  exclusion.
- **Leading evidence:** service-specific availability, price, wait, eligibility
  and geographic coverage with exact denominators.
- **Confirming evidence:** completed access and experienced outcome for the
  intended population.
- **Counterevidence:** unmet need, abandonment, repeat attempts, appeals,
  informal workarounds and subgroup gaps.
- **Harm evidence:** denial, delayed care, unsafe substitution, privacy loss,
  stigma, cost shifting and loss of non-digital routes.
- **Actor WHEN:** a service institution could test one candidate barrier when
  all viable registered routes, omission groups and a human appeal path are
  visible.
- **Do not cross:** a service, appointment, county or business count cannot be
  described as people receiving the outcome.

### CP5: Fast private deployment without institutional response capacity

**Question:** Can law, public administration, worker representation, service
delivery, audit, incident response and remedy adapt before harms or dependency
become difficult to reverse?

- **Possible beneficial path:** clear duties, interoperable records, tested
  services and rapid correction allow useful adaptation.
- **Possible adverse path:** institutions inherit vendor timelines, weak
  evidence, brittle systems or responsibilities without funds and staff.
- **Rival accounts:** ordinary procurement failure, legacy systems, fiscal
  constraints, fragmented jurisdiction, regulatory delay or overreaction.
- **Leading evidence:** implementation timelines, incident and complaint flow,
  audit coverage, staffing, funding, dependency and appeal capacity.
- **Confirming evidence:** response time, correction, remedy, service
  continuity, rights protection and trusted exit.
- **Counterevidence:** unresolved backlogs, bypass routes, unfunded duties,
  vendor lock-in, inaccessible challenge routes and repeated incidents.
- **Harm evidence:** wrongful decisions, inaccessible services, surveillance,
  administrative exclusion and service collapse during reversal.
- **Actor WHEN:** public institutions could prepare standards, reserves,
  exercises and procurement options when the preparation is reversible and
  useful across several plausible paths.
- **Do not cross:** a repository validator, policy document or dashboard cannot
  manufacture legal authority, operational capacity or consent.

### CP6: Scaling capability through concentrated physical bottlenecks

**Question:** Does growth in compute and deployment fit the relevant energy,
water, grid, land, materials, supply-chain and community conditions?

- **Possible beneficial path:** efficient systems, clean supply, demand
  flexibility and local agreements expand capacity with acceptable burden.
- **Possible adverse path:** concentrated projects raise local cost, delay other
  connections, consume scarce resources or shift pollution and fiscal risk.
- **Rival accounts:** non-AI data-centre demand, industrial growth, grid
  underinvestment, project speculation, efficiency gains and location shifts.
- **Leading evidence:** project-level capacity, connection queues, local energy
  and water requirements, supply contracts and material lead times.
- **Confirming evidence:** metered resource use, marginal generation,
  reliability, price and community-level burden after operation.
- **Counterevidence:** cancelled capacity, idle reservations, efficiency,
  load-shifting and demand lower than project announcements.
- **Harm evidence:** reliability loss, household or business price pressure,
  water stress, pollution, land conflict, fiscal exposure and local exclusion.
- **Actor WHEN:** grid, planning, environmental, community and project actors
  could negotiate one bounded development when lifecycle evidence, affected
  places, alternatives and enforceable exit conditions exist.
- **Do not cross:** a global energy projection cannot establish a local project
  impact or justify overriding community authority.

### CP7: Concentrated control without credible alternatives

**Question:** Can people, organisations and countries switch, interoperate,
refuse, audit and build local capacity, or does technical and market
concentration reduce agency?

- **Possible beneficial path:** interoperable services, competition, public or
  commons capacity and portable data expand choice.
- **Possible adverse path:** control of compute, models, distribution, identity,
  data or standards creates dependency and unilateral rule changes.
- **Rival accounts:** economies of scale, security benefits, temporary market
  structure, open-source substitution, procurement fragmentation or network
  effects.
- **Leading evidence:** concentration, switching cost, contract restrictions,
  interoperability, compute access, local-language coverage and dependency.
- **Confirming evidence:** successful switching, entry, continuity and audited
  refusal without loss of essential access.
- **Counterevidence:** viable substitutes, portability, diversified supply and
  user-controlled deployment.
- **Harm evidence:** lock-in, discriminatory access, abrupt withdrawal,
  unappealable rules, rent extraction and national capability gaps.
- **Actor WHEN:** competition, procurement, standards, community and national
  capability actors could prepare options when the dependency is scoped and
  the intervention preserves useful services.
- **Do not cross:** concentration is not automatically harm, and scale is not
  automatically efficiency for the affected population.

### CP8: Information speed without epistemic or democratic capacity

**Question:** Can people distinguish observation, estimate, inference,
scenario, forecast, option and commitment before high-salience claims move
markets, institutions or behaviour?

- **Possible beneficial path:** clear provenance, uncertainty, correction and
  challenge routes improve public reasoning and coordination.
- **Possible adverse path:** persuasive dashboards, synthetic media, selective
  metrics or false authority accelerate panic, passivity or coercion.
- **Rival accounts:** ordinary media dynamics, political conflict, low trust,
  statistical literacy, platform incentives and institutional opacity.
- **Leading evidence:** claim propagation, source retention, correction lag,
  comprehension, uncertainty calibration and authority recognition.
- **Confirming evidence:** fewer consequential misunderstandings, faster valid
  correction, accessible dissent and appropriate plural action.
- **Counterevidence:** increased anxiety, misplaced confidence, authority
  confusion, stigma or less willingness to challenge.
- **Harm evidence:** financial loss, discrimination, unsafe personal action,
  retaliation, polarisation and loss of institutional trust.
- **Actor WHEN:** publishers and public institutions could prepare correction,
  incident and help routes when they are staffed, independently reviewable and
  available through the same channels as the original claim.
- **Do not cross:** engagement, sharing, trust or agreement with the thesis is
  not evidence of comprehension or public value.

### CP9: Uneven national capability and a widening agency divide

**Question:** Can countries and communities adapt capability to local needs,
language, law and infrastructure, or do gaps in connectivity, compute, context,
competency and bargaining power compound existing disadvantage?

- **Possible beneficial path:** local adaptation, public digital foundations,
  portable standards and international cooperation expand usable capability.
- **Possible adverse path:** dependence, data extraction, imported failure
  modes and uneven infrastructure widen the distance between capability and
  lived outcomes.
- **Rival accounts:** broader development gaps, state capacity, conflict,
  education, finance, geography and non-AI infrastructure constraints.
- **Leading evidence:** affordable connectivity, reliable energy, compute
  access, local data governance, language coverage, skills and institutional
  procurement capacity.
- **Confirming evidence:** locally valued service, productivity and agency
  outcomes, distributed by population and place.
- **Counterevidence:** exclusion, unused systems, externally controlled data,
  fiscal burden, skills bottlenecks and service withdrawal.
- **Harm evidence:** widening development gaps, sovereignty loss, dependency,
  inaccessible remedies and resource burdens without local benefit.
- **Actor WHEN:** national and cross-border actors could negotiate shared
  infrastructure or standards when local authority, data governance, benefit,
  financing, exit and remedy are explicit.
- **Do not cross:** a country readiness index cannot erase regional, community
  or individual distributions.

## Condition evolution is the programme's control loop

The IF is not a static caveat attached to a prediction. It is the object that
changes as evidence, actors and possible paths change.

For each condition, record these axes separately:

| Axis | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | What exactly is the condition? | person, verb, object, standard, place, period |
| Logical role | How does it relate to the route? | necessary, alternative sufficient route, enabling, inhibiting, correlated, decision rule |
| Lifecycle | How mature is the record? | hypothesised, defined, instrumented, baselined, reviewed, retired |
| Evidence state | What do current retained sources do? | supported, contradicted, mixed, insufficient, expired |
| Binding hypothesis | Does it appear to constrain this route now? | not evaluated, candidate binding, candidate relieved, disputed |
| Decision relevance | Which choice could it inform? | none, watch, prepare, negotiate, act, pause, reverse, recover, graduate |
| Authority | Who may decide or deliver? | unknown, proposed, externally verified, revoked, expired |

A condition event must re-run every dependant object. Use this minimum event
loop:

```text
condition added, narrowed, split, merged, challenged, evaluated,
disputed, expired, superseded or withdrawn
→ invalidate stale paths, forecasts, WHENs, options and dashboard projections
→ re-evaluate scope and evidence joins
→ preserve prior public meaning
→ publish what changed, what did not, and what now remains unknown
```

An IF may evolve in five materially different ways:

1. **The definition changes.** The old and new claims are not directly
   comparable until an explicit crosswalk passes.
2. **The evidence changes.** The condition wording stays fixed while support,
   counterevidence or uncertainty moves.
3. **The path changes.** A condition may stop binding one route while remaining
   important in another.
4. **The actor changes.** Control, duty, capacity or authority may move without
   the condition itself becoming true.
5. **The value choice changes.** The protected outcome, acceptable burden or
   loss function changes through legitimate governance, not through a data
   update.

No event may silently convert one axis into another. Better evidence is not
authority. A changed actor is not a relieved condition. A passed registered
route is not proof that no unregistered condition exists.

## Preparation by scale

Preparation is justified by usefulness across plausible paths, reversibility,
low burden and the cost of delay. It is not justified by drama.

| Scale | Safe candidate preparation before a forecast | Conditions before use |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Preserve records, understand current rights and verified services, map income and service dependencies, identify trusted human advice and avoid irreversible decisions from a dashboard. | The information is accurate for the person's jurisdiction, privacy is protected, help is real and the page does not personalise unsupported risk. |
| Household or community | Map essential-service, income, care, language, disability and connectivity dependencies; maintain mutual-aid and offline routes; record who may be excluded by official data. | Participation is voluntary, burdened members are not averaged away, safeguarding and referral routes exist, and community knowledge is governed appropriately. |
| Worker or union | Negotiate task-level transparency, consultation, no-retaliation participation, workload and surveillance limits, skill and transition options, evidence access, appeal and reversal. | Affected workers define scope, representatives are legitimate, employer deployment evidence is independently testable and refusal rights are explicit. |
| Enterprise | Inventory bounded uses, affected people, failure modes, dependencies and rollback; reserve evaluation and remedy capacity; test a no-deployment path. | Authority, worker and user involvement, funding, capability, safety, privacy, appeal, incident reporting and reversal all pass for the scoped use. |
| Local institution | Map concentrated outcomes hidden by aggregates, service capacity, appeal load and offline access; rehearse reversible response and recovery. | Lawful role, community review, funding, delivery, data governance, accessibility, help, appeal and coordination are verified. |
| Country | Build statistical joins without ecological fallacy, incident and correction infrastructure, social dialogue, delivery capacity, fiscal options, interoperable public foundations and independent evaluation. | Democratic authority, appropriation, rights, affected-party and jurisdictional governance, implementation capacity, transparency, review and sunset are real. |
| Cross-border | Develop portable evidence, incident, identity and service standards; fund local capability; negotiate infrastructure, data and exit rules. | Participating authorities and communities retain sovereignty, benefit, consent where required, audit, remedy and the ability to leave. |

## Forecast only what can resolve

The crisis-point map produces forecast candidates only after a precise target
exists. Valid questions include:

- Will a named, retained signal cross a predeclared threshold by a date?
- Will a named service meet its registered capacity and appeal standard by a
  date?
- Will a specific deployment show a defined outcome and harm profile in the
  same cohort during a fixed window?
- Will a condition evaluation change state under a frozen rule after the next
  source release?

Invalid questions include “Will abundance arrive?”, “Will AI replace these
people?” and “Will this country win?” They lack a resolvable event, stable
denominator or useful decision relation.

Every forecast needs a no-change baseline, relevant rival model, frozen
resolution rule, retained outcome evidence, source-family clustering, scoring
rule, missing-outcome policy and declared decision use. A forecast can score
well and still have no authority or practical value.

## The validation programme

### Stage 1: one complete synthetic transition

Build one synthetic transition whose exact condition and scope resolve through
the evolution ledger, agency map, signals, possible paths, forecast,
preparation, dashboard and experiment. Keep truth, authority, publication and
action false. This tests integration, not society.

**Exit condition:** every downstream artifact derives from the same condition
history and scope; any material evolution invalidates it; no caller can promote
the result by changing a local label.

### Stage 2: one real no-consequence observation chain

Freeze one Australian clerical occupation, region, vintage and outcome. Add
one source-independent task or adoption measure and one source-independent
worker-flow or service outcome. Pre-register all credible mechanisms and
crosswalks. Do not issue a warning or option.

**Exit condition:** the retained sources can be reproduced, their populations
and units join honestly, and reviewers can distinguish exposure, adoption,
task change, worker outcome and causal inference.

### Stage 3: validate the IF grammar

Use at least ten heterogeneous real service promises. Compare independent
encoders using the IF grammar, an ordinary assumptions register and a causal
diagram. Register scoring and thresholds before encoding.

**Exit condition:** the grammar shows a measurable advantage in finding
material omissions or preventing invalid decisions without unacceptable
disagreement, burden or false certainty. Otherwise simplify or retire it.

### Stage 4: validate the public instrument

Compare a conventional statistical release, the same release plus scope and
uncertainty, and the Observatory using identical facts. Include affected,
low-numeracy, disability, language and dissenting perspectives. Measure
comprehension, denominator recognition, uncertainty calibration, authority,
anxiety, challenge, correction and plural defensible action.

**Exit condition:** the Observatory adds decision value inside
affected-party-approved harm bounds. Preference, trust, time on page and thesis
agreement cannot rescue a harmful result.

### Stage 5: shadow early-action rehearsal

Run watch, prepare, pause, reverse and recovery evaluations prospectively
without public warnings or real consequences. Measure false alarms, misses,
lead time, operational feasibility, information harm and performance against
naive and conventional baselines.

**Exit condition:** a named legitimate institution could act within the lead
time, the response is funded and rehearsed, benefits exceed harms under the
approved loss function, and the warning adds value over simpler practice.

### Stage 6: independent and affected-party release review

Freeze exact bytes, validators, facts, displays and reviewer instructions.
Separate technical integrity, source truth, methods, affected-party,
accessibility, ethics, operational, legal, authority and publication reviews.

**Exit condition:** no unresolved stop-line remains for the proposed use. Agent
agreement cannot satisfy identity, consent, authority, law, ethics or lived
experience.

## Stop lines

The programme stops or narrows when any of these occurs:

- a capability, exposure or adoption measure is presented as a lived outcome;
- a unit, population, denominator, place, period or modelled status is lost;
- a condition is called binding without route coverage and an open-world caveat;
- a changed IF fails to invalidate dependant paths, forecasts or options;
- a severe harm is averaged away or an affected group disappears;
- an actor marks another actor ready, consenting, represented or capable;
- a warning has no reachable, verified response and help route;
- no action is represented as evidence that waiting is safe;
- a repository artifact is represented as authority, truth or public consent;
- the Observatory performs no better than a simpler release within the harm
  boundary; or
- a claim cannot survive a motivated independent attempt to disprove it.

## Ninety-day execution sequence

The sequence is dependency-ordered. Calendar dates begin only after owners and
external participation are available.

| Wave | Build | Challenge before advancing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One coherent synthetic transition bundle | Identity, scope, time, evolution, evidence reuse and authority-forgery attacks |
| 2 | Public projection of the same bundle | Mobile, assistive, low-numeracy, crop, quote and false-wait attacks |
| 3 | Complete Round 03 disposition and Round 04 freeze | Clean-checkout reproduction and independent attack of every open finding |
| 4 | Australian source and estimand protocol | Domain review of population joins, causal alternatives and archive retention |
| 5 | IF-grammar pre-registration | Blind encoder disagreement, omitted-route and simpler-method comparison |
| 6 | Public-instrument formative study package | Ethics, safeguarding, recruitment, compensation, data and adverse-event review |
| 7 | Shadow forecast and early-action protocol | Baseline, resolution, cluster, missingness, lead-time and operational-feasibility attacks |

The first public success should not be a green transition signal. It should be
a page that can say, with precision and humility: **this is the outcome we
care about; these are the conditions we currently know how to examine; this is
what changed; this is what remains unknown; these are the people and possible
paths the evidence does not cover; these actors could prepare under these
conditions; no action or waiting instruction follows without legitimate
authority; and this is exactly what would change our reading.**
