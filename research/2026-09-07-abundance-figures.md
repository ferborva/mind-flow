# Fact-check and figures for the abundance rant

Everything the draft needed and could not assert. Sources and dates on every
line. Retrieved 2026-09-07.

## 🦅 TL;DR

- **His company figures are roughly 2x too high as revenue, and wildly too high
  as profit.** The defensible version is stronger anyway: Nvidia and Alphabet
  each clear about **a third of a billion dollars a day in net profit.**
- **The Musk quotes are real** and close to how he characterised them. One is
  even sharper than he remembered: Musk explicitly says universal *high* income,
  not universal *basic* income.
- **His "20, 30, 40% lose productive value" is close to the IMF exposure number
  but far above every displacement estimate.** Exposure is not displacement, and
  the piece should not blur them.
- **The biggest finding is one that cuts against his own remedy.** See the last
  section. Redistributing the profits of the most profitable companies on earth
  yields about **8 cents per person per day.** The money is not there.

## 💰 The company figures

**What he said:** "a business that makes a billion a day, like Nvidia, two
billion a day, like Google".

| Company | Period | Revenue | Net income | Revenue/day | **Net profit/day** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nvidia | FY2026 (ended Jan 2026) | $215.9bn | $120.1bn | $591m | **$329m** |
| Alphabet | FY2025 | $402.8bn | $132.2bn | $1.10bn | **$362m** |

Derived: annual figures divided by 365.

**Verdict: the framing needs fixing, but the fix is more impressive, not less.**

- Nvidia at ~$591m/day revenue is not "a billion a day", though Q4 FY2026 alone
  was $68bn, which is a $747m/day run rate and rising.
- Alphabet is the one that crosses a billion a day, at $1.10bn, but that is
  revenue and not profit. "Two billion a day" is roughly 2x the actual figure.
- **Both companies land within 10% of each other on net profit per day, at
  roughly a third of a billion dollars.** That is the sentence worth using,
  because it is true, it is checkable, and it is startling.

## 📉 Labour displacement

**What he said:** "20, 30, 40% of the population, over the course of a few
years, starts losing productive value."

| Source | Measure | Figure |
|---|---|---|
| IMF | Global employment **exposed** to AI | ~40% |
| IMF | Advanced-economy employment exposed | ~60% |
| Goldman Sachs | Full-time jobs exposed worldwide | ~300m |
| Goldman Sachs | US work hours automatable | 25% |
| Goldman Sachs | Workforce **displacement** | 6-7% (range 3-14%) |
| WEF Future of Jobs 2025 | Displaced by 2030 | 92m |
| WEF Future of Jobs 2025 | Created by 2030 | 170m (net +78m) |

**Verdict: the 40% is defensible only as exposure, never as displacement.**
Exposure counts jobs the technology touches. The IMF is explicit that roughly
half of exposed advanced-economy jobs may *benefit* from it. Every actual
displacement estimate is single digits to low teens.

This matters for the piece. **"40% of people lose their productive value" is the
kind of number a hostile reader kills the whole argument with.** The honest
version, that 40% of global employment is exposed and displacement estimates run
3-14%, is still more than enough to carry his point, because his argument does
not need mass unemployment. It only needs enough displacement that the money
condition bites for a meaningful number of people.

Sources: [CNBC on Goldman's labour study](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/goldman-ai-impact-employment-jobs.html),
[Piktochart roundup of IMF/Goldman/WEF figures](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-many-jobs-will-ai-replace-by-2030/)

## 🌍 The scale of the "if you have money" condition

He argues money is the biggest conditional. The distribution data makes that
concrete, and it is worse than the piece currently implies.

| Threshold (2021 PPP) | Share of humanity below it, 2026 | People |
|---|---|---|
| $8.30/day, World Bank upper-middle-income line | **44.4%** | 3.68bn |
| $30/day, roughly a developed-world floor | **80.0%** | 6.64bn |

**Four in five people alive today live on less than $30 a day.** The World Bank
raised its lines in June 2025 (from $2.15/$3.65/$6.85 to $3.00/$4.20/$8.30 in
2021 PPP), which moved 125m more people into the extreme poverty count on
methodology alone.

**Verdict: use this.** It is the single strongest empirical support for the whole
thesis, and it is not currently in the draft. When the abundance conversation
says "you will be able to", it is addressing the fifth of humanity for whom the
money condition is already largely met.

Sources: [Our World in Data, share below $30/day](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/poverty-share-on-less-than-30-per-day)
(World-level series, 2026: 80.04%),
[Our World in Data, share below $8.30/day](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/share-living-with-less-than-upper-middle-income-poverty-line)
(2026: 44.38%),
[Our World in Data on the new $3 line](https://ourworldindata.org/new-international-poverty-line-3-dollars-per-day),
[World Bank on the 2021-PPP lines](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/the-world-bank-s-new-global-poverty-lines-in-2021-prices)

## ⚠️ The finding that cuts against his own remedy

This is the important one, and it is uncomfortable.

His closing argument is that the companies hold the resources, so the companies
must choose to make the money flow. Test it arithmetically.

Nvidia and Alphabet are two of the most profitable companies in history.
Combined net profit: **$252.3bn a year, $691m a day.**

Spread across 8.3 billion people:

> **$30.40 per person per year. Eight and a third cents a day.**

Derived: $252.3bn ÷ 8.3bn people. Population from
[Worldometer/IMF, 2026](https://www.worldometers.info/gdp/gdp-by-country/).

Widen it as far as you like and the shape does not change. To lift the 6.64bn
people below $30/day up to $30/day would cost **$72.7 trillion a year, about 62%
of global GDP** ($118.18tn, IMF WEO April 2026). That is not a redistribution
problem. That is a production problem.

**Verdict: his diagnosis survives, his remedy does not, at least not as
redistribution.** The `if` framing is untouched by this. What breaks is the
implicit assumption that the profits of a handful of giants are the right
denominator. They are three orders of magnitude short.

But notice where that leaves him: **the only mechanism that closes a gap that
size is the cost of the service collapsing toward zero, which is exactly what he
proposes at the end with the Tesla gyms and the free clinics.** His conclusion is
right. The reasoning that gets him there needs replacing. He should arrive at
free services because redistribution is arithmetically impossible, not because
companies ought to be generous.

That is a better piece than the one currently drafted.

Source for global GDP: [IMF World Economic Outlook April 2026 via StatisticsTimes](https://statisticstimes.com/economy/world-gdp.php)
($118.18tn for 2025).
