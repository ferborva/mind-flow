                     <section id="section-7136-4-7136" class="row ps-section section-no-title">
                        <div id="7136-4" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h1 class=" h1" id="toc-7136-4-1" data-prefix="">4. Patient Charges</h1>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-13" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h2 class=" h2" id="toc-7136-13-1" data-prefix="">Type of patient</h2>
                           <p>There are two types of PBS beneficiaries, general patients, who hold a Medicare card,
                              and concessional patients, who hold a Medicare card and one of the following:</p>
                           <ul>
                              <li>Pensioner Concession Card</li>
                              <li>Commonwealth Seniors Health Card</li>
                              <li>Health Care Card</li>
                              <li>Repatriation Health Card for All Conditions (gold) — concessional patients under RPBS</li>
                              <li>Repatriation Health Card for Specific Conditions (white) — only regarded as concessional
                                 patients for RPBS prescriptions unless they hold a separate entitlement from Centrelink,
                                 otherwise they are general patients</li>
                              <li>Repatriation Pharmaceutical Benefits Card (orange) — concessional patients under RPBS</li>
                              <li>Safety Net Concession Card or Safety Net Entitlement Card — issued by Services Australia.</li>
                           </ul>
                           <p>Under the Reciprocal Health Care Agreements, visitors from participating countries
                              (see the introduction of this section for the list of countries) are treated as general
                              patients and do not have concessional entitlements. To receive pharmaceutical benefits
                              these visitors may need to present a temporary Medicare card or their passport. Pharmacists
                              should contact Services Australia if they have enquiries about these arrangements.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-12" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h2 class=" h2" id="toc-7136-12-1" data-prefix="">Establishing entitlement</h2>
                           <p>PBS prescription forms supplied by Services Australia have spaces provided for details
                              of a patient's entitlement status. Anyone can enter this information, which must include:</p>
                           <ul>
                              <li>a cross (x) in the appropriate box to indicate the level of patient contribution;</li>
                              <li>the complete Medicare number (including individual reference number) or complete Veteran
                                 file number on the card; and</li>
                              <li>if applicable, the complete concession number on the card.</li>
                           </ul>
                           <p>All PBS prescriptions must have a Medicare or Veteran file number. All concessional
                              PBS prescriptions must have a concession number. However, it is not necessary for
                              the Medicare or Veteran file number, or the concession number to be endorsed on the
                              PBS prescription if it is included in the electronic prescription details supplied
                              by a pharmacist who is using the Claims Transmission System.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-11" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h2 class=" h2" id="toc-7136-11-1" data-prefix="">What to charge</h2>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-20" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h3 class=" h3" id="toc-7136-20-1" data-prefix="">Patient contribution</h3>
                           <p>Under the PBS, the maximum cost for a pharmaceutical benefit item at a pharmacy is
                              $25.00 for general patients and $7.70 for concession card holders, plus any applicable
                              special patient contribution, brand premium or therapeutic group premium. General
                              patients who exactly meet or exceed the Safety Net threshold (see details under '5.
                              The Safety Net Scheme') may receive pharmaceutical benefits at the concessional rate,
                              plus any applicable special patient contribution, brand premium or therapeutic group
                              premium.</p>
                           <p>Since 1 January 2016, pharmacists have had the option to discount the PBS patient
                              co-payment amount (unless the prescription is an early supply of a specified medicine,
                              in which case, the full co-payment amount applies). From 1 January 2026, the allowable
                              discount amounts will decrease by the amount of indexation (in dollars) that would
                              have normally applied to the relevant PBS patient co-payment amount (also see details
                              under&nbsp;<a href="../../../about/what-is-the-pbs/what-are-the-current-patient-fees-and-charges" class="xref">What are the current patient fees and charges?</a>). In 2026, concession card holders will be eligible for a discount of up to $0.60
                              off the concessional co-payment of $7.70, and the general patient allowable discount
                              will be reduced to nil. &nbsp;</p>
                           <p>Since 1 January 2023, pharmacists also have the option to provide a discretionary
                              increased discount to general patients for specific eligible medicines. This is not
                              mandatory, and it is the pharmacist’s choice to provide a discount.</p>
                           <p>To be eligible for the increased discount the prescription must:</p>
                           <ul>
                              <li>be an ordinary prescription for a general patient; and</li>
                              <li>have a Commonwealth price in the range above $25.00 to $51.10 (inclusive), with both
                                 amounts normally indexed over time. The general co-payment will reduce to $25.00 for
                                 the calendar year of 2026.</li>
                           </ul>
                           <p>The amounts counting towards the Safety Net for these prescriptions is calculated
                              in the same way as for non-discounted under co-payment prescriptions.</p>
                           <p>Patients who have a Safety Net Entitlement Card (see details under '5. The Safety
                              Net Scheme') may receive PBS items free of charge, except for any applicable special
                              patient contribution, brand premium or therapeutic group premium.</p>
                           <p>The supply of a pharmaceutical benefit or a Repatriation pharmaceutical benefit to
                              a patient is GST-free. Goods and services tax must not be included in the price charged
                              to a patient for the supply of a PBS or RPBS script.</p>
                           <p>It is the patient's responsibility to pay any charge lawfully imposed by an approved
                              pharmacist or supply may be refused.</p>
                           <p>The patient contribution rates are normally adjusted on 1 January each year in line
                              with inflation.</p>
                           <p>The general co-payment amount will reduce to $25.00 for the calendar year of 2026,
                              and continue to be adjusted on 1 January each year from 2027 in line with movements
                              in the Consumer Price Index (CPI). The concessional co-payment amount will remain
                              at $7.70 for five calendar years from 2025 to 2029, and continue to be adjusted on
                              1 January each year from 2030 in line with movements in CPI (also see details under
                              <a href="../../../about/what-is-the-pbs/what-are-the-current-patient-fees-and-charges" class="xref">What are the current patient fees and charges?</a>).</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-19" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h3 class=" h3" id="toc-7136-19-1" data-prefix="">Patient contributions for early supply of some PBS medicines</h3>
                           <p>Prescriptions for some PBS and RPBS pharmaceutical benefits are not eligible for Safety
                              Net benefits if re-supplied within a specified period following a previous supply
                              of the same or an equivalent pharmaceutical benefit for the same person. Early supply
                              arrangements were introduced in 2006 as the ‘Safety Net 20 day rule’. Since 1 January
                              2016, expanded PBS early supply provisions replaced the Safety Net 20 day rule and
                              extend early supply rules to more medicines.</p>
                           <ul>
                              <li>the patient contribution does not count towards the Safety Net;&nbsp;</li>
                              <li>after the Safety Net threshold is reached, the prescription will be supplied at your
                                 non-Safety Net payment or co-payment amount; and&nbsp;</li>
                              <li>discounting of the patient co-payment is not permitted.&nbsp;</li>
                           </ul>
                           <p>The Safety Net early supply rule applies to Pharmaceutical benefits if the prescription
                              is:</p>
                           <ul>
                              <li>supplied within 20 days of a previous supply usually for a month’s quantity of the
                                 same medicine or any brand of an equivalent medicine to the same person under ‘immediate
                                 supply’ provisions; or</li>
                              <li>supplied within 50 days of a previous supply of a 60-day maximum dispensed quantity
                                 (60-day prescription) of the same medicine or any brand of an equivalent medicine
                                 to the same person under ‘immediate supply’ provisions.</li>
                           </ul>
                           <p>The options for pharmacists to discount PBS co-payment amounts do not apply for early
                              supply prescriptions.</p>
                           <p>For example: The payment for an early supply prescription for a patient with a Safety
                              Net Entitlement Card would be the full concessional co-payment amount — not free.
                              For a general patient with a Safety Net Concession Card, the full general co-payment
                              amount would apply — not the concessional amount.</p>
                           <p>The Safety Net early supply rule does not apply to PBS/RPBS prescriptions originating
                              from hospitals.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-18" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h4 class=" h4" id="toc-7136-18-1" data-prefix="">Special patient contributions, brand premiums and therapeutic group premiums</h4>
                           <p>A special patient contribution is payable for a pharmaceutical benefit when a supplier
                              will not supply it at the benchmark price. Any extra charge for a higher priced benefit
                              is paid by the patient, together with their usual patient contribution. Exemptions
                              on medical grounds are available, but must be granted by Services Australia. For RPBS
                              special patient contribution arrangements see the RPBS Explanatory Notes.</p>
                           <p>Under the brand premium arrangements, reimbursement to pharmacists is based on the
                              lowest-priced brand. Any extra charge for a higher priced brand is paid by the patient,
                              together with their usual patient contribution.</p>
                           <p>Under the therapeutic group premium arrangements, reimbursement to pharmacists is
                              based on the lowest priced benefit items within identified therapeutic groups. Any
                              extra charge for a higher priced benefit is paid by the patient, together with their
                              usual patient contribution. Exemptions on medical grounds are available, but must
                              be granted by Services Australia.</p>
                           <p>Special patient contributions, brand premiums and therapeutic group premiums apply
                              to maximum quantities. When a quantity is less than, or — on an authority or 'Regulation
                              49' PBS prescription — more than, the maximum, the contributions or premiums will
                              be a factor of the maximum quantity, using standard pricing rules.</p>
                           <p>There are separate arrangements for PBS prescriptions in certain public hospitals.
                              To obtain pharmaceutical benefits under these arrangements a patient must attend a
                              participating public hospital and be a discharge patient or non-admitted patient.
                              Only a medical practitioner providing medical treatment or a midwife providing midwifery
                              treatment or a nurse practitioner providing nurse practitioner treatment in a participating
                              public hospital may prescribe PBS subsidised medication. Victoria, Queensland, South
                              Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania and the Northern Territory have these arrangements.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-17" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h3 class=" h3" id="toc-7136-17-1" data-prefix="">Increased quantities</h3>
                           <p>Where a prescriber has written an authority PBS prescription for a quantity greater
                              than the listed maximum quantity, one patient contribution should be charged for each
                              supply of the increased quantity.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-16" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h3 class=" h3" id="toc-7136-16-1" data-prefix="">Section 49</h3>
                           <p>For 'Section 49' (National Health (Pharmaceutical Benefits) Regulations&nbsp;2017) PBS
                              prescriptions, a pharmacist should charge the usual patient contribution for the original
                              and for each repeat quantity needed to make up the equivalent of the total supply
                              (plus any applicable special patient contribution, brand premium or therapeutic group
                              premium, for the original and each repeat quantity in the total supply).&nbsp;For example,
                              six co-payments would be charged for dispensing the original and five repeat prescriptions.</p>
                           <p>Safety Net benefits for Section 49 prescriptions apply for the quantity as a whole
                              single supply, ie the total combined co-payment is a single payment with regard to
                              Safety Net eligibility.</p>
                           <p>For early supply purposes, Section 49 prescriptions are treated as a single supply.
                              Usual Safety Net benefits apply for the entire supply if the interval between the
                              Section 49 supply and the previous supply is greater than the specified period for
                              the medicine. If the Section 49 supply is an early supply (made within the specified
                              period), Safety Net benefits do not apply for the original or any repeats making up
                              the equivalent of the total supply.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-15" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h3 class=" h3" id="toc-7136-15-1" data-prefix="">After hours</h3>
                           <p>A pharmacist may charge an extra fee if supplying a PBS item outside normal trading
                              hours. This charge is paid by the patient and does not count towards the Safety Net.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div id="7136-14" class="psml-fragment col-xs-12">
                           <h3 class=" h3" id="toc-7136-14-1" data-prefix="">Delivery</h3>
                           <p>A charge can be added for delivering pharmaceutical benefits from the pharmacy. This
                              charge does not count towards the Safety Net. For RPBS delivery arrangements refer
                              to the RPBS Explanatory Notes.</p>
                        </div>
