                      <h2 class="paragraph-plain--heading">What is NERO?</h2>
                    <div class="paragraph-plain--body">
            <div class="field field--name-field-html field--type-text-long field--label-hidden field__item"><p>The Nowcast of Employment by Region and Occupation or NERO provides estimates of employment in 355 occupations across 88 regions in Australia. Previously this type of data was only readily available every five years as part of the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Census of Population and Housing. With NERO these insights are produced monthly.</p>
<p>NERO has been developed using an emerging methodology known as nowcasting, which combines traditional, real-time and big data techniques - including machine learning - to estimate employment trends shortly after data becomes available. This approach enables more frequent updates and greater detail than conventional methods.</p>
<p>NERO follows the occupation and region classifications of the Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification of Occupations’ (ANZSCO) 4-digit level and the Australian Statistical Geography Standard’s Statistical Area 4 (SA4) regions.</p>
</div>
      </div>
        </div>
            </div>
      </div>
</div>
              <div class="field__item">    <div class="paragraph paragraph--type--plain paragraph--view-mode--default  spaceSTop">

              <div class="content">
                      <h2 class="paragraph-plain--heading">What can NERO be used for?</h2>
                    <div class="paragraph-plain--body">
            <div class="field field--name-field-html field--type-text-long field--label-hidden field__item"><p>NERO is a valuable tool for understanding occupational distribution and trends for workforce planning. It can help answer questions such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which regions of Australia employ the most Electricians?</li>
<li>Which regions of Australia have experienced the strongest growth of employment of Registered Nurses over the past year, or the past five years?</li>
<li>For the Ballarat region (or any other region), which are the largest employing occupations, and which are the strongest growing occupations over the past year, or the past five years?</li>
</ul>
<p>Understanding changes in occupation composition over time helps users unpack local labour markets dynamics. For example, tracking the growing employment numbers for Professional occupations in a region can show in detail the advancing role of the knowledge economy. Whether a regional economy thrives on primary industry, tourism or high-tech manufacturing, NERO can help gauge changing economies at very detailed levels.</p>
</div>

                      <h2 class="paragraph-plain--heading">Dashboard</h2>
                    <div class="paragraph-plain--body">
            <div class="field field--name-field-html field--type-text-long field--label-hidden field__item"><p>The NERO dashboard provides an interactive way to explore employment estimates by occupation and region. Users can view annual and five-year changes, examine national employment levels at the ANZSCO 4-digit and SA4 regional levels, and use a map view for spatial insights. It also includes filters to customise regions and occupations, with options to download tailored datasets for deeper analysis.</p>

              <div class="content">
                      <h2 class="paragraph-plain--heading">Methodology</h2>
                    <div class="paragraph-plain--body">
            <div class="field field--name-field-html field--type-text-long field--label-hidden field__item"><p>The approach to nowcasting employment draws on the framework outlined in the journal article <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1467-8462.12464"><em>Nowcasting the Australian Labour Market at Disaggregated Levels</em></a>. This methodology combines traditional labour market data with real-time and big data sources, applying advanced machine learning techniques to produce timely, detailed estimates of employment by occupation and region. By integrating diverse datasets and refining models through stacked approaches, NERO delivers granular insights while maintaining alignment with ABS Labour Force Survey benchmarks.</p>

              <div class="field__item">  <div class="paragraph paragraph--type--plain paragraph--view-mode--default">
        <div class="wrapper">
              <div class="content">
                      <h2 class="paragraph-plain--heading">Caveats</h2>
                    <div class="paragraph-plain--body">
            <div class="field field--name-field-html field--type-text-long field--label-hidden field__item"><p>NERO estimates employment based on what it has “learned” from historical data. If changes to regional employment patterns exist outside of the historical dataset, NERO may not capture this effect.</p>
<p>NERO estimates are smoothed to highlight long-term trends in local labour markets. Due to this smoothing process, the estimates may not reflect short-term fluctuations or rapid changes in labour market conditions. When interpreting NERO data, the scale and direction of change over time is more informative than the precise values.</p>
<p>For users who need high-level insights, sources like the ABS Labour Force Survey and Census may be more appropriate. JSA therefore recommends using NERO alongside other datasets to analyse current labour market trends, including the <a href="/data/internet-vacancy-index" data-entity-type="node" data-entity-uuid="9ed96d83-57b7-4f38-89b3-965999e9c200" data-entity-substitution="canonical" title="Internet Vacancy Index (IVI)">Internet Vacancy Index (IVI)</a>, the <a href="/data/recruitment-experiences-and-outlook-survey" data-entity-type="node" data-entity-uuid="f63151cc-7a10-4f94-bb01-ad1e7a4ff201" data-entity-substitution="canonical" title="Recruitment Experiences and Outlook Survey">Recruitment Experiences and Outlook Survey (REOS)</a>, and ABS labour statistics.</p>
<p>NERO is designed specifically for estimating employment numbers at the ANZSCO 4-digit and ASGS SA4 level. Estimates should not be summed or combined to generate aggregated results across geographies or occupation groups, as this may lead to inaccurate or misleading estimates.</p>
</div>
