# A metric for abundance, and the labour paradox underneath it

**Context.** Typed, short, a week after the first abundance rant and while
reading the research that fact-checked it. Two ideas in two paragraphs. The
first proposes a way to measure progress toward abundance. The second names a
problem that the first one creates.

**Fidelity note.** Barely touched. He typed this rather than spoke it, so there
was no filler to remove. Verbatim at
`capture/raw/2026-09-07-deflation-metric-and-labour-paradox.txt`.

---

## The metric

> I'm thinking here… a possible path we could have businesses target and
> countries track towards abundance is the rate of deflation of products and the
> total number of products that cost close to 0 to produce and reach the
> consumer (end to end production and distribution at zero cost).

Two measures, not one:

1. **The rate of deflation of products.** How fast cost is falling.
2. **The total number of products at close to zero cost.** How many have
   arrived.

And a definition attached to the second: **end to end.** Zero cost to produce
*and* to reach the consumer. Distribution counts.

He frames it as something two different actors can act on: a thing "businesses
target" and a thing "countries track".

## The paradox underneath it

> At the same time, this is a problem of productivity transition and how you
> deal with the retention of certain products that require human labour BUT
> those humans don't have an incentive to do the job because they don't need the
> salary as all they need reached FREE levels

The structure of the problem, in his terms:

- Some products still require human labour.
- Those humans need retaining in the job.
- But their needs have already reached free.
- So the salary has lost its power to motivate.

His own capitals: **BUT** and **FREE**. He is marking the hinge and the
condition.

He calls it "a problem of productivity transition", which places it in the gap
between now and abundance rather than in the end state.

## Loose ends

- He does not say what the deflation metric would be measured against. A basket?
  Which basket, and chosen by whom? A country tracking it would need one.
- "Close to 0" is doing a lot of work. Where is the line, and does it count
  goods that are free to the consumer but costly to produce, which is most of
  what the ad-funded internet does?
- He does not resolve the paradox, only names it. No proposal for who does the
  remaining human work, or why.
- The two ideas pull against each other and he does not say whether he has
  noticed. See `research/2026-09-07-labour-supply-and-cost-disease.md`.
- "Retention" is an interesting word choice. He is thinking about keeping people
  in jobs, not about filling them, which suggests he pictures this happening to
  an existing workforce rather than a future one.
