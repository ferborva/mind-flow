# Why the benchmark shrug happens, and the categories become his

**Context.** Typed, in the working session on 2026-09-08, minutes after reading
the three drafts. Two things happen: he adopts the condition categories as his
own, and he supplies the mechanism underneath the "it's just another model"
anecdote, which until now the draft only observed. **The second one is a new
claim and it is the best answer yet to why the frame matters to people who are
not in the argument.**

Short, typed rather than spoken, so it is verbatim.

## Why people shrug at a benchmark

> A lateral thought just came by. When we talk about Peter's child saying that
> "it's just another model, so what?" We should relate it within the reframe at
> some point in the document.
>
> For example, when people hear "there is a new model released that tops all the
> benchmarks" they will all be wondering, so what!? But the reason for that is
> that none of those people have a sentence in their mind that looks like this:
> "I will be able to <verb> <fill in the blank> if a new model that tops the
> benchmarks is release with an x% of power on the Pareto frontier".

The move: the shrug is not indifference and it is not cynicism. **An
announcement can only land against a sentence somebody is already holding.**
Benchmark releases are stated in a currency nobody's personal if-clause is
denominated in, so there is nothing for the news to attach to.

## Loose ends

- He says "at some point in the document", not where. Drafted into the opener,
  since that is where the anecdote already sits, with a callback at the close.
- The Pareto frontier reference is his and it is doing real work: he is
  describing a benchmark gain as a movement of a condition, in a space that has
  no meaning in anyone's life sentence. Worth asking whether he wants that
  pushed further, because it is the beginning of an argument about which
  measurements are worth announcing at all.
- Unanswered from earlier and still the biggest one: does the sequencing
