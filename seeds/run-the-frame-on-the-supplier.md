# The frame runs recursively, and the supplier has an if too

Every promise of a free or unlimited thing rests on somebody who has to supply
it, and that somebody has their own conditions. **So the frame does not bottom
out in a complaint. Run it on the consumer, get a condition. Run it on whoever
removed that condition, get the next one.** That is what makes it a tool rather
than a lens: it produces the next question instead of a verdict.

## In his words

> If we believe in a future where AI availability for people is made available
> through space compute, connected to global satellites with chip to meter
> internet connectivity, then yes, indeed, everyone will have access to
> internet, they will have access to compute, then everyone will have AI
> potentially. And this is the part that we could challenge or try to understand
> how that comes to be. The company offering that service would be able to offer
> it close to zero price. And if that's the case, then the question would be,
> okay, how does that company survive? Because that company has a condition in
> itself. A company will only be able to offer endless free compute if, and then
> we can talk about that condition. And then we can work on seeing how that
> condition has broken down, on the steps that need to be taken to get there.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

> I believe that framing the steps based on the conditions that need to be met
> is very much an Elon Musk approach to things, where you actually go down the
> entire vertical, understand the conditions for it, and break them down one at
> a time to unlock the future you're after.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

## Stories and evidence

**Demonetisation is a condition being removed, not a condition disappearing.**
His own gloss: compute gets so cheap that the "if you have money to pay" clause
stops being the load-bearing one. He gives the abundance crowd that point in
full, which is what buys him the right to ask what becomes binding next.

**It is the move that fixes the ending of the first draft.** `abundance-has-an-if`
ends by asking what enormous companies should do with their profits, which is a
moral question, and the red team broke it there. Run recursively, the same
material becomes an engineering question: what would have to be true for a
company to offer this at zero, sustainably. An entrepreneur can work that.
*(The observation that this repairs the draft is Claude's; the recursive move
itself is his.)*

**One level down is already interesting.** Free global compute from orbit needs,
at minimum: launch cost per kilo, power and thermal budget in orbit, spectrum
and landing rights per country, terminal cost per household, and someone
carrying the loss until one of those falls. Every one of those is an if with an
owner. *Illustrative, not his, and not researched.*

## Caveats and edges

Recursion has no natural floor. Followed far enough it reaches physics and stops
being useful, so the piece has to say how far down to go, and why one level is
usually enough. He has not said where he would stop.

## Gaps

- Which promise does he want run recursively as the worked example? Free
  compute, free healthcare, or free education?
- How many levels down before it stops paying? One is a demonstration, three is
  a research programme.
