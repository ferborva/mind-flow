# Money does not disappear, it is the substrate

"Money won't matter in the future economy" does not survive contact with how
goods and services actually get made. Some token of exchange is structural, not
incidental, and any serious abundance argument has to carry it.

## In his words

> There's always going to be some sort of value attributed to things. Not
> everything is worth nothing. At least we're not going to be there any time soon.
> from: 2026-09-01-abundance-is-conditional

> Even if you were given a ration system, the rationing is still based on some
> sort of token that you get on a regular basis.
> from: 2026-09-01-abundance-is-conditional

## Stories and evidence

**The rationing argument** is the sharp one: the apparent alternative to money
is itself denominated in a token. You have not removed the conditional, you have
renamed it.

**The producer side.** The groups of people and companies that come together to
offer services and goods will do so in exchange for something. Free-to-consumer
is not free-to-produce.

**The company's own conditions.** A business continues only if it keeps
customers, which holds only if nothing better exists in the market and it keeps
innovating. "If that condition isn't met, that company will die." The `if`
structure applies to the companies too, not just to people.

## Caveats and edges

He explicitly brackets this as an assumption to reason from, not a proven claim:
"let's work under the assumption that money is still around". Honest framing,
worth preserving rather than hardening.
