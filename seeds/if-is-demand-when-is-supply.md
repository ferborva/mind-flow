# The consumer gets an if, the enterprise gets a when

Abundance is an exchange, so it has two sides and the frame has two forms.
Demand side: **I will be able to X if** these conditions hold, and I am subject
to them. Supply side: **we will be able to offer X when** we have overcome these
constraints, and that is a list I own. **Every condition on the consumer's if is
somebody's supply-side project.** That is what makes abundance a system rather
than a gift arriving.

## In his words

> There's two types of consumers of this reframe. There's the consumer side, and
> we've been talking about this as consumers and enterprises so far. From a
> consumer perspective, it is indeed verb plus fill in the blank plus if. I will
> be able to verb, if I meet these conditions or if the conditions of my
> environment are met.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

> But if you take the point of an enterprise and you actually think about the
> offering side of this contract, there's the other side of abundance, which is
> someone has to have the offer. This is an exchange system. So we'd be able to
> frame something like: I, as an enterprise, will be able to offer fill in the
> blank when I can do this and this and this. And that is the list for the CEO.
> That is the list for the entrepreneur. That is the list of constraints that I
> need to overcome in order to be able to offer that.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

> I will be able to, as a company, offer free and infinite AI compute to the
> world when I overcome these constraints.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

## Stories and evidence

**He wants this held back for a second piece.** His own call, said as he landed
it: "as a corollary or a second part two of the piece". Piece one carries the
reframe and the invitation only.

**It has a better audience than piece one does.** The supply-side list is an
operator's document. Anyone building anything has one implicitly and almost
nobody writes it out, which makes part two useful to founders and operators
rather than to podcasters.

**The tense is not decoration.** If is a state of the world you are subject to.
When is a sequence you are executing. Same conditions, and which word you reach
for tells you which side of the exchange you are standing on. *(He made the
switch instinctively while speaking; naming why it matters is Claude's.)*

**This is where `the-choice-belongs-to-enterprises` should go next.** That seed
asks what enormous businesses should do with their profits, which is a question
about generosity, and it is the part of the first draft the red team broke.
Restated as a supply-side when, it stops being a moral demand and becomes a
condition list a company can actually work: what has to be true for us to serve
this at zero and survive.

## Caveats and edges

The two lists are not symmetrical. A consumer cannot act on their ifs at all,
which is the whole reason the frame reaches ordinary people. An enterprise can
act on most of its whens. Presenting them as mirror images would flatter the
consumer side.

## Gaps

- Does the state get a third form of the sentence? He has consistently refused
  to make this about what governments should do, and the supply side he names is
  always a company.
- Whose "when" is it when the supplier is a public system, as in the healthcare
  example he wants to use?
