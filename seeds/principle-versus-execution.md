# Know which layer the argument belongs to

**Seven standards down to one is not a strategic decision. It is a consequence
of one.** The guiding principle is "conform to the design system". What falls
out of it is that seven patterns become two. How fast you get there, and whether
you stop at six on the way, is execution and timeline. **Arguing the number at
the principles table is an altitude error, and it is how the high level
conversation gets eaten by the low level one.**

## In his words

> I wouldn't even go that far. I would actually say that, for example, to the
> point of reducing seven standards down to one, that's a call of execution. And
> a process of execution.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> You could, as a guiding principle in this case, say, well, the solution needs
> to abide with the design system. That is a high level principle. And then you
> start digging and you go, okay, for navigation patterns, if there's seven
> navigation patterns, what is the correct navigation pattern? The design system
> should guide us there. And maybe the final answer comes down to, well, there's
> likely these two options that we can follow.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> So if indeed we execute on all these hundreds of pages and reduce the overall
> deviation that there is across patterns from seven down to two, great. If we do
> it progressively and we come from seven down to six, that might also be good
> from an execution perspective, and strategic timeline.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

## Stories and evidence

**Three layers in one worked example**, which is why this is the most concrete
part of the transcript:

| Layer | The settings redesign |
|---|---|
| Analysis | There are seven navigation patterns across hundreds of pages |
| Guiding principle | Any solution abides by the design system |
| Execution | Seven to two, or seven to six this quarter, on a timeline |

**Seven to six counts as progress.** He is explicit and unbothered about it.
This is a partial-credit position and it belongs to the execution layer, where
partial credit is allowed. At the principle layer it would be incoherent.

**It is also a defence of the guiding policy conversation.** The reason to keep
the number out of that room is that the number is arguable and the principle is
not, and a room arguing numbers never gets to a principle.

## Caveats and edges

**The progressive path has an unanswered hole.** Claude put it to him directly:
going seven to six tends to ship an eighth pattern, because the new correct one
lands before the old ones die, and that is how legacy sprawl actually happens.
He declined to go there - "now it's talking about execution and coordination,
that's not where I want to go" - so the retirement mechanism is missing rather
than answered.

He also does not say who arbitrates when someone insists a number is strategic.

## Gaps

- Is there a test for which layer something belongs to, or is it judgement?
- What retires a pattern? Without an answer the progressive path is a claim
  without a mechanism.
- Does the same three-layer split hold outside design work?
