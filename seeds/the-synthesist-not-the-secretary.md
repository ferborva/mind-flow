# Gathering is not leading until you sign the diagnosis

The objection he raises against his own framework: **if I go around asking
everyone what they think and never put my own view in, have I not just demoted
myself to secretary?** The answer is that everyone else holds a fragment and you
are the only one holding all of it, so synthesis is the scarce thing - **but only
if you actually land it.** You write the diagnosis down, in your own words, and
put your name on it. Their fingerprints are on the content; the shape is yours.

## Stories and evidence

The answer, as it stands in the transcript. **Not his words.**

- **Everyone else holds a fragment.** Their two in the morning, their bit of the
  room. You are the only one holding all of them. Opinions are abundant,
  synthesis is scarce.
- **Whoever frames the question owns the shape of the answer.** Deciding what
  counts as the problem is more power than proposing a solution.
- **The catch is real.** Gather and never synthesise and the objection is
  correct: you are a secretary.
- **The move is to sign it.** Not "here is what you all said" but "here is what
  I think is going on, given what you told me". One paragraph, sharp enough to
  be wrong.
- **It has to cost something.** A diagnosis that offends nobody is just a
  summary.
- **And the ask changes shape.** It stops being "what do you think?" and becomes
  "does this hold?" Different question, different posture.

## Caveats and edges

The whole thing rests on the diagnosis being sharp enough to be contested and
still recognisable to the people who fed it. Nothing in the material says how to
get that balance right, and it is the hardest part.

## Gaps
