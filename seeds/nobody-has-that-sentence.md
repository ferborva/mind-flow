# The shrug happens because nobody holds the sentence

"A new model just topped every benchmark" reliably produces *so what*, and the
reason is mechanical rather than emotional. **An announcement can only land
against a sentence somebody is already holding, and nobody's personal sentence
has a benchmark in its if-clause.** The news is denominated in a currency that
does not appear in anyone's life.

## In his words

> When people hear "there is a new model released that tops all the benchmarks"
> they will all be wondering, so what!? But the reason for that is that none of
> those people have a sentence in their mind that looks like this: "I will be
> able to <verb> <fill in the blank> if a new model that tops the benchmarks is
> release with an x% of power on the Pareto frontier".
> from: 2026-09-08-the-benchmark-shrug

And the anecdote it explains, which he recalls from the podcast:

> Even Peter Diamandis himself said that his kids are just saying, dad, it's
> just another model. So what? They just hear things happening, but they don't
> understand. They can't relate to the thing that this next progress or this
> next piece unlocks, since they don't have that framing around what it's
> currently blocking.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

## Stories and evidence

**It reframes the shrug as accurate rather than lazy.** The people shrugging are
not failing to appreciate the technology. They are correctly reporting that
nothing in their own conditional was touched. That is a much more generous
reading than "the public doesn't get it", and it puts the burden on the people
making the announcement.

**It gives the frame a job for ordinary people, not just for arguments.** Once
you hold your own sentence, every release becomes checkable against it: does
this touch my if-clause, or not? Usually the answer is no, and saying so out
loud is honest reporting rather than pessimism.

**It is the mechanism behind the phrase he landed on earlier**, that the frame
"connects with what people will see and live and understand on a day to day
basis". Everyone knows what it feels like to be blocked by a price, a postcode
or a form. Nobody knows what it feels like to be blocked by a benchmark.

**The Pareto frontier detail is doing work.** He describes a benchmark gain as a
condition moving in a space with no meaning in anyone's life. That is the
beginning of a separate argument about which measurements are worth announcing
at all, and he has not made it yet.

## Caveats and edges

The reverse is also true and he has not said it: some announcements really do
move a condition and still get shrugged at, because the person had no sentence
either way. The frame's value here is prescriptive, not just diagnostic. It
tells you to go and write your sentence first.

## Gaps

- Does he want the "which measurements are worth announcing" argument pulled
  out? It is one step past this and it would land hard on that audience.
- The Diamandis anecdote still needs an episode and a date. It is the opener of
  `name-the-if` and the only falsifiable line in the message.
