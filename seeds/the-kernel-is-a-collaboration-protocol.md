# The kernel is a communication tool, not just a thinking tool

Rumelt's kernel - diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action - is normally read
as a way to think. **His claim is that it is also the order in which you are
allowed to talk to people, and that presenting at the wrong stage is what makes
conversations lock down.** Not the words you use. The maturity of what you bring
and when you bring it.

## In his words

> I'm going to push back on your pushback. The kernel, while it's not a
> communication tool, at the same time - not a communication tool in the sense of
> how you talk or the words you use - the reality is that it is, at the same
> time, exactly that.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> If you come to someone and present to them, or communicate to them, at a wrong
> stage or too early phase, with content that is not mature enough or is not
> ready for that point in time, you will not be received by the listener the same
> way as if you actually engage with them through the kernel's process itself.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> That conversation would quickly lock down very, very easily, when someone just
> starts challenging your first gut proposal.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> At least in the kernel from a collaboration standpoint too. There is a
> collaboration aspect, a collaboration enablement side to the kernel.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

## Stories and evidence

**The wrong opening move, in his own staging of it:** "Hey, I've seen all of
this, and I'm thinking we should do blah. What do you think?" Two sentences,
and the conversation is now a referendum on a gut proposal.

**Why it locks down.** A first-idea proposal invites challenge of the idea. It
gives the listener nothing else to engage with, so the only available move is to
attack it, and once that has happened the diagnosis can never be had.

**The size threshold is his.** He is explicit that jumping to the answer is fine
for small problems - obvious answer, few minutes, done. The kernel is what you
reach for when the problem is big enough that the foundations matter.

**"Collaboration enablement side of the kernel" is his own name for the thing.**
Said mid-sentence and never defined. It is the closest thing in the material to
a title for the whole framework.

## Caveats and edges

He never states what the right stage actually is for each audience. The claim is
that too early is wrong, but the transcript does not contain a rule for when
each kind of person gets brought in.

He also declines to extend the framework into coherent action. Twice, the
conversation reaches execution and coordination and he pushes it away. So the
collaboration layer is currently a claim about two thirds of the kernel.

## Gaps

- Define the collaboration enablement side. Is it the two principles below it,
  or is there more?
- Is there a stage-to-audience mapping? Who gets brought in at diagnosis, who at
  guiding policy?
- Does coherent action have a collaboration layer too, or does execution work
  differently on purpose?
- Attribution: he read Rumelt. Which edition, and does he want the book named
  in any published piece?
