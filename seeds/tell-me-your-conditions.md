# If everything is conditional, then tell me your conditions

The obvious attack on the frame is that it is a restatement: everything ever
said has conditions, so a lens that applies to every claim discriminates between
none of them. **His answer is a reversal. The frame does not discriminate
between claims, it discriminates between people who name their conditions and
people who do not.** A promise with no conditions attached cannot be reasoned
about, planned around, or disagreed with, which is why the conversation loops.

## In his words

> Indeed, it is just a reframe, and indeed, there's conditions in everything
> that's ever been said of humanity, which basically renders the thesis of
> endless abundance without any limits that you guys in the podcast are talking
> about, incomplete. And we'd circle back to: if my take is just a reframe, then
> please share what your conditions are within the abundance thesis so that I can
> better understand what that world looks like.

> Unfortunately, [it] presents a world of no limitations that we simply cannot
> reason about. And when you take it to the extreme of saying that that's just
> gonna happen in the next ten years, it just takes all the possibility of
> discussion with the audience off the table.

> My take here is not being pessimistic in terms of conditions, like you will
> say, Alex, everything has conditions, that's just a answer. Right? Looking
> into the face of the reality of not what stands in the way, but what are the
> conditions that need to be met to enable agency.

## Stories and evidence

**The attack it answers, in full.** Put to him in the voice of Wissner-Gross:
you have not built a framework, you have built a restatement. "Everything is
conditional" is true of "I can walk to the shop". Show me an abundance claim it
rules out and one it lets through, or it is vocabulary rather than analysis.

**The claims he is reversing on.** He recalls hearing, repeatedly: everything
will be unlimited, you will be able to have anything you want, learn everything
you want, live forever, transfer your brain into a digital space where
everything is possible, and this within ten years. *Recalled from memory,
unverified. See gaps.*

**Why it is not pessimism.** His framing is "not what stands in the way, but
what conditions need to be met to enable agency". Same content as an obstacle
list, opposite posture, and it is the register that room respects: first
principles, work the vertical down, break the conditions one at a time. He
explicitly calls that an Elon Musk approach to problems, approvingly.

**It is the reason the frame is generous rather than deflating.** The optimist's
job stops being "paint the world" and becomes "name your conditions and tell me
which one goes first", which is a question they can work on rather than defend
against.

## Caveats and edges

The reversal is a rhetorical move as much as an analytical one, and it wins the
exchange rather than the argument. If they do produce a condition list, he has
to engage with it on the merits, and he has not yet said what a good answer
would look like.
