# Borrow the guiding policy, or find the gap where it should be

**A new lead does not have to invent the frame alone.** Somewhere in the
organisation there is usually a group whose job is to own principles - a design
system team, an architecture group, a standards owner. Go and ask them for
theirs. **And if they do not have one, you have not hit a dead end, you have
found a gap in their system, and you arrive holding the analysis that proves
it.** That turns you into a contributor to the system rather than a consumer of
it, which is a far better position for someone new to leading.

## In his words

> What I pointed her to is the design system itself. Hey, you've got a
> connection to the design system team. Do they have design principles within
> which any solution that you build needs to fit? And if not, then you're likely
> calling out a potential gap in the design system that you'd want to collaborate
> on with the design system team. You can come to them with the evaluation that
> you've done of the problem, and then you can work with them to help them devise
> a set of standards and a set of principles for any future solution, that can
> then be divided out more broadly. And that's a higher level conversation, to be
> held before any solutions take place.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

## Stories and evidence

**It composes with the time-poverty principle exactly.** The design system team
is time poor too. The ask is not "come and help me redesign settings". It is
"here is analysis you would want, and a gap in your own thing".

**The gap is the gift.** Seven navigation patterns across hundreds of pages is
evidence a design system team cannot easily gather for themselves, and it is
evidence about their system, not hers.

**Timing is load-bearing.** "Before any solutions take place." Turn up after you
have a design and the same conversation is a review, and reviews are where
proposals go to be challenged.

**It answers the hardest bit of the lead transition.** The frame has to come
from somewhere, and inventing it from scratch on your own authority is exactly
what a new lead has least of.

## Caveats and edges

The move assumes such a team exists and is reachable. She has "a connection to
the design system team". Many people do not have one.

It also has a political edge he does not name: telling a team their system has a
hole is only a gift if they receive it that way.

## Gaps

- What is the equivalent move outside design? Architecture principles, a
  security bar, a tech strategy doc?
- What do you do when the principles-owning team says no, or has principles that
  are wrong for your problem?
