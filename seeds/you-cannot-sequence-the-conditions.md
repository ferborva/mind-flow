# You cannot sequence the conditions, only diagnose which one binds

He concedes the strongest attack on the frame rather than defending it.
Conditions do not fall in an order anyone can plan. They come down
progressively, often sideways, frequently by something nobody had on the list,
and the one you can target is rarely the one that was binding. **What survives
is smaller and still more than anyone has: you cannot say which condition falls
next, but you can say which one is binding now, and notice when the answer
changes.**

## In his words

> I don't think the sequencing is something that can actually be planned for to
> bring down conditions. That's something that's progressive and sometimes not
> forecasted or unintended. Other times, you can target it, but not always the
> load bearing one. So that's a very broad and complex space, but the frame
> around conditions still holds in my eyes.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

And, earlier in the same conversation, the ambition he is conceding against:

> I'm thinking of this very much in the Foundation style, like Hari Seldon's
> psychohistory, where we should be able to forecast at which points in the road
> as we bring down these conditions, human society is potentially not going to
> be fully ready, and we have to live through that transition, but we can
> prepare for it as we tackle the conditions that we remove towards this
> unleashed agency.
> from: 2026-09-08-abundance-frame-conversation

## Stories and evidence

**The attack it answers.** Naming conditions is what pessimists have always
done. Every technology arrived with a list of reasons it could not spread, the
list was usually accurate and usually irrelevant, because conditions get
dissolved sideways by things nobody had on the list. Sequencing is the weak
joint in any condition-based frame.

**The reply that costs him nothing.** If conditions really do fall unpredictably,
the burden of that unpredictability lands on the people putting a decade on it,
not on the person declining to. He is making the smaller claim.
*(Claude's line, not his.)*

**The residue is diagnosis rather than forecast**, and it is enough to produce a
recurring segment: each model release, chip rule or national regulation
answerable as which condition just got lower, for whom, and what is binding now.
Sometimes the honest answer is "none yet, and that is fine", which is exactly
why Diamandis's kid shrugged at the new model. *(The diagnosis / forecast
distinction is Claude's. He agreed with it: "I think it does.")*

## Caveats and edges

**This pulls directly against `drafts/from-if-to-when.md`**, which is built on
five named phases with dates running to 2055 and five forecast crossings. That
draft is a commissioned proposal and was always his to approve. This capture is
the first time he has said something that cuts against its spine, and he did not
appear to notice he had. The likely resolution is that the phases stand as
scenarios rather than as a schedule, and the dashboard does the diagnosis, but
that is his call and it is a real one.

The concession also weakens the "conditions have an order, so the frame produces
a path" pitch. A frame that only ever describes the present is less of an unlock
than one that produces a sequence, and the piece should not promise the latter.

## Gaps

- If the order cannot be planned, what exactly is he offering the Moonshot crew
