# People need to be heard, not obeyed

**Listening to someone does not commit you to following them.** It commits you
to explaining why you landed somewhere else. **And having listened and diverged
beats never having asked, every time, which is why the early conversation is
what buys sponsorship at approval time.**

## In his words

> What's interesting is that getting their input makes them feel listened to. It
> doesn't mean that the solution necessarily has to follow what they said. People
> like to be listened to. If you don't follow their guideline, but you've already
> listened to them at the very least, and you can explain why eventually you've
> landed on something else, that is often better than going to a solution without
> having talked to them to start with.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> If I'm working on a problem and I know that whenever we come to a solution
> phase, or even a solution approval phase, I'm going to need approval or
> sponsorship or even support from other people, I want to loop those people in
> as soon as possible to get that early engagement and early pre-approval.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> Engaging with people, being conscious of their time, number one, and number
> two, acknowledging their input and their value, their experience, are in my
> eyes the key foundations for a good early phase evaluation and a successful
> final phase approval.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

## Stories and evidence

**It is a two-move sequence, not a nicety.** The early ask is not politeness.
It is the same people you will later need approval, sponsorship or air cover
from, engaged before the moment where saying no is cheap for them.

**Collaboration end to end.** His words. Asking without reporting back is not
half of the practice, it is a different practice, and a worse one.

**His sister and the shared hospital office.** Three paediatric surgeons and a
tiny room. She proposed, repeatedly, and got pushback every time. She had never
asked. The diagnosis was probably not shared: hers was the room, theirs may have
been something else entirely.

## Caveats and edges

The obvious failure mode is consultation after the decision is already made.
Claude named it in the conversation as consultation theatre and he did not
disagree, but he also did not say it, so it is not yet his.

He does not say what to do with someone who cannot accept a divergent outcome no
matter how well it is explained.

## Gaps

- What does the explaining actually look like? A doc, a conversation, a line in
  a summary? The mechanism matters more than the principle here.
- Has this ever failed for him? Someone who felt heard and still blocked him.
- Does the credit expire? Input given in March, decision in September.
- The research support for this is real and unverified in this repo. See the
  backlog item on procedural justice.
