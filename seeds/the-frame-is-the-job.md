# Becoming a lead means nobody hands you the frame any more

**As an individual contributor, the frame arrives with the problem** - usually
with a pre-solution attached. **As a lead, defining that frame is the job, and
it is the part nobody tells you has changed.** The guiding policy layer stops
being something you receive and starts being something you and your peers have
to produce.

## In his words

> As she transitions from individual contributor to leader, she's moving from a
> space where they're telling her what to do, or they're pointing towards a
> problem, very likely with a pre-solution, or they've already given her a frame
> within which to work. And she's moving towards a space where this frame has to
> be defined by her and her lead colleagues.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> That space of those guiding principles, those high level conversations, is
> where things can get really, really interesting.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

> So let's assume that these lead teams, or these young lead teams, haven't
> talked about the guiding principles that they want to stay within. Their
> values, their core, the leaps around how solutions should be built, and which
> boundaries they shouldn't cross.
> from: 2026-09-09-collaboration-kernel

## Stories and evidence

**His wife, at Moodle, mid-transition.** A designer building her way towards
lead, bringing him people communication problems and strategic organisational
problems, on a redesign of the settings and administration pages of a legacy
system. The problem is ambiguous by nature and there is no frame in the box.

**Do not assume the frame exists.** He is explicit that when guiding principles
are already clear the work is easy, and that clarity "usually comes from
assuming that people have already talked about this". His default assumption is
that a young lead team has not.

**This is the diagnosis behind the whole coaching pattern.** The reason people
jump to solutions on large ambiguous problems is not laziness. It is that the
move that worked for years - receive frame, produce solution inside it - has
quietly stopped being available.

## Caveats and edges

Only one case is documented, and he is married to it. He says he has coached
several people through the same thing, and none of the others appear.

He also does not say whether a lead ever gets to refuse the frame handed down
from above, which is the case where the transition is partial.

## Gaps

- The other people he has coached. Names not needed, situations are. Was any of
  them not a promotion case?
- Did it work for her? The transcript is advice, with no outcome attached.
- Where does the frame come from when there is no design system to borrow one
  from and no peer group yet?
