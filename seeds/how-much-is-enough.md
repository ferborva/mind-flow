# How much is enough to make working optional

"Working will be optional" is already true for a small number of people. That
makes it a threshold question rather than a utopian one, and the threshold is
measurable. The people crossing it next should be the ones losing their jobs.

## In his words

> When Elon Musk says working will be optional, that is already the case for a
> very small subset of humanity.
> from: 2026-09-01-abundance-is-conditional

> If we can expand the number of people that live in that threshold today, we
> can gradually expand the number of people that can live in that world.
> from: 2026-09-01-abundance-is-conditional

## Stories and evidence

**The reframe**, which is the valuable move: an abstract promise becomes a
number you can count people against. Working is already optional above some
level of accrued wealth, where the wealth generates enough on its own that a
person living above-average could not exhaust it in 100 years accounting for
inflation.

**His own position** as the anchor: middle upper class, well placed, no access to
endless luxuries, has to work every day, cannot hypothetically retire whenever he
wants. But he can picture the threshold for himself.

**The allocation claim.** "Those people should, in principle, be the ones that
lose their job." If you are replaced in the next three to five years, you should
have enough to sustain you for life.

## Caveats and edges

Runs straight into the next seed: a threshold implies a defined quality of life,
and he says defining that for humanity as a whole is "close to impossible".

## Gaps

- **He can picture his own threshold but never says the number.** That number, or
  the reasoning he'd use to get to it, would make this whole argument concrete.
  Highest-value question from this capture.
- Is the threshold absolute, or relative to a country's cost of living? The
  national-conditions seed suggests the latter, but he doesn't say.
