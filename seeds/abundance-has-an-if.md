# Abundance has an if

Every abundance claim has the shape **verb + fill in the blank**. You can learn
X. You will be able to cure Y. The claim is incomplete: the real structure is
**verb + fill in the blank + if**. The conditions are the whole argument, and
they are the part nobody says out loud.

## In his words

> There's actually a corollary to it. There's a part that follows, and this is
> the part that's really the most interesting and where I think a lot of the
> conversation really gets stuck. And it's that it should be verb plus fill in
> the blank, plus **if**.
> from: 2026-09-01-abundance-is-conditional

> Today, you can buy anything. Anything is literally anything. If.
> from: 2026-09-01-abundance-is-conditional

## Stories and evidence

**Buying anything today**, unpacked into its conditions: if you have enough
money, if there is stock available, if you're close enough to the stock to put
your hands on it, if there is a supply chain that can get it to you, if it's
legal where you live, if you're old enough, if you hold the certificate that
allows you to possess it. His example: guns in Spain.

**Curing anything tomorrow**, unpacked the same way: if I have the monetary
resources to request the service, and I have access to the cure, and I live in a
country where it's legal, and where it's produced, and it's available at a price
affordable to my pocket.

The second list is the first list with the nouns swapped. That is the point.

## Caveats and edges

He is not arguing against abundance. He says plainly "we really do want to have
this world of abundance". The complaint is about an incomplete sentence, not a
false one.

## Gaps

- Does he want the pattern named? "The Missing If" is my proposal, not his.
- Is the list of conditions meant to be exhaustive, or illustrative of a class?
